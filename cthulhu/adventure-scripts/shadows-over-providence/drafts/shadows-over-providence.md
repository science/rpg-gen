# Shadows over Providence

*A Call of Cthulhu 7e scenario for 3–4 investigators. Providence, Rhode Island — Friday, October 14, 1921, from early evening to dawn.*

*Revision 2. The original script is in `../source/shadows-over-providence-v1.md`. This version keeps what worked at the table (the heist, the police-and-politics scene, the finale) and rebuilds the middle around people instead of item pickups.*

---

## Keeper Overview

### The dramatic question

**Who killed Caitlin Bronson, and can the people who failed to save her set free what is left of her?**

Everything in the adventure serves that question. The party meets Bronson before she dies and likes her. She dies in front of them. For the rest of the night they hear her voice in the walls of the hotel, and they slowly learn that the voice is really her, trapped inside the thing that killed her.

### What is really going on (Keeper only)

- **Ibnhotep** was a priest of Isis at Philae under Ramesses who extended his life by consuming the *ka* (life-force) of the faithful. Each soul he ate made him stronger and less human, until he was a hunger in the shape of a man, and then not even that. The temple guardians could not kill him. Isis taught them to bind him: a clay vessel blessed in her name, sealed with honey, bound with the **tyet** (the knot of Isis), and closed with a spoken prayer.
- The bound jar came to the **British Museum**. In **1879** a cleaner knocked it from a shelf. Three staff died before a young curator, **Elisabeth Hartley**, rebound the thing using fragments of the temple rite. She wrote it up in 1903 as a dry academic monograph that nobody read.
- In 1921 the museum sent the Ibnhotep collection on an American tour under its keeper, **Dr. Caitlin Bronson**, with her aide **Owen Hartley** (Elisabeth's grandson). In Southampton the jar's ancient twine binding frayed in its crate. Owen re-tied it himself with museum linen tape rather than report the damage, which would have cancelled the tour. The binding has been leaking ever since. Bronson felt it: bad dreams, a jar that seems to shift on its plinth. She now wears the tyet on a cord under her collar.
- **Rupert Merriwether** (from *The Paper Chase*) underwrote the Providence leg of the tour. He is too unwell to travel, so he has sent the investigators as his personal emissaries. Bronson, who corresponded with him, knows only that "Rupert's friends have seen strange things and did not run."
- **The robbery** was commissioned by **Dr. Heinrich Voss**, a German Egyptologist in New York who knows exactly what the jar is and wants it *sealed and intact*, to study or to use. Voss's money came to **Danny Walsh**, Providence's racket boss, through **Councilman Harold Sullivan**, who sits on Walsh's payroll. Walsh hired a Boston professional, **Fox Iona**, to lead the crew. His inside woman, **Caroline Thornwood**, was assigned to keep Bronson away from the jar.
- At the robbery the jar *wants* to fall, and it does. Ibnhotep comes out starving and weak. He kills Bronson the instant the tyet leaves her throat (see the heist, Phase 4), and flees into the hotel's ventilation. He has her ka now. He can speak with her voice, and **she is genuinely in there.**
- Over the night he regenerates in the disused elevator shaft between the basement levels. He feeds, spawns lesser bodies (proto-shoggoths), and hunts three things: **the tyet** (to destroy it), **the scarab** (his old focus of power, stolen by the gang), and **the jar fragments** (they pull at him; he wants to *not* go back). By dawn he is whole.

### What changed from the first version

- **New cast and setting date.** Set in 1921 to match the party's sheets. The party are Merriwether's emissaries; Merriwether himself is absent.
- **Bronson becomes a friend first.** A private meet-and-greet before the reception (Scene 1) gives every investigator a personal moment with her and gives one of them her room key.
- **A drop-in investigator.** Owen Hartley, Bronson's aide, is a pregen for the fourth player and a fully motivated NPC if that player does not return.
- **The tyet necklace moves into the heist.** It is around Bronson's neck, not in a jewelry box. Who ends up holding it is decided by the investigators' choices in the dark.
- **The vents become "the voice in the walls."** Bronson's voice, a séance, a victim the party knows, and a creature whose behavior follows rules the players can learn.
- **Room 808 has an intruder.** Someone got there first.
- **Politics is a board of power players**, with a mid-game swing where the party loses its access and must win it back.
- **Every outside location is optional.** Brown University, Federal Hill, and the geology convention are threads the party may choose to pull. Federal Hill comes to them if they do not go to it.
- **The finale is kept,** with the negotiation offers rewritten for this party and the binding rules converted cleanly to 7th edition.

### The shape of the night

1. **Scene 1 — The Meet-and-Greet** (6:00 pm, required). Private preview with the Mayor, Bronson, Councilman Sullivan, and Owen.
2. **Scene 2 — The Reception** (7:00 pm, required). Society, suspects, and marks.
3. **Scene 3 — The Heist** (about 9:30 pm, required). The jar falls. Bronson dies.
4. **Scene 4 — Police and Politics** (9:45 pm, required). The Mayor forces Drummond to let the party in.
5. **Scene 5 — The Lobby** (10:00 pm onward). Interviews against a release clock.
6. **Scene 6 — The Darkroom** (any time after 10:30 pm). Mary Mazars develops her film.
7. **Scene 7 — Room 808** (any time). Someone got there first.
8. **Scene 8 — The Voice in the Walls** (from about 11:30 pm). Séance, vents, the first victim.
9. **Scene 9 — City Hall Turns** (about 2:00 am). The party loses its access and must win it back.
10. **Act 3 — Threads** (optional, any order): Brown University, the Thornwood interrogation, Federal Hill, the geology convention, the coroner.
11. **Act 4 — Dawn** (about 6:00 am). Ibnhotep is whole. Bind him, or lose the hotel.

The required spine (Scenes 1–4, a little of 5, and Act 4) is about one session. Everything else expands to fill a second session.

### Three clocks

Run the night against three clocks. Tell the players the time whenever they ask, and say what the hotel is doing.

- **The Hotel Clock** (the creature). Hours since the heist, from 0 at about 9:30 pm to 9 at about 6:30 am. It drives attacks, victims, and the creature's strength. See *The Creature*.
- **The City Hall Clock** (the party's access). It starts at **Welcome**. Embarrassments, newspaper stories, and Sullivan's whispering push it toward **Shut Out**. Scene 9 fires it on schedule regardless.
- **The Release Clock** (the witnesses). Every 20 minutes of game time after 10:00 pm, lawyers or rank get another witness out of the lobby. See Scene 5.

## The Investigators

The party is not "society," and the adventure gets much of its flavor from that. They have been sent as a rich man's emissaries, so they are treated as VIPs by people who would normally never look at them. Play that feeling: the corsages, the Mayor's handshake, the flashbulb, the doorman who has to be told twice. Some of them will enjoy it. Some will feel like frauds. At least one of them *is* a fraud.

**Table conventions.** Racism exists in this 1921 world but stays off-stage. Class friction is fair game and is the main social texture here. Mercure's male persona is never turned into a plot threat (exposure, blackmail) unless the player asks for that story.

### Monsieur Mercure (Liang Mei-Yan) — the stage magician

- **Why they matter tonight:** Mercure has played the Keith-Albee houses in Providence. The Mayor's wife has seen the act and is delighted to meet "the Quicksilver Conjurer." Mercure is the party's VIP in the Mayor's eyes.
- **Hooks:**
  - Bronson asks Mercure for a trick at the meet-and-greet. Then, half-joking, she asks how a magician would steal the jar. That question is foreshadowing.
  - At the reception, a professional eye spots **Fox Iona** casing the cases. Iona is a thief who moves like a performer.
  - Mercure has spent a career exposing fake mediums. Tonight a fake medium (Blanche) will hear a real voice. Mercure is the right person to verify that no trick is involved, and the most shaken when none is.
- **Niche in play:** sleight of hand, locks, misdirection, keeping a crowd's eyes where they are wanted. Ideal for the séance (Scene 8) and for the binding, where someone has to keep the creature's attention.

### Blanche Cormier ("Madeleine Séraphine") — the spirit medium

*Player: Devon. Sheets in Drive: `Player Characters/Blanche_Cormier (Devon).pdf`.*

- **Who:** 30, from Biddeford, Maine; a Boston medium who works Beacon Hill parlours as "Madeleine Séraphine." Trained in cold reading and misdirection by "Aunt Vera," who ran séances in the North End. Charm 75, Sleight of Hand 70, Occult 60, Psychology 40. She carries the party's lycopodium flash powder.
- **Her own words:** "Every ghost I've ever conjured was me. Every single one. But some nights the room answers back in ways I didn't plan, and I haven't found the trick in that yet." Tonight the room answers back.
- **Why she matters tonight:** the reception is full of her natural prey. **Prudence Tillinghast** (72, newly divorced, bored, rich) and **Agnes Blackwood** (an heiress obsessed with Egypt) are exactly the clients she cultivates.
- **Hooks:**
  - At the meet-and-greet, Bronson asks lightly, "Can you tell me whether the dead are restless?" When Blanche goes into her patter beside the jar, the room goes cold and she hears something real. Call for a POW roll (her POW is 60); on a success she hears a whisper in a language she does not know. It is the remainder she has always wondered about.
  - After Bronson dies, Prudence begs "Madame Séraphine" for a sitting to "reach poor Dr. Bronson." This is the spine of Scene 8. The fraud runs a séance, and Bronson answers.
  - **Mrs. Adelaide Carruthers**, Blanche's possessive Boston client, has a sister in Providence and turns up at the reception, delighted, calling her "Madeleine" across the room. She is useful (she vouches for Blanche to Prudence) and dangerous (she is erratic, jealous of new clients, and talks to Paul Mazars). Use her as the leash Blanche's sheet describes.
  - Blanche is protective of people being hustled by anyone but herself. Sullivan working the Mayor, and Ives leaning on him, are hustles she will recognize on sight.
  - The binding prayer requires **belief**. The person least likely to believe in anything is the one who has spent years pretending to. Give Blanche the chance to find out whether she can mean it.
- **Risk:** if Blanche works a mark at the reception and the mark later recognizes the con, or Mrs. Carruthers makes a scene, that is a City Hall Clock embarrassment.
- **Mercure and Blanche:** Mercure has exposed mediums for a living, and Mercure's sheet names a rival Boston medium, Cornelius Vane, who stole half of Mercure's act. Blanche may know Vane, or know of him. Professional friction between the two is good table material; keep it collegial unless the players push.

### Iris Bose — the cab driver and union organizer

- **Why they matter tonight:** Iris drove the party down from Boston in her cab, with Merriwether paying the fare and expenses. That makes Iris the party's transport for every optional thread.
- **Hooks:**
  - The doorman tries to send her to the service entrance until the Mayor greets her by name. (Class, not race.)
  - **Harrison Ives** owns textile mills and breaks strikes. **Danny Walsh's** men are the muscle that breaks them, and Iris knows Walsh's crew by reputation; she may have seen Eddie Mars on a picket line. **Councilman Sullivan** is Walsh's friend at City Hall. The criminal and political threads run straight through Iris's world.
  - At the reception Iris spots a Walsh car (a black Packard with a dented running board) idling on Dorrance Street with a driver who is not dressed for a party.
- **Niche in play:** Drive Auto, Mechanical Repair, Intimidate. She can get the party to Brown, Federal Hill, or the newspaper in minutes. In the hotel's basement she understands the machinery.

### Owen Hartley — Bronson's curator-courier (the drop-in player)

*Sheets: `cthulhu/character-sheets/characters/owen.yaml` (character pages) and Drive `Player Characters/Owen_Hartley attribute pages.pdf`.*

- **Who:** 36, from Ardingly, West Sussex; a land agent's son who rose into the British Museum's scholarly ranks. Assistant Keeper in the Department of Egyptian and Assyrian Antiquities and curator-courier for the touring collection. Bronson has been his mentor since 1909. He is the grandson of Elisabeth Hartley, who bound the creature in 1879. The family told it as "Gran's tale of the jar," a ghost story.
- **War:** an infantry officer in the 1/4th Royal Sussex at Gallipoli and in Palestine. A machine-gun bullet through the right shoulder at Third Gaza (November 1917) left the arm weak (STR 35). He is calm under fire, quick with a field dressing (First Aid 50), better in a scuffle than he looks (Brawl 50), and a good shot with a shotgun from a boyhood of Sussex partridge and Yorkshire grouse (Rifle/Shotgun 50). Give him Drummond's shotgun and he will use it well.
- **Secret guilt:** he re-tied the jar's frayed binding in Southampton with linen tape. He suspects that is why "the jar moved." The player knows; the party does not until he tells them.
- **Niche:** the party's only scholar. History 70, Library Use 70, Language (Egyptian) 65, Appraise 60, Archaeology 50. He can read the tablet slowly, recognize the tyet and the shabti, and knows the 1879 story in its family-legend form.
- **Treasured possession:** a small blue-glazed shabti his grandmother kept on her desk "against the jar." It is consecrated to Isis: **the creature cannot perceive whoever carries it**. Nobody knows this yet.
- **If the player plays:** Owen is the party's emotional center for the curator's death, its knowledge engine, and its steadiest gun.
- **If the player does not return for session two:** Keeper's choice of exits:
  - Owen goes to Brown with Margaret Pemberton and returns with the ritual (he becomes the Brown thread, run off-screen).
  - Owen goes into the vents after Bronson's voice and does not come back. His voice joins hers in the walls, and his shabti is found in the duct (Scene 8).
  - Owen stays as an NPC ally with the party, carrying the shabti, and becomes the person who holds the container during the binding.

### Why the party stays involved

Do not rely on any single reason. Layer them:

1. **Bronson asked them for help, and they failed her.** She gave one of them her key (Scene 1).
2. **Merriwether sent them,** and the Mayor has publicly put the city's hospitality in their hands. Walking away embarrasses their patron.
3. **The creature marks them.** Anyone who touched the residue, the fragments, or the tyet is being hunted (see *What it hunts*).
4. **Her voice is in the walls.** From Scene 8 on, "we can free her" is the strongest motive in the adventure.
5. **Per-investigator stakes:** Mercure's need for something real, Blanche's first true contact, Iris's war with Walsh and Sullivan, and Owen's guilt.

## ACT 1: THE RECEPTION

### Scene 1: The Meet-and-Greet (6:00 pm)

**Where:** the 17th-floor ballroom of the Biltmore Hotel, an hour before the doors open. Display cases are lit, waiters are setting out the punch (officially dry; this is 1921), and the ballroom smells of new carpet and floor wax. The Ibnhotep jar stands alone on a central plinth under a glass case.

**Who is present:**

- **Mayor Joseph H. Gainer.** Genial, careful, facing re-election next year. He reads aloud from Merriwether's letter of introduction (Handout 1) and welcomes the party as "the personal emissaries of one of Providence's most beneficent friends." He means it, and he means them to tell Merriwether how well they were treated.
- **Mrs. Gainer.** A fan of Monsieur Mercure. A small kindness that makes the Mayor like the party.
- **Dr. Caitlin Bronson.** 50s, brisk, warm, funny, tired. Visibly relieved that Rupert's friends came.
- **Owen Hartley.** Bronson's aide. Hovering at the jar case, checking the cord around the jar's neck as if it might be gone.
- **Councilman Harold Sullivan.** 40s, handsome, glad-handing, with a very good watch. He is sizing up the party: can Merriwether's money be steered, and are these people going to be trouble?
- **Mary Mazars.** The *Providence Journal's* society photographer, borrowed from the wedding down the hall to take the Mayor's picture with the VIPs. Her flash is the first one of the night.

**Running the scene.** It is a 15–20 minute roleplay scene. Give each investigator one moment with Bronson. Do not hurry it. This is the scene that makes her death land.

**The tour.** Bronson walks them through the collection. Key objects:

- **The Ibnhotep jar:** a tall, sealed funerary vessel of red clay, its neck bound with ancient twine knotted in a pattern Owen calls "the knot of Isis." The cord looks newer in one place, a band of pale linen tape that Owen keeps glancing at. Spot Hidden notices the tape. If asked, Owen says it is a conservation repair (true, but not the whole truth).
- **The scarab amulet:** a large green-stone heart scarab in a side case. Bronson: "Ibnhotep's own. The texts say it was the seat of his power. I'd rather it were in London."
- **The shabti figures:** small funerary servants. Owen may mention that his grandmother kept one on her desk.
- **The tablet:** a hieratic inscription Bronson is still translating. "It's not a funerary text. It's instructions."

**Personal moments.** Pick one per investigator.

- **Mercure:** Bronson asks for a trick, and laughs with real delight at it. Then, quietly: "Tell me, if *you* wanted to steal that jar, how would you do it?" Let the player answer. Their answer is roughly what Fox Iona will do in three hours.
- **Blanche:** "Rupert says you speak to the dead. Can you tell me whether they are restless?" She is only half joking. If Blanche performs beside the jar, the room's temperature drops sharply and Blanche hears a whisper in old Egyptian (POW roll; on a success they hear it clearly; on a failure they *feel* it). Bronson goes pale: she felt it too. 0/1 Sanity.
- **Iris:** Bronson asks Iris how a woman got a hack license in Boston, and wants the real answer. If Sullivan overhears union talk, he turns cold and polite. Later, if Iris looks out a window onto Dorrance Street, she spots the Walsh Packard.
- **Owen:** Bronson, in front of the others, praises him extravagantly. "Owen is the best registrar the Museum has had in thirty years. His grandmother bound this collection once, you know. Family business." Owen looks sick.

**The ask.** Before the doors open, Bronson takes the investigator she connected with most aside. She presses her room key (No. 808) into their hand.

> "Put that in your pocket and don't argue. If anything happens tonight — and I'm aware how that sounds — my papers are in 808. Get them to Margaret Pemberton; she'll be here this evening, woman in blue. And look after Owen. He'll blame himself for everything that goes wrong between here and London."

If asked what she is afraid of: "That my predecessors were right about this jar and I have been very, very stupid." She touches something under her collar as she says it. Spot Hidden or Psychology: it is a necklace, and she touches it the way people touch a crucifix.

**Sullivan's moment.** At some point Sullivan takes one investigator aside (the one who seems most useful or most dangerous). He asks how long they are in town, whether Mr. Merriwether is "looking at other Providence projects," and offers "any help you need with the city, any help at all." Psychology: he is fishing, and he is nervous about something tonight. He keeps checking the time.

**What the scene leaves in play:**

- One investigator holds the key to room 808.
- The party has met Bronson, Owen, the Mayor, Sullivan, and Mary Mazars by name.
- Mercure has given the player's own heist plan; Blanche has heard something; Iris has seen a Walsh car.
- The Mayor likes the party. The City Hall Clock starts at **Welcome**.

### Scene 2: The Reception (7:00 pm)

The doors open. About eighty guests: city council, Rotary and Masons, old money, mill money, academics, the press. A string quartet. Hip flasks in the cloakroom.

Run this as a quick round of introductions and rumors rather than full conversations. Each investigator gets two or three encounters. Use the list below as a menu.

**Society and power:**

- **Harrison Ives** (mill owner): loud, bored, talks business. Recognizes nobody, unless Iris's name has ever been in a strike report.
- **Reginald Ashworth III** (old money, police commission): wants to know who the VIPs' people are.
- **Judge Philip Cronin** (federal bench): stiff, decent, out of place.
- **Edmund and Agnes Blackwood** (banker and Egypt-mad heiress): Agnes wants to talk Egypt with Owen and spirits with "Madame Séraphine."
- **Prudence Tillinghast** (socialite, 72): attaches herself to Blanche within minutes. "My late husband... well, my *former* husband, but he's dead to me."

**Academics:**

- **Dr. Margaret Pemberton** (Radcliffe, Egyptian mortuary practice, "the woman in blue"): talking urgently with Bronson in a corner. Her grandmother's family were temple guardians in Egypt. Cool to strangers until she learns they are Caitlin's friends.
- **Dr. Harold Rikards** (Yale geology): comes over from the convention down the hall to complain about the string quartet drowning out his paper on uranium deposits. Plant him now. If anyone is polite to him he shows them a lump of green torbernite from his pocket. "Radioactive. Perfectly harmless in small doses. Probably."

**The press:**

- **Paul Mazars** (society columnist, 62): knows everyone and everything. Mary's father.
- **Mary Mazars** (photographer, 24): back from the wedding, shooting the crowd. She is frustrated by fluff assignments and curious about the odd VIPs who don't fit. This is the start of her alliance with the party.

**The crew (playing guests):**

- **Fox Iona:** red hair, beautiful gown, too interested in the case locks. Mercure (or any Spot Hidden) notices her timing the guards.
- **Caroline Thornwood:** hovering near Bronson, making small talk that goes nowhere. Paul Mazars has never heard of her, which means she is not who she claims to be.
- **Danny Walsh:** in a good dinner jacket. Shakes Sullivan's hand. Iris knows the face, or Paul can name him ("our local businessman, in a manner of speaking").
- **Eddie Mars, Gilbert Angell, Gil Friar:** waiters' jackets that don't fit. Gerald the waiter thinks their shoes are wrong.

**Rumors (give one to each investigator who goes looking):**

- "Bronson's been nervous for a week. Wouldn't let the hotel move that jar." (Jeanette, waitress)
- "Sullivan's been in Walsh's pocket since the cab strike." (anyone working-class, or Iris)
- "The British Museum had trouble with that collection once. Before the war. The one before that." (Agnes Blackwood)
- "That red-haired woman isn't from Providence. I know everyone from Providence." (Paul Mazars)
- "There's a fellow from New York who's been writing the Museum, wanting to buy the collection outright." (Owen)

### Scene 3: The Heist (about 9:30 pm)

Keep the decision-phase structure from the first version: it worked. At each phase, describe the change, then ask each investigator what they do in the next few seconds. Their choices fill in the Heist Consequences list.

**Phase 1: The Robbery.** The lights stay on. Fox Iona stands on a chair: "This is a robbery! Stay calm and nobody gets hurt." Three gunmen in waiters' jackets draw revolvers at the doors.

*Decision:* move toward the jar and Bronson, take cover and watch, move toward the gunmen, protect a specific person, or grab the nearest artifact.

**Phase 2: The Theft.** Iona smashes the jewelry case. The gunmen sweep artifacts into sacks: the scarab goes into Iona's bag. Eddie Mars breaks the jar case. Bronson and Owen both shout, "Not that one!" Mars laughs: "Lady, it's a robbery, we're taking it all." As he reaches for the jar, it slips from his hands and falls toward Bronson.

- Investigators within 30 feet: Spot Hidden or Occult. The best success notices **the jar moved before Mars touched it.**
- Owen (if played) can dive for the jar: a DEX roll at a penalty die. He gets a hand to it and it slides through his fingers anyway. 0/1 Sanity for him: it was *warm*, and it *pushed*.

**Phase 2a: The Break.** The jar bursts into pieces. On a successful roll in Phase 2, it bursts *before* it hits the floor. Every light on the floor goes out.

*Decision in the dark:* rush a gunman (opposed roll to reach one), shout a warning to Bronson, or grab a specific display item (the shabti, the tablet, a jar fragment).

**Phase 3: The Release.** A long moan rises from the floor where the jar fell, and the air goes cold and wet. A gunman panics and fires: muzzle flashes light a crowd in chaos.

*Decision:*

- **Go toward Bronson.** Anyone who chooses this reaches her at the start of Phase 4 (see the tyet below).
- **Get light** (lighter, matches, a window curtain pulled open on the city).
- **Pursue the fleeing gunmen.**
- **Help bystanders toward the exits.**

**Caroline Thornwood's move.** Thornwood was assigned to keep Bronson away from the jar. In the dark, she does what a thief does. If **no investigator chose "Go toward Bronson,"** she feels the necklace at Bronson's throat while pretending to steady her, snaps the cord, and pockets the tyet.

**Phase 4: The Death.** Mary Mazars's flash goes off: a long, white instant.

- Spot Hidden or Occult: a haze, like heat shimmer full of shapes, hanging over the broken jar.
- Bronson screams.

**The tyet.** The creature cannot take Bronson while the tyet is at her throat. Run whichever case applies:

- **An investigator reached her.** Bronson knows what is coming. She tears the tyet from her own neck and forces it into the investigator's hand. "The knot — Isis — *keep it from him*." The moment it leaves her, the haze closes over her. The investigator holding the tyet feels the cold slide off them like water off oiled cloth.
- **Nobody reached her.** Thornwood has already taken it. Bronson clutches at her empty throat, and the haze takes her. Nobody sees why; Thornwood barely understands it herself, and she will spend the rest of the night trying not to.

Either way: anyone who succeeded at Spot Hidden or Occult makes a Sanity roll (0/1D4). On a failed Sanity roll they also *see* it: something drawing the life from her in a single breath, then streaming up toward the ceiling grate like smoke drawn up a chimney.

*Decision:* go to Bronson's body, secure items (jar fragments, the tablet, the shabti, the scarab if it is somehow still here), or go after the gunmen.

**Phase 5: The Escape.** The gang escapes down the service stairs unless the party seriously stops them. The lights come back on.

- Bronson is dead, desiccated, light as paper. There is no blood. (Sanity 0/1D3 to anyone who looks closely.)
- Caroline Thornwood is on the floor clutching a bleeding leg, crying that she's been shot. (It is glass from the case. If an investigator helps her, they learn it, and she is grateful in a way that becomes leverage later.)
- The Mayor takes charge: "Nobody leaves this room!" He has the Masons block the doors and sends Gerald to telephone the police.
- If Owen is present, he is on his knees by the jar fragments. The linen tape is there on the floor, still knotted.
- **Blanche** (if she is anywhere near the grate): a whisper from the duct, in a voice she heard an hour ago. Her own name. Nobody else hears it. 0/1 Sanity.

**Heist consequences** (record these; later scenes use them):

- **Reached Bronson:** that investigator holds the tyet and heard "the knot, Isis, keep it from him." The creature knows who has it.
- **Nobody reached Bronson:** Thornwood has the tyet. Recovering it becomes Thread B (required).
- **Captured a gunman:** fast route to Walsh; the gunman gives up Thornwood.
- **Helped Thornwood:** she owes you. Thread B starts with a bonus die.
- **Grabbed the shabti (from the case):** the holder cannot be perceived by the creature. Nobody knows that yet. (Owen carries a second shabti of his own.)
- **Grabbed jar fragments:** the binding's container is half-solved, and the fragments pull the creature toward whoever carries them.
- **Grabbed the tablet:** the translation source (Owen, Pemberton, or Brown can read it; Handout 3).
- **Helped bystanders:** grateful witnesses talk freely in Scene 5, and the City Hall Clock gets one step of goodwill.
- **Attacked the haze:** it knows that investigator's face.
- **The scarab** is gone with Fox Iona unless someone took it back during the heist. Whoever holds it is hunted hardest of all.

### Scene 4: Police and Politics (9:45 pm)

This worked at the table. Keep it and let it run.

- **Officers Hannigan and Nilsen** arrive first, secure the room, and start taking names. Hannigan is by the book. Nilsen wants to make detective.
- **Detective Benjamin Drummond** (homicide) arrives about 30 minutes later, pulled from a card game. He looks at the body for a long time and says nothing.
- **The Mayor** pressures Drummond, publicly, to let Merriwether's emissaries help. His line: *"I will not have Mr. Merriwether's guests, the guests of a man who has been so beneficent to this city, treated as suspects in a matter they're plainly equipped to help with."* Drummond resents it and complies.
- **Sullivan** objects, smoothly: "With respect, Joe, these are amateurs. Let the police work." Psychology: he wants the party *out*, and he is frightened. The Mayor overrules him (for now). Sullivan starts the City Hall Clock's slide (Scene 9).
- **If an investigator has already made friends with the Mayor** (likely Mercure, via Mrs. Gainer), the Mayor goes further and orders Drummond to *work with* them: shared findings, room access, introductions. This happened in the first run and it was a good moment. Let a player earn it.

**What Drummond can give:** room access (hotel cooperation follows his lead), official findings (autopsy, statements), introductions (coroner, precinct), and later police backup and a shotgun.

**What loses him:** a crime committed in front of him, a witness abused in his presence, or a lead that wastes his night. He gives exactly one warning: "I don't know what you think you're doing, but if I see it again, I won't have a choice."

**Drummond's disposition** runs on four steps: **Hostile, Resentful, Working, Ally**. He starts at **Resentful**. Each solid lead moves him one step up; each embarrassment moves him one step down.

## ACT 2: THE NIGHT AT THE BILTMORE

From here the party chooses where to go. Scenes 5 through 8 can happen in any order, and the party can split. Scene 9 fires on the clock. Keep the Hotel Clock moving: every scene costs time, and every hour the hotel gets worse.

---

### Scene 5: The Lobby (10:00 pm onward)

The guests have been moved to the Biltmore's lobby. Wealthy people who have never been told "no" by a policeman are being told "no" by a policeman. Drummond takes statements one at a time in a side office. The party can sit in on Drummond's interviews (if he is at **Working** or better), work the room on their own, or leave for other scenes.

**The Release Clock.** Starting at 10:00 pm, every 20 minutes of game time someone with rank or a lawyer gets a witness released. Once released, a witness can still be found later, but only by going to them (a home, a club, a hotel room), which costs time. Release order:

1. 10:20 pm — **Harrison Ives** (his lawyer arrives in a Pierce-Arrow).
2. 10:40 pm — **Reginald Ashworth III** (one call to the police commission).
3. 11:00 pm — **Judge Cronin** (nobody detains a federal judge for long).
4. 11:20 pm — **The Blackwoods** (Edmund's bank's counsel).
5. 11:40 pm — **Paul Mazars** (he has a story to file; Mary stays if the party asks her).
6. Midnight — **Prudence Tillinghast** (unless Blanche has promised her a séance, in which case she refuses to leave).
7. 12:20 am — everyone else except Thornwood, who is held.

This gives the party a real choice: talk to the right people now, or leave to chase leads and lose them. In the first run the players enjoyed exactly this tension; the clock makes it explicit.

**Who knows what** (full entries in the *Cast* section):

- **Caroline Thornwood:** the gang, Walsh, the warehouse, and possibly the tyet in her bandage. She is held for Drummond. See Thread B.
- **Margaret Pemberton:** what the creature is, the binding, the tyet. Talks if approached as Caitlin's friends. Wants to go to Brown tonight.
- **Edmund Blackwood:** saw "a shadow" pour into the ceiling grate, and points out exactly which one.
- **Agnes Blackwood:** saw Bronson's necklace earlier ("a tyet: the knot of Isis; I know it from the Budge catalogue"). Has copies of Bronson's correspondence at home.
- **Paul Mazars:** Thornwood isn't Providence society. Iona was casing the room. Sullivan and Walsh shook hands at 8 pm.
- **Mary Mazars:** has the film. Wants to develop it now. See Scene 6.
- **Prudence Tillinghast:** saw Iona's face clearly before the robbery; saw Thornwood chatting with "rough men." Wants a séance. See Scene 8.
- **Harrison Ives:** knows Walsh's men from his mills. Offers money if it stays quiet. Dislikes Iris on sight if he knows her name.
- **Judge Cronin:** Walsh has been before his court. He suspects someone at City Hall protects Walsh. Can sign warrants if evidence is proper.
- **Reginald Ashworth:** police commission. Can lean on Drummond or the Mayor, either direction.
- **Rikards:** not in the lobby. He's down the hall at the convention and never left. See Thread D.
- **Gerald and Jeanette (waiters):** the gunmen's wrong shoes; staff passages; Bronson touching her necklace all evening; the kitchen (honey).
- **Hannigan:** the vents near the ballroom are ice-cold and smell of copper.
- **Owen:** the registrar's ledger lists every piece, so he can say exactly what the gang took, and that the scarab was in Iona's bag.

### Scene 6: The Darkroom (any time after 10:30 pm)

**Where:** Mary's darkroom at the *Providence Journal* building on Fountain Street, a few minutes' walk (or a one-minute cab ride with Iris).

**Getting there:** Mary is an ally by now if the party was decent to her at the meet-and-greet and the reception. She is ambitious, underestimated, and an outsider in her own profession, and she sees the same in them. She develops the film willingly but wants to be in the room, and she wants the story eventually.

**Developing takes about an hour.** What the prints show:

- **The creature.** A smear of translucent shapes over the broken jar, with something like a face. 0/1 Sanity. Anyone who studies it gets a bonus die on Sanity rolls against the creature for the rest of the night: they've seen it already.
- **Eddie Mars with the jar,** the jar already tilting *away* from his hands.
- **The crowd before the robbery,** with Thornwood standing next to Walsh, and Sullivan shaking Walsh's hand. This print is political dynamite (Scene 9).
- **Bronson's death:** partly obscured, but showing the haze closing on her and her hand at her empty throat. 1/1D4 Sanity. It proves the tyet left her neck *before* she died.

**The attack (fair version).** The creature does not care about photographs. It attacks the darkroom **only if** someone present carries something it hunts: residue on their clothes, jar fragments, the scarab, or the tyet (see *What it hunts*). If so, about 45 minutes in, a proto-shoggoth flows out of the building's heating duct.

- The room is cramped: penalty die on Fighting.
- Developing chemicals are improvised weapons. Acetic stop bath stings; silver nitrate burns.
- Mary hides and screams. If the party saves her, she is a friend for life, and her father becomes the party's champion in print.

**If nothing the party carries is hunted,** there is no attack. Instead, as the last print comes up, the darkroom's heating grate breathes cold and a woman's voice says, very clearly, "Mary." Mary drops the tray.

### Scene 7: Room 808 (any time)

**Getting in:** an investigator has Bronson's key (Scene 1), and Owen has the spare. No skill roll is needed. This is a promise kept, not a break-in.

**Someone got there first.** When the party arrives, the door is ajar and the room has been searched: drawers out, mattress slit, papers everywhere. Councilman Sullivan has told Walsh that Voss now wants Bronson's translations, since the jar is broken. Walsh sent **Gil Friar**, still in his ill-fitting waiter's jacket, to get them.

Choose one of the two versions, or let the clock choose: before **Hotel Clock hour 2**, use the chase; after it, use the drained intruder.

**Version A: Catch him at it.** Friar is still in the bathroom, stuffing papers into a pillowcase. He bolts through the connecting door and down the service stairs.

- A chase through the staff corridors (DEX or Climb rolls, Gerald's shortcuts if he is helping, and Iris knows how a building's back passages work).
- If caught: Friar is muscle, not brains. He knows Walsh sent him, that "the councilman" told Walsh what to get, and that "some German in New York" is paying. He has a jar fragment in his pocket as a souvenir. *That* is why the creature has been following him.
- Drummond will want him; Walsh will want him dead.

**Version B: The intruder found.** Friar is on the floor by the window, desiccated to the waist, alive and whispering. The creature followed the jar fragment in his pocket, fed, and left when someone came down the hall.

- 0/1D4 Sanity.
- He can say three things before he dies: "Walsh sent me," "the councilman," and "it talked to me in *her* voice."

**What the room still holds** (the papers are scattered but nothing important was taken):

- **Bronson's diary.** The key entries are in Handout 4. The last entry, written after the meet-and-greet, is about the party: *"Rupert's friends came. One of them heard it too. I feel less mad tonight than I have in a month."*
- **The translation folder:** her working translation of the tablet, nearly complete (Handout 3).
- **British Museum correspondence:** warnings about "the documented history of the Ibnhotep collection," a reference to the Hartley monograph of 1903, and a cool note from Deputy Keeper Dr. Lionel Fanshawe declining to send "Mrs. Hartley's private papers."
- **A letter from Voss** on New York letterhead, offering to buy the collection outright, "the vessel in particular, provided it remains sealed."
- **A photograph** of Bronson and Pemberton at a dig, signed "M.P. knows the old stories."
- **The touring ledger copy:** if Owen is not played, the condition report for the jar reads *"Southampton: cord repaired, condition unchanged — O.H."* in his hand. A Psychology roll on Owen when he sees it: he goes white.

**The creature and the room.** The room is a danger only if the party lingers more than 30 minutes while carrying something it hunts. Then a proto-shoggoth comes through the airshaft window.

### Scene 8: The Voice in the Walls (from about 11:30 pm)

This scene replaces the "spirit in the vents" sequence, which dragged in the first run because there was nothing to do in it. Now the vents have a voice, a victim, and a choice.

**The setup.** From about Hotel Clock hour 2, Bronson's voice is in the building's ductwork.

- Staff report "a lady calling for help in the walls" on the 8th and 17th floors.
- A guest on 12 complains to the desk that a woman is crying in his ventilator.
- Hannigan, on watch in the ballroom, hears his own name called and goes white.

**Is it really her?** Yes and no. Ibnhotep holds Bronson's ka. Sometimes the voice is Bronson, terrified and lucid, trying to warn them. Sometimes it is Ibnhotep using her voice as a lure. Players should not be able to tell which is which, and should come to *want* to tell.

- **Bronson herself** speaks in fragments and knows things only she knew: details of the meet-and-greet, the trick Mercure did, the joke she made to Iris. She warns: "Don't come into the walls. It wants the knot."
- **The lure** is smoother, calmer, and wants something: "Bring me the necklace, I can't breathe." "Owen, come down to me."

**The séance.** The best way into this scene is Blanche. Prudence Tillinghast (and probably Agnes Blackwood) begs for a sitting "to reach poor Dr. Bronson." It is Blanche's ordinary con: a dark room, joined hands, and a few raps.

- Hold it in a hotel parlour on the 17th floor. Mercure can help stage it, or can sit in the circle and watch for tricks.
- Blanche begins the patter. Let the player perform it. Then the room's grate breathes cold, and **Bronson answers.**
- She speaks through the vent, then, chillingly, through Blanche's own mouth if she fails a POW roll. She is not possessed; she is a better conductor than she knew. 1/1D4 Sanity for everyone at the table, and 1/1D6 for Blanche.
- What she says (mix the true and the lure):
  - "Thank you for coming." (true)
  - "He is in the shaft below the cellars. He's so hungry." (true)
  - "There are so many of us in here." (true; first hint of the consumed ka)
  - "Bring me the necklace. Please." (the lure)
- Mercure gets a clean read: there is no trick in the room. They checked. 0/1D3 Sanity for a professional skeptic.
- Prudence faints. Agnes is ecstatic. The story will be all over Providence by breakfast (City Hall Clock: one step toward **Shut Out**, unless the party contains it).

**Following the voice.** If the party follows the voice through the ducts or the service passages, run it as a cat-and-mouse scene. Ibnhotep is weak (before hour 5 he cannot fight the party head-on), so he uses proto-shoggoths and the voice to split them up and lure one investigator off alone. The service stairs, the laundry chutes, and the maintenance level are all in play.

**The first victim they know.** Somewhere during this scene (around hour 3), the creature takes someone the party met. Pick based on who they liked:

- **Jeanette** the waitress, found in the linen room. The default choice.
- **Officer Nilsen,** who went into the vents alone to make detective.
- **Prudence Tillinghast,** if she stayed for the séance and wandered back to "speak to Dr. Bronson again."
- **Owen,** if his player has left and you use that exit. His shabti is found in the duct where he went in, and from then on there are two voices in the walls.

From then on the victim's voice joins Bronson's. That is the horror: the voices in the walls are the people the party met tonight.

**The maintenance level.** Following the trail down (or asking Gerald, or Iris reading the building like an engine) leads to the basement.

- **Ventilation blueprints** in the engineer's office (Library Use or INT): every duct route leads to the central elevator shaft. A disused section between basement levels B1 and B2 is the lair. The ducts on the south side of the 17th floor are "blocked," because the geology convention is there (Thread D).
- **Signs:** wet, faintly phosphorescent residue; ducts torn wider; drag marks; a single shoe; the smell of copper.
- **Danger:** going deeper toward the shaft draws an ambush of 1–3 proto-shoggoths, depending on the hour.
- Supplies: rope, lanterns, tools, a staff uniform.

### Scene 9: City Hall Turns (about 2:00 am, Hotel Clock hour 4–5)

This scene repeats what worked at the table, with the stakes raised. The party won its access through the Mayor; now Sullivan takes it away, and the party has to win it back.

**What happens.** Sullivan has spent the night quietly working on the Mayor. Now three things land at once:

- **The late edition.** Paul Mazars's story is on the street: "MURDER AT THE BILTMORE: Mayor's Guests at Center of Bizarre Death." If the séance happened, there is a paragraph about it.
- **Harrison Ives** telephones the Mayor from his club: the city looks ridiculous, and his donations to the re-election committee depend on it not looking ridiculous.
- **Sullivan** delivers the conclusion: "Joe, these people are a fortune-teller, a vaudeville act, and a strike agitator. Merriwether's a sick man who doesn't know what he sent you. Cut them loose before it's your name in that paper."

The Mayor, apologetic but firm, withdraws the party's access. Drummond is told to send them back to their rooms. **The City Hall Clock reads Shut Out.** Drummond's reaction depends on his disposition: at **Working** or **Ally** he looks the other way ("I didn't see you"); below that, he enforces it.

**Ways to win it back** (any one restores access; two make the Mayor an active ally):

- **The Merriwether lever.** Telephone Merriwether long-distance (the hotel switchboard can place it). He cannot be argued with and cannot be talked out of anything, but he can send a telegram to the Mayor by 4:00 am. Use it once; it is the trump card.
- **Expose Sullivan.** Mary's photograph of Sullivan shaking Walsh's hand, Friar's "the councilman," Thornwood's testimony, or Judge Cronin's suspicions. Shown to the Mayor in private, it flips him completely. Shown in public, it also starts a political war that lasts beyond the adventure.
- **Make Drummond the hero.** Hand Drummond a solid lead (Friar, Thornwood's confession, Walsh's warehouse) and let him take credit. A detective with a case moving forward gives the Mayor cover.
- **Social leverage.** Ashworth (police commission), Judge Cronin (procedure), Agnes Blackwood (money), or Ives himself, if someone can make him owe them.
- **Ignore it.** The party can keep working without permission. Every scene now needs Stealth, staff uniforms, or Gerald's passages. Being caught by a policeman is a real problem.

**Sullivan afterward.** If the party exposes him, he goes straight to Walsh. Walsh decides Sullivan is a liability. Whether the party saves Sullivan from that is up to them.

## ACT 3: THREADS

These are the places the party can choose to go. All are optional except Thread B when Thornwood has the tyet. Each thread provides one pillar of the finale:

- **Method** (how to bind it): Thread A, Brown University. Backups: Bronson's translations in room 808, Owen, Pemberton in person.
- **The tyet** (the one irreplaceable item): the heist, or Thread B.
- **Weapons** (what hurts it): Thread D, the geology convention. Backups: acid from the coroner (Thread E), Walsh's guns and dynamite (Thread C), hotel kitchen honey.
- **Intelligence** (how it behaves): Thread B, Thread C, Scene 8.

A party that pulls none of the optional threads still has the tyet, Bronson's translation, and Pemberton or Owen. That is enough to attempt the binding, at real cost. A party that pulls two or three threads walks into the finale prepared.

**Splitting the party.** The first group split three ways and it worked: one group to Brown, one interrogating Thornwood, one at the geology convention. Cut between groups every 15–20 minutes of real time. Iris's cab makes Brown and Federal Hill reachable in about ten minutes each.

---

### Thread A: Brown University — the John Hay Library

**Opens:** Pemberton asks to go to Brown tonight ("Caitlin's notes are not enough, and the library has Hartley"), or the party decides to research.

**Who:** Pemberton (guide), Professor Ambrose Worthington (elderly, delighted, lectures at length), and whichever investigators go. Owen belongs here if played.

**Cost:** about 2–3 hours of game time.

**Beat 1: Pemberton's trust.** Pemberton asks, bluntly: "Do you believe that what killed Caitlin was real?"

- One investigator must succeed: Occult, Psychology (affirm her experience honestly), Anthropology, or Persuade (Hard).
- **Blanche's moment:** if Blanche answers with the truth ("I've faked it for years, and tonight I didn't"), that is an automatic success. The truth is the thing Pemberton respects.
- **Success:** she opens her family notebook. Bonus die on Library Use and Occult rolls here, and Beat 4 opens.
- **Failure:** she helps, but holds the notebook back. No Beat 4.

**Beat 2: The binding ritual.** Library Use (Regular) finds Elisabeth Hartley's 1903 monograph, *Anomalous Containment Practices in Late Period Egyptian Temple Ritual.* Owen knows the title, so with him present the roll gets a bonus die.

- **Success:** the complete procedure and the prayer text (Handout 5). The party can perform the binding without Pemberton.
- **Failure:** Pemberton can reconstruct the steps but not the words. She must be physically present at the binding to speak them.
- **Secondary roll, Archaeology or History:** understanding *why* each step works.
  - The container need not be the original jar: any vessel holding fragments of the original carries its blessing.
  - Honey is not a symbol. The creature cannot pass through it. A honey-coated threshold blocks proto-shoggoths, and honey on the creature freezes that part of it for 1D3 rounds.

**Beat 3: The 1879 letters.** Hartley's correspondence is in the restricted cage. Get in by the librarian's spare key (Spot Hidden), Locksmith (Mercure, easily), STR (Hard, noisy), or Pemberton talking to the night watchman.

- **Library Use success**, which gives the Hartley Method:
  - The creature is drawn to fragments of its own vessel. Fragments in an open container are bait.
  - "The creature could not perceive Mr. Adair, who carried the little Isis servant." This confirms the shabti. Owen goes very quiet and takes his grandmother's shabti out of his pocket.
  - "Four of us spoke it. We lost Dr. Marsh in the third round. The binding held regardless: the prayer requires belief, not survival." A speaker who falls mid-prayer still counts.
- **Psychology (Hard):** Hartley did not believe it would work. Terror and need became belief in the moment. Every speaker may roll their belief check at Regular difficulty (see the binding rules).

**Beat 4: The consumed ka** (requires Pemberton's trust). No roll needed.

> "My grandmother's family said he is not a demon. He is a hunger. A man who ate so many souls he lost his shape. The souls are not dead. They are inside him."

This turns Bronson's voice into a goal: **if the binding is spoken with an appeal to the consumed dead, the souls are released to rest instead of being sealed in with him.** Mechanically: it adds a fourth round to the prayer, halves the creature's STR for escape contests, and prevents the *Consumed Ka Event* in the finale.

- **Optional, Occult (Hard):** the tyet is an active ward. Whoever wears it suffers only half the creature's drain and feels it within 100 feet as cold pressure.

**Interruption:** if someone brought jar fragments, the scarab, or residue to Brown, there is a 25% chance per hour that a proto-shoggoth arrives through the library's basement vents and goes for the restricted cage.

**Fallback if nobody goes to Brown:** Bronson's translation (room 808) and Pemberton, wherever she is, can supply the ritual steps and the prayer. Owen, if present, can make the Archaeology roll himself. Without Brown, the party lacks the Hartley Method, the belief shortcut, and the consumed ka unless Pemberton tells them in person.

---

### Thread B: Thornwood and the tyet

**Opens:** immediately; Thornwood is held in Drummond's side office. **Required if Thornwood took the tyet in the heist.**

**Where is the tyet?** In the gauze bandage on her leg. She stuffed it there when the lights came on. If the party helped her in Phase 5, they may have touched it without knowing. If Drummond's people searched her, they missed it.

**Drummond's opening:** he lays out the facts. Seen near the gunmen, wound is glass not a bullet, didn't run. Cooperate or be charged as an accessory to robbery and murder. Thornwood denies everything. Then Drummond turns to the party: "Your turn. The Mayor seems to think you people have some special insight."

**Breaking her** (one roll; one retry at Hard with a different skill):

- **Psychology:** she is not afraid of prison. She is afraid of Walsh and of whatever she saw. Name the real fears and she cracks.
- **Intimidate:** she breaks fast and gives up Walsh, then gives nothing more willingly.
- **Persuade (Hard)** or **Charm (Hard):** offer protection from Walsh *and* from the thing in the walls. She becomes cooperative and gives more.
- **If she was helped in the heist:** bonus die on any of these.
- **The tyet confession.** If Thornwood took it, she gives it up once broken, and it comes out as guilt: "I took it off her. In the dark. It was just a necklace. And then she —" She cannot finish. The investigator who was Bronson's friend now knows Thornwood's theft killed her. 0/1D3 Sanity. Let that investigator decide what Thornwood is owed.
- **Pushed failures:** keep the first version's pushed-roll consequences. A pushed Psychology failure means she thinks the party works for Walsh and demands a lawyer. A pushed Intimidate failure means a panic attack and Drummond throws the questioner out. A pushed Persuade failure means she calls the bluff and Drummond confirms the party can't promise anything. A pushed Charm failure means she turns the investigator's sympathy against them (1 Sanity) and talks only to Drummond.

**What she gives** once cooperating (pick one in depth; a second needs Persuade (Hard) while Drummond grows impatient):

- **Walsh and the warehouse.** Walsh organized it. The warehouse is on Federal Hill (Atwells Avenue, behind a macaroni importer). The crew is Iona, Mars, Angell, Friar. "The councilman" set it up. This opens Thread C.
- **The buyer.** A German in New York, "some professor," who called the jar "the priest's vessel" and said it must be delivered **sealed**. Walsh got the money through Sullivan. If this reaches the Brown group, Worthington identifies Dr. Heinrich Voss, who wrote a 1911 monograph on Ibnhotep. That monograph has an emergency trap: a circle of salt and iron filings holds the creature in place for 2D6 rounds. It buys time during the binding.
- **What Eddie saw.** Eddie told her on the lobby telephone that the jar jumped out of his hands, that something cold tried to get *into* him, and that it went straight for Bronson "like it knew her." The lesson: **the creature targets whoever threatens it most.** In the finale it will go for whoever holds the tyet, and then for whoever is speaking the prayer.

**Drummond afterward:**

- **Clean break:** he moves up a step. "Not bad."
- **Walsh plus more:** he moves up a step and hands one investigator a 12-gauge shotgun. "I'll want that back."
- **Failure or abuse:** he moves down a step. He still gets Walsh's name himself, two hours later.

### Thread C: Federal Hill (optional)

**Opens:** Walsh's warehouse location from Thornwood, Friar, Iris's own knowledge of Walsh's operation, or Drummond.

**What the party finds** (around Hotel Clock hour 4–6): the creature got there first. It followed the scarab in Fox Iona's bag across the city through the sewers.

- The warehouse door is open. Walsh's guards are dead, desiccated, in the loading bay. Bottles of bonded whiskey are smashed in the aisles. 1/1D4 Sanity.
- **Eddie Mars** is barricaded in the office, drunk and shaking, with a revolver he has already emptied into the dark. He touched the jar and something tried to get into him. Now it is in his dreams.
- **Fox Iona** is on the roof with the scarab in her fist. She has worked out that it is what the thing wants. She will not let go of it, because she suspects that holding it is the only reason she isn't dead.
- **Danny Walsh** is gone. He ran when the screaming started, and he is now furious, frightened, and dangerous to Sullivan.

**What the party can get:**

- **The scarab.** Iona will trade it for protection from Walsh and from the creature. Whoever holds it can try to command the creature once (opposed POW against POW 100). If that fails, they become its primary target. It is a desperate gamble, and also bait.
- **Walsh's armory:** shotguns, a box of dynamite, a Thompson gun in a violin case. These are useful, illegal, and noticed by Drummond.
- **Iona herself:** a cool professional who shot at the creature and watched the bullets go through. She tells them flatly that guns don't work on it.
- **Walsh, later:** if the party finds him (Iris knows the speakeasies he uses), he will give men, cars, and guns to have the thing dead. His men will run from it. He wants immunity and will expect it.

**Proto-shoggoth:** one is still in the building, feeding on a guard in the cold room.

**If the party doesn't go:** Federal Hill comes to them. At about hour 6, **Fox Iona** turns up at the Biltmore's service entrance, alone, with the scarab, asking for "the people from Boston." She wants to trade it for protection. Drummond wants to arrest her. The creature is right behind her, and it has just brought the scarab back into the hotel.

### Thread D: The Geology Convention (optional; strongly encouraged)

**Opens:** any time. The convention is down the hall from the ballroom on the 17th floor, and Rikards was introduced at the reception. In the first run, the party that engaged here got the most powerful weapon in the adventure.

**The secret:** the convention rooms are full of radioactive mineral samples, and the creature cannot abide radiation. **The whole south wing of the 17th floor is a safe zone.** The residue trail curves away from it, and the ducts there are "blocked."

**Beat 1: Getting in.** The geologists are cliquish and half-drunk, arguing about continental drift. Any one of these works:

- Fast Talk ("I'm with the exhibition; we need a chemist"), Credit Rating, or APP. Being a famous conjurer counts. Mercure is good here.
- Find the convention bar. Rikards's graduate student **Thomas Yee** drinks there, is starstruck by performers, and will introduce anyone to his advisor. Thomas has Science (Chemistry) 45% and Science (Geology) 35% and can make rolls for a party that lacks them.
- Rikards already met the party at the reception. If anyone was polite to him, he is pleased to see them.

**Beat 2: Convincing Rikards.** Rikards is a materialist and will stay one. Frame it as a problem, not a haunting.

- **Scientific frame** (Persuade or Fast Talk): "an organism that is fluid, regenerates, and drains moisture from tissue." He gives **three torbernite samples**, each making a 5-foot radius in which the creature cannot regenerate and proto-shoggoths dissolve in 1D6 rounds.
- **Showman's frame** (Fast Talk or Persuade, with evidence: residue, a photograph, a vivid witness): he examines it and becomes *invested*. Torbernite **plus** Rikards as an active ally.
- **Failure:** Thomas Yee can make a Science roll to bring him round. Otherwise the party can steal one sample (Sleight of Hand; Mercure).

**Beat 3: The radiation device** (Rikards invested only). Lead-lined sample boxes, a copper reflector, and every sample he has, built over about 3 hours with someone handy assisting (Mechanical Repair: Iris is ideal).

- **The device:** a 15-foot cone. In the cone the creature cannot regenerate and takes 1D3 damage a round, and proto-shoggoths dissolve in 1 round. It is two-handed, heavy, and fragile (Luck roll if dropped). It works for about 30 minutes before the arrangement degrades.
- **Failure:** the samples are still usable; there is no time to rebuild.

**Beat 4: Supplies** (Spot Hidden): hydrochloric acid (1D6 damage per splash, stops regeneration where it lands), two Bunsen burners, lead-lined sample boxes. On a failure, the party takes acetic acid by mistake, which is useless; a Science (Chemistry) roll catches it.

**The safe zone** becomes the party's headquarters for planning the finale.

### Thread E: The Coroner (optional, short)

**Opens:** Drummond's introduction, or a Stealth (Hard) break-in at the Rhode Island Hospital morgue.

**Dr. Marcus Webb** has had the body for three hours and is frightened. "Every cell is dry. Not dried. *Emptied.* Something fed on her." Medicine confirms it was life-force, not just water.

**What the party gets:** acid from the cabinet, rubber aprons and gloves, a sober ally who will testify to "an unknown chemical agent" if Drummond needs a story afterward. And one detail: a fine abrasion around Bronson's neck, the mark of a cord snapped from behind. If Thornwood took the tyet, this proves it.

## ACT 4: DAWN

### The convergence (about 5:00–6:00 am)

The party gathers. The geology convention rooms (the safe zone) are best if they found them; otherwise, Mary's darkroom, a locked hotel suite with honey on the threshold, or Iris's cab parked on Dorrance Street.

**Lay out what they have.** Ask the players to list it aloud. It makes the plan theirs. Prompt if they stall:

- Who wears the tyet? Who holds the container? Who speaks the prayer, and how is the 15 Magic Points split?
- Who carries the torbernite, or the device?
- Do they go down to the shaft, or bait the creature up to them with the jar fragments?
- What's the plan if the binding fails?
- Do they use the appeal to the consumed dead? (If they learned it, this is where they decide whether freeing Bronson is worth an extra round.)

Give them 15–20 minutes of real time. Then the creature forces the issue.

**The forcing event.** The hotel's power dies. Red emergency lamps. Screaming from the floors below. The elevator cables part with a sound like a gunshot and the car falls nine floors into the basement. The building shudders. Drummond, from the stairwell: "We're evacuating. Whatever you people are going to do, do it now."

---

### Ibnhotep as a person

He was a priest: intelligent, proud, and desperate to survive. Three thousand years in the jar sharpened all of that. He is not a beast and does not fight like one.

**He speaks** in two ways:

- **Projection:** pressure behind the eyes, a taste of copper, and then meaning without words, within 30 feet.
- **The consumed:** in the voices of the people he has eaten. Tonight that includes Bronson, the victim from Scene 8, and anyone else taken during the night. He uses their memories, including Bronson's memories of the meet-and-greet. He knows the party through her eyes.

**His goals, in order:**

1. Destroy the tyet.
2. Kill whoever is speaking the binding.
3. Get out of any radiation field.
4. Survive.
5. If all else fails, bargain.

He retreats from losing positions, feints, targets the most dangerous person rather than the nearest, and tries to split the party.

---

### First contact

Wherever the party meets him, the first moment is the same. He does not attack. Bronson's voice, through the walls and the pipes:

> "I know what you're carrying. The knot. The stones. The pieces of my house. I've felt them all night. Caitlin liked you, you know. She thought you were kind. Let's be kind to each other."

The glow brightens. He is enormous: a tide of translucent flesh filling the space, shapes moving inside it, a hand pressing out against the surface from within, fingers spread, then sinking back. 1D6/1D20 Sanity on first full sight. (Investigators who studied Mary's photograph get a bonus die on this roll.)

**The party's options:**

- **Attack.** The fight starts at full intensity.
- **Listen.** While he talks, he does not attack. He is also moving proto-shoggoths into the ducts behind the party. After 3 rounds of talk, one strikes the most exposed investigator: "Forgive that. My children are hungry." It is a test.
- **Pretend to listen while getting into position.** Fast Talk or Stealth per investigator, opposed by his INT 80. If he notices, he attacks with a bonus die. Mercure is the natural lead here: misdirection is the job.
- **Bargain.** See *The Offers*.

**The shabti.** Whoever carries a shabti is invisible to him. They can walk right up to him, set the container, and lay honey, as long as they don't touch or attack him. The moment they do, he sees them, and someone he couldn't see is the most frightening thing in the room. They get one free action. Make it count.

---

### The Offers

He makes these from strength, early (in first contact), or from desperation (below 10 HP). He reads each investigator through Bronson's memories and offers each the thing they want most. Each offer is real. Each costs time: every round spent listening or accepting is a round he regenerates, unless radiation is suppressing him.

**To Mercure:**

> "Twenty years of false miracles. You have exposed every medium you ever met, and every time you hoped to fail. I am the miracle. Let me teach you one true working. Not a trick. Something real, that will save your life someday."

*If accepted:* Mercure learns the **Elder Sign** (the spell, with its normal Sanity cost). There is no opposed roll. He means it. It is a bribe, and a real one, and he hopes a magician with a real spell might one day be persuaded to use it for him.

**To Blanche:**

> "You have pretended to hear the dead for years. Tonight you heard them. Let me open the door the rest of the way, and you will never have to pretend again. And Caitlin can stay. You could speak with her whenever you like."

*If accepted:* opposed POW roll. **Success:** Blanche can truly hear the dead from now on (a lasting Keeper hook), at a cost of +5 Cthulhu Mythos and the matching Sanity loss. **Failure:** 1D10 Sanity, and a lingering whisper that is not Bronson. The second half of the offer is a lie: Bronson stays inside *him*, not with Blanche.

**To Iris:**

> "The man who breaks your strikes. The councilman who keeps him safe. I tasted them both on the fat one's thoughts tonight. Open the door and I will go to them first. Before dawn. No one will ever know it was you."

*There is no mechanical payoff.* It is true: he would. Then he would keep going. It is the most dangerous offer in the room because the person receiving it has real enemies. (Optionally, he adds: "Your old man knew about things like me. Ask me how he died." Whether he knows anything about Yakov is the Keeper's call.)

**To Owen** (if present):

> "Caitlin knows about Southampton. About the tape. She forgives you. Let me go, and I will let her go. You will hear her say it."

*It is a lie.* If he is released, Bronson stays inside him. Owen's player may not know that. A Psychology roll suggests it; the consumed ka appeal is the only true way to free her.

**To whoever wears the tyet:**

> "She wore that because she was afraid of me. She was right to be. But the binding isn't forever. It held three thousand years because nobody opened the jar. How long until someone opens the next one? You're sealing me away for someone else to deal with."

*This is not an offer.* It is true. What the party does with the container afterward is the epilogue.

---

### The fight

**Phases by his hit points:**

- **Above 20 HP: Predator.** Pseudopods keep the party apart. Drain tendrils weaken anyone alone. He uses psychic assault on whoever is giving orders. In the first two rounds he spawns a proto-shoggoth each round. He goes after the tyet.
- **10–20 HP: Desperate.** He stops spawning. Each round he tries to engulf the weakest investigator. He flees radiation across ceilings and through ducts and makes them chase. He speaks in Bronson's voice: "Please, it hurts, it's always hungry, I can feel everyone it's ever eaten —" That is really her.
- **Below 10 HP: Cornered.** If radiation is suppressing him, every attack goes at the radiation source; if it drops, he regenerates and the fight resets. If the binding has begun, every attack goes at the speaker and he ignores everything else. Otherwise, he bargains.
- **0 HP: The window.** He collapses into a trembling mass. He is not dead. Without radiation he regenerates 5 HP a round. Under radiation he stays at 0 and can still use psychic assault. Everyone within 30 feet hears the consumed all at once (1/1D6 Sanity, once).

**Rounds 1–2: the tyet.** He lashes at whoever wears the tyet, not to kill but to tear it loose. If he gets it, he draws it into his body. It resists for 1D6 rounds; if not recovered by then, it is destroyed, and the binding is impossible. *Decision for the wearer:* the tyet halves the drain on them, and makes them the target of everything. Do they keep it on?

**Rounds 3–4: the radiation question.** If the device comes out, the fight changes. In the cone he cannot regenerate, takes 1D3 a round, and proto-shoggoths dissolve. He flows away fast: up the walls, over the ceiling, behind the cone. The device-holder needs a DEX roll each round to keep him in it and cannot dodge or do anything else. *Radiation is not selective:* anyone in the cone for more than 3 rounds in a row loses 1 CON per further round (temporary: nausea, a metal taste).

**Rounds 5–6: he adapts.**

- **If radiation is the main threat:** he splits. A piece of himself (8 HP, attacks like a proto-shoggoth at double damage) circles behind to go after the device-holder, while the main body retreats.
- **If guns and acid are the main threat:** he backs off and uses drain tendrils only (20-foot reach), like a sniper.
- **If the binding is being prepared:** everything at the speaker.

**Golden rule:** every round, at least one investigator should face a choice beyond "I attack." If a round passes with nothing but attack rolls, add a complication: a proto-shoggoth from a vent, the container knocked from someone's hands, the device flickering (Luck roll or it fails for a round), the ceiling coming down between the party and the creature.

---

### The binding

**The five steps:**

1. **Subdue him:** reduce him to 0 HP, or pin him.
2. **The container:** a vessel holding fragments of the original jar, open, within 5 feet of him (DEX roll to place it under pressure).
3. **The honey seal:** applied once his mass begins to enter the container (DEX roll).
4. **The tyet** laid so it touches both the container and the seal (no roll, but the wearer must take it off).
5. **The prayer:** 3 rounds (4 with the appeal to the consumed), 15 Magic Points total, split among the speakers.

**Pinning him.** At least three investigators must each succeed at Fighting (Brawl) in the same round. Each takes a penalty die unless he is below 10 HP or honey has frozen part of him. A "honey mop" (the first group's plan) works well: every honey application on him immobilizes part of his body for 1D3 rounds. Honey is finite, though: one bucket is about four good applications, and the seal needs one.

**The speakers.** Each speaker must be within 10 feet of the container and do nothing but speak for the whole round: no dodging, no fighting. A speaker who dies mid-prayer still counts (the Hartley letters).

**Belief.** At the start of the first prayer round, each speaker makes a POW roll:

- **Regular difficulty** if they have Occult 20% or more, a genuine faith, the belief shortcut from the Hartley letters, or if they have *heard Bronson's voice for themselves tonight.* (Blanche almost certainly qualifies, and her Occult 60 qualifies her anyway. The fraud is the believer now.)
- **Hard difficulty** otherwise.
- **On a failure** the speaker's Magic Points count for half, rounded down. The prayer can still finish if others make up the difference.

**The escape roll.** Each round of the prayer, the Keeper rolls d100 against his escape chance. Start at **60%** and adjust:

- **−20** if the tyet is properly placed (without it, no binding is possible at all).
- **−15** if the honey seal is strong (DEX roll succeeded, and enough honey left).
- **−15** if he is at 0 HP or pinned by three or more investigators.
- **−10** if a salt-and-iron circle (Voss's method) surrounds him.
- **Halve the result** if the prayer includes the appeal to the consumed.

A well-prepared party faces about 10% a round (5% with the appeal). A desperate one faces 40% or worse. If the roll succeeds, he partly tears free: the speakers make a POW roll to keep going, and on a failure they must start the prayer again. **Two escapes in a row** and he is loose.

**The consumed ka (round 2 of the prayer).** If the party did *not* use the appeal, the souls inside him feel the goddess reaching for them, and they try to get out *into the nearest living person*.

- Everyone within 30 feet makes a POW roll.
- **Success:** a rush of other lives (incense, river light, a child laughing) and the understanding that these people are about to be sealed in again. 0/1 Sanity.
- **Failure:** a ka tries to climb into them. 1/1D6 Sanity, and a second POW roll to push it out. If that fails too, it nests. For 1D4 weeks they lose 1D3 Sanity a week, dream in Egyptian, and feel a hunger that is not theirs; they keep +5 Occult afterward.
- **If Blanche fails:** the ka that nests is *Bronson*. She is with them for a few weeks. She is kind about it. Then she fades.

**Using the appeal** adds a round and an extra escape roll, and prevents this event entirely. The souls go to rest instead of back into the jar. **Bronson goes free.** This is the emotional choice at the end of the adventure: three rounds and done, or four rounds and she is released.

---

### Aftermath

**If the binding succeeds.** His body contracts violently into the container, like water down a drain. Ears pop. The honey hardens into something like amber, and the tyet glows deep blue for a moment, then goes dark. The container is warm and hums faintly with something compressed and furious.

If the appeal was used: just before the end, a dozen voices go quiet one by one, and the last is Bronson's.

> "Thank you. Tell Owen it wasn't the tape. It was always going to be someone. Tell Margaret I was wrong about the Middle Kingdom chronology and she can stop gloating."

**Who keeps the container?** There is no right answer. This is a seed.

- **Pemberton and Brown:** academic custody. She will guard it with her life.
- **The British Museum:** the most experience, and they lost it once already. Owen's rival Fanshawe wants it crated and shipped home by Monday.
- **Merriwether:** the party's patron, a sick man with deep pockets, who once opened a door he couldn't close. (Keeper: if *The Paper Chase* went badly for him, this is a loaded choice.)
- **Voss** will write offering to buy it within a month.
- **Keep it:** the most dangerous option. He is aware of who holds him.

**Loose ends:**

- **Drummond** (at **Working** or better): a long silence, then: "Dr. Bronson was killed by an unknown chemical agent released in the robbery. The other deaths were structural collapse. The perpetrators are the Walsh gang. Case closed." He looks at the container. "I don't want to know what's in that. I want you out of my city by tonight." At **Resentful** or worse, he claims the container as evidence. That is a bureaucratic nightmare and a future adventure, if someone at the precinct opens it.
- **Sullivan:** exposed and ruined, or still in office and now the party's enemy, or dead in a ditch by Walsh's hand.
- **Walsh:** wants his stolen artifacts back, wants the immunity he was promised, and has a long memory.
- **Thornwood:** prison, protection, or the next train to anywhere, depending on what the party promised and whether they keep it.
- **Mary Mazars:** has the story of her career, and a choice the party may influence about how much of it to print.
- **Owen:** Fanshawe's cable recalls him to London to answer for the loss. He can confess about Southampton or not. Either way, the party's word, or Pemberton's, will decide whether he still has a career.
- **Pemberton** takes them aside: "If you find other things like this, sealed places and old warnings, write to me at Radcliffe. I may not be able to help. But I'll know someone who can." She is a standing contact.

**If the binding fails.** The creature is free and the party is spent. Fallbacks:

- **Destruction:** reduce him to −29 HP under radiation. It is a war of attrition they will probably lose.
- **The scarab** (if they have it): opposed POW against 100. On a success, one command: "Go back into the vessel and sleep." On a failure, the holder is drained on the spot.
- **Retreat** to the safe zone. He will not follow. When they come back, he will be whole, and he will have planned for them.
- **He leaves the hotel.** The adventure becomes a campaign across Providence.

## THE CREATURE

### What it hunts

The creature's attacks follow rules the players can learn. Run them consistently, and let the players work them out. Then they can bait it, avoid it, and plan around it.

1. **The tyet.** It wants the tyet destroyed above everything. Before Hotel Clock hour 5 it is too weak to face it, and it *avoids* whoever wears it. (The wearer notices: the cold slides off them.) From hour 5 it hunts the tyet directly.
2. **The scarab.** Its old seat of power. It follows the scarab anywhere, which is why Federal Hill happens.
3. **The jar fragments.** They pull at it, and it hates them. It goes after whoever carries them to scatter them, never to touch them.
4. **The marked.** Anyone who handled the residue, or was touched by it, smells of it for the rest of the night. It can find them anywhere in the building, and eventually anywhere in the city.
5. **Food.** Between hours 2 and 6 it feeds on anyone alone in a quiet place: the linen room, the stairwell, a duct.

**What it avoids:**

- **Radiation.** It will not enter the geology convention's wing.
- **Honey.** A honey-coated threshold stops it.
- **The shabti.** It does not avoid the shabti; it simply cannot perceive whoever carries one.

### The Hotel Clock

Hour 0 is the heist, about 9:30 pm.

- **Hours 0–1 (9:30–11:30 pm).** It hides in the shaft, weak and disoriented. Cold drafts, groaning pipes. No attacks unless someone approaches the lair.
- **Hours 2–3 (11:30 pm–1:30 am).** The voice begins (Scene 8). It can spawn one proto-shoggoth at a time and hunts the marked and the fragment-carriers. The first victim the party knows.
- **Hours 4–5 (1:30–3:30 am).** Two or three proto-shoggoths are loose in the ducts. Lights flicker and some elevators stop. The Federal Hill massacre happens (hour 4). City Hall turns (Scene 9). From hour 5 it hunts the tyet.
- **Hours 6–7 (3:30–5:30 am).** It is near full strength and leaves the shaft; guests report "something" in the corridors. Fox Iona arrives with the scarab if the party skipped Federal Hill. A second named victim if the party is slow.
- **Hours 8–9 (5:30–6:30 am).** Full manifestation at dawn. The power dies, the elevator falls, and the Mayor orders the hotel evacuated. The finale.

**If the party is fast,** they can go down to the shaft early. Before hour 5 he is beatable but has not yet fully formed. Allow it; a bold, early, well-prepared strike should be rewarded. Use his full stats with HP 15 and only one spawn.

### Proto-shoggoth (spawned)

- **Characteristics:** STR 65, CON 80, SIZ 35, DEX 60, INT 30, POW 40.
- **HP** 12. **Damage bonus** +1D4. **Build** 1. **Move** 8 (flowing).
- **Armor:** none. Regenerates 2 HP a round unless in radiation, or where acid has landed.
- **Attacks:** 2 per round. Pseudopod 45%, 1D6 + damage bonus, may grapple.
- **Drain** (while grappling): the victim loses 1D6 CON a round (temporary), and it heals that many HP.
- **Torbernite:** dissolves in 1D6 rounds within 5 feet of a sample; in 1 round in the device's cone.
- **Sanity:** 0/1D6.

### Ibnhotep (full form)

- **Characteristics:** STR 150, CON 200, SIZ 90, DEX 70, INT 80, POW 100.
- **HP** 29. **Armor** 5. Regenerates 5 HP a round unless in radiation, or where acid has landed.
- **Damage bonus** +2D6. **Build** 3. **Move** 10 (flowing, climbing, through any gap).
- **Sanity:** 1D6/1D20.
- **Damage note:** rules as written, STR 150 plus SIZ 90 would give +3D6 and Build 4. The scenario uses +2D6 and Build 3 on purpose. Even so, a lash averages 14, which drops most investigators in one hit. For a small party, use 1D6 + 2D6.

**Attacks:**

- **Pseudopod lash:** 60%, 2D6 + 2D6, reach 15 feet, up to 3 a round at different targets.
- **Engulf:** 40%, within 5 feet. The victim is pulled inside and drained 3D6 CON a round. Each round the victim gets a STR roll (Extreme) to escape. Anyone who deals 10+ damage in one hit forces him to spit the victim out. The victim can still act inside him, and attacks from inside ignore his armor.
- **Drain tendril:** 70%, reach 20 feet, 1D6 CON drain and no physical damage. Spot Hidden (Hard) to see it coming. The target gets a flash of his hunger: 0/1 Sanity.
- **Spawn:** costs 5 HP, and the proto-shoggoth acts the round after.
- **Psychic assault:** a free action once a round, range 30 feet, opposed POW. On a loss the target is stunned for their next action and loses 1/1D4 Sanity, from living one of his victims' deaths. Anyone who has seen him before (photograph, earlier encounter) gets a bonus die to resist.

**Weaknesses:**

- **Torbernite and radiation:** no regeneration, normal weapons work fully, and he tries to flee the field.
- **Acid:** 1D6 per application; no regeneration where it lands.
- **Honey:** freezes part of him for 1D3 rounds; impassable as a barrier.
- **Fire:** normal damage, but he regenerates it normally.
- **The tyet:** halves his drain on the wearer. It is the irreplaceable key to the binding.
- **The shabti:** he cannot perceive the carrier.
- **The scarab:** one command, at a terrible risk.

### Quick-reference combat card

- **IBNHOTEP:** HP 29, Armor 5, regenerates 5 a round (stopped by radiation or acid).
  - Lash 60%, 2D6+2D6, 15 ft, 3 a round.
  - Engulf 40%, 5 ft, 3D6 CON a round.
  - Tendril 70%, 20 ft, 1D6 CON.
  - Psychic assault: opposed POW, 30 ft, free action, stun plus 1/1D4 Sanity.
  - Spawn: 5 HP.
- **PROTO-SHOGGOTH:** HP 12, no armor, regenerates 2 a round.
  - Pseudopod 45%, 1D6+1D4, 2 a round.
  - Drain while grappling: 1D6 CON a round.
  - Torbernite kills it in 1D6 rounds (1 round in the device cone).
- **TORBERNITE:** device = 15-foot cone; samples = 5-foot radius.
  - No regeneration. 1D3 a round to the creature from the device.
  - Humans lose 1 CON a round after 3 rounds in a row in the field.
- **HONEY:** freezes part of him for 1D3 rounds; a coated threshold is a barrier; one application is needed for the seal.
- **BINDING:** subdue, container (DEX), honey seal (DEX), tyet, then prayer (3 rounds, or 4 with the appeal).
  - 15 MP split among speakers; POW roll for belief.
  - Escape roll each round: 60% base, −20 tyet, −15 seal, −15 subdued, −10 salt and iron, halved with the appeal. Two escapes in a row and he is free.

### Sanity summary

- Hearing the whisper at the meet-and-greet: 0/1.
- The heist (seeing it take Bronson): 0/1D4.
- Bronson's body up close: 0/1D3.
- The creature photograph: 0/1. The death photograph: 1/1D4.
- The séance: 1/1D4 (1/1D6 for Blanche). A professional skeptic finding no trick: 0/1D3.
- Friar found drained: 0/1D4. Federal Hill: 1/1D4.
- A proto-shoggoth: 0/1D6. Ibnhotep in full: 1D6/1D20.
- The consumed ka (at 0 HP, or during the binding): 0/1 to 1/1D6.
- Thornwood's confession, for Bronson's friend: 0/1D3.

Expect 8–20 points lost over the adventure. The goal is dread and grief, not madness.

## THE CAST

### The power players

The politics worked at the table. These are the people who can open or close doors, each with what they want, what they can give, and what turns them.

- **Mayor Joseph H. Gainer.**
  - *Wants:* no scandal before next year's election, and Merriwether's goodwill.
  - *Gives:* access, and orders to Drummond.
  - *Turns when:* the party's name appears in a newspaper next to the word "séance," or Ives threatens his money.
- **Councilman Harold Sullivan.** The secret enemy.
  - *Wants:* the party gone, and the whole affair blamed on the Walsh gang without his name in it.
  - *Gives:* "any help you need with the city" (meaning information he can pass to Walsh).
  - *Turns when:* exposed. Then he runs to Walsh, who decides he is a liability.
- **Detective Benjamin Drummond.**
  - *Wants:* a case he can close and a night with no more surprises.
  - *Gives:* access, findings, men, a shotgun, and silence afterward.
  - *Turns when:* a crime in front of him or a wasted lead. Steps run Hostile, Resentful, Working, Ally.
- **Reginald Ashworth III** (police commission).
  - *Wants:* deference, and Providence's dignity.
  - *Gives:* pressure on Drummond or the Mayor, in either direction.
  - *Turns when:* anything public and vulgar.
- **Harrison Ives** (mills).
  - *Wants:* quiet, and an end to the union troubles.
  - *Gives:* money and lawyers.
  - *Turns when:* the story goes public, or he recognizes a union organizer among the Mayor's guests. Then he leans on the Mayor and may hire private detectives to follow the party.
- **Judge Philip Cronin** (federal bench).
  - *Wants:* procedure.
  - *Gives:* warrants, and his suspicion of City Hall.
  - *Turns when:* the party breaks the law in a way he hears about.
- **Paul Mazars** (society column).
  - *Wants:* the story.
  - *Gives:* everyone's background, and the late edition.
  - *Turns when:* Mary is hurt. If she is saved, he becomes the party's champion in print.
- **Rupert Merriwether** (absent).
  - *Wants:* his emissaries to do well by Providence.
  - *Gives:* one telegram to the Mayor that settles things.
  - *Turns when:* never. He cannot be argued with from a sickbed two states away.

### Allies and witnesses

- **Dr. Caitlin Bronson.** The curator. Warm, brisk, funny, frightened. Dead at about 9:30 pm; her voice after that.
- **Owen Hartley.** Bronson's aide; pregen (see *The Investigators*).
- **Mary Mazars** (24, *Journal* photographer). Ambitious, underestimated, an outsider in her own trade. The party's most natural ally. She has the film. Wants the story, and will hold it if given a good reason.
- **Dr. Margaret Pemberton** (Radcliffe). Bronson's friend. Her grandmother's family were temple guardians, and she knows the old binding. She will not be condescended to. She can perform the binding herself if no investigator can, but she must be convinced she can do it (Psychology). If the party stalls, she tries it alone and dies.
- **Professor Ambrose Worthington** (Brown). Elderly Egyptologist, delighted to help, lectures. A backup translator. Can identify Voss.
- **Dr. Harold Rikards** (Yale geology). Materialist, socially awkward, brilliant. Torbernite and the radiation device. Will insist it is all science, even after he has seen it.
- **Thomas Yee** (Rikards's grad student). Starstruck by performers. Makes Science rolls for the party.
- **Dr. Marcus Webb** (coroner). Frightened, honest, and will help write the official lie.
- **Edmund Blackwood** (banker). Literal-minded; saw the shadow go into the grate and can point to the exact one.
- **Agnes Blackwood** (heiress). Egypt-obsessed; recognized the tyet. Will want to come along to the séance and the finale, and is a liability in a fight.
- **Prudence Tillinghast** (72). Blanche's would-be client. An excellent observer under the affectations. Saw Iona's face. Cannot keep a secret.
- **Gerald** (waiter, 30). Wrong shoes; staff passages; kitchen honey. Cautious about his job.
- **Jeanette** (waitress, 22). Bronson touched her necklace all evening. Kind, nervous. The default first victim.
- **Officers Hannigan and Nilsen.** Hannigan is by the book and will follow into danger if ordered. Nilsen wants to make detective and takes risks.
- **Rotary, Masons, council members.** Corroborating witnesses. Thomas Greene of the Rotary tackled a gunman and got pistol-whipped, and wants justice.

### The crew

- **Danny Walsh.** Rackets, bootlegging, strike-breaking. Middleman for Voss's money via Sullivan. Frightened of the creature, furious about the botch, and dangerous to anyone who knows too much, including Sullivan.
- **Fox Iona.** A Boston professional, cool, practical. Led the job, took the scarab, and shot at the creature and watched the bullets pass through. Will trade for protection.
- **Eddie Mars.** Muscle. Touched the jar, and something tried to get into him. Drunk and broken. Kind words or threats both work.
- **Gilbert Angell.** Muscle. Knows Walsh ran the job and little else.
- **Gil Friar.** Muscle. Sent to room 808 for the translations (Scene 7).
- **Caroline Thornwood.** The inside woman. Fall River family come down in the world, fired from a law firm, recruited by Walsh. Possibly holding the tyet (Thread B). Wants to survive the night, and is ashamed in a way she cannot say.
- **Dr. Heinrich Voss** (offstage, New York). Egyptologist; wrote a 1911 monograph on Ibnhotep. Wanted the jar sealed. Wants it still.
- **Dr. Lionel Fanshawe** (offstage, London). Deputy Keeper and Owen's rival. Cables orders all night.

### Handout 5: The binding prayer

## IF THINGS GO WRONG

- **The party never gets the tyet.** If Thornwood had it and they never broke her, Drummond's matron finds it in her bandage at the precinct at hour 6 and Drummond brings it, or sends Nilsen with it. Nilsen might not arrive.
- **Nobody can read the prayer.** Pemberton speaks it. She must be in the room, and she becomes the creature's first target.
- **A total party kill early.** The creature is gorged and slow. New investigators: Mary Mazars, Drummond, Owen, Fox Iona, with the dead investigators' notes.
- **The party goes all-in with Walsh.** It works, technically. His men die horribly as distractions. Drummond may arrest the investigators afterward, and Walsh will want paying.
- **The creature escapes the hotel.** The adventure becomes a Providence campaign: multiple lairs, more voices, and Voss arriving from New York with an offer.
