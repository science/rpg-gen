## Structural Philosophy {#structural-philosophy}

The adventure should function as a **hub-and-spoke investigation** with an **escalating threat**. The investigators have multiple paths to gather resources (knowledge, items, allies), and the monster's activity creates pressure and signals when they're making progress. The final confrontation's difficulty scales inversely with their preparation.

---

## ACT 1: THE HEIST {#act-1:-the-heist}

### Pre-Heist Setup {#pre-heist-setup}

The cocktail party serves as social introduction:

- Investigators arrive as Merriwether's guests, immediately given deference by the Mayor and city council members present  
- Detective Drummond is not yet there   
- Photographer (Mary Mazars) is down the hall at the Sterling-Holmes wedding; she stops by briefly because her father, the society columnist (Paul Mazars), is covering the exhibition opening  
- Investigators naturally encounter key NPCs: Fox Iona (casing the job), Margaret Pemberton (talking to Bronson), Reese (looking for marks), the journalist/photographer pair

This establishes relationships that pay off later—particularly the photographer, who becomes a trusted contact rather than a stranger they need to charm.

### The Heist Sequence (Decision-Based) {#the-heist-sequence-(decision-based)}

Replace the rigid 5-round structure with **decision phases**. At each phase, the situation changes and investigators must choose what to do. Their choices create consequences tracked for the rest of the adventure.

**PHASE 1: The Robbery**

- Fox Iona: "This is a robbery\! Stay calm, and you won't be hurt."  
- **Decision point:** What do you do in the first seconds?  
  - Move toward the jar/Bronson  
  - Take cover and observe  
  - Move toward the gunfire/gangsters  
  - Protect a specific NPC  
  - Grab the nearest artifact

**PHASE 2: The Theft**

- Fox Iona breaks the glass on the central jewelry case and grabs loot.  
- Criminals start breaking glass around the room grabbing artifacts.  
- Eddie Mars breaks open the Ibnhotep jar case and Caitlin Bronson says to him "No not this one please\! You shouldn't take this one."  
- Eddie Mars laughs and says "Lady I don't think you understand. This is a robbery, we're taking it all\!"  
- As he grabs at the jar, it slips from his hands and falls towards Caitlin Bronson  
  - Players within 30 feet of Mars, make Occult or Spot Hidden checks  
  - Best check (if successful) from group notices that the jar seemed to move before Mars touched it

**PHASE 2a: The Consequences**

- As the jar hits the ground, it explodes into pieces  
  - If a successful check from before, the jar exploded *before* it hit the ground  
- As the jar hits the ground, all the lights go out.  
- **Decision point in the dark:** React to what you saw  
  - Rush one of the criminals (opposed roll; success \= you reach him)  
  - Shout a warning to Bronson  
  - Grab for a specific display item (scarab, shabti)

**PHASE 3: The Release**

- The jar contents are spilled. A horrible moan rises.  
- Shooting starts, illuminating the scene briefly showing chaos.   
- The scene is a mass of confusion and people are starting to run and panic.  
- **Decision point:**  
  - Go toward the sound (reach Bronson's location to help her)  
  - Get light (find/create illumination)  
  - Pursue fleeing gangsters  
  - Help panicked bystanders toward exits

**PHASE 4: The Death**

- A camera flash goes off. This is a more extended illumination  
- Spot hidden or Occult check: You can see a strange haze floating around the broken jar  
- Caitlin Bronson screams.   
- More gunshots illuminate the scene briefly.  
- Anyone who succeeded on Spot hidden / Occult in Phase 3 must make a sanity check during Phase 4\. On failure: lose 1d3 Sanity AND see Ibnhotep draining life force from Dr. Bronson and then floating into dust up towards the ceiling  
- **Decision point:**  
  - Go to Bronson (she's dying but if you reach her, she gasps one clue: "The seal... the knot... Isis...")  
  - Secure other items (jar pieces, tablet on floor, scarab if still on display)  
  - Attack the criminals

**PHASE 5: The Escape**

- Walsh and his male criminal accomplices escape unless the party really tries to stop them.  
- The lights come back on.  
- Caroline Thornwood has a bullet wound in her leg and is in need of medical care. If the party helps, this might be a major lead. (The wound turns out to be glass shrapnel and not a bullet, if the party helps her.)  
- The mayor begins to call for order, and tells someone to call the police (waiter)  
  - He says "Nobody leaves this room," and orders The Masons group to block the doorway.  
- Dr Bronson is now dead and shriveled, her body is dessicated.  
- The party can discuss what happened. If the idea of a floating dust monster is covered (likely) everyone can make a mechanical repair check.

### Heist Consequences Tracker {#heist-consequences-tracker}

| Action | Consequence |
| :---- | :---- |
| Captured a gangster | Fast-track to Walsh connection; gangster reveals Caroline Thornwood's role. Caroline can reveal Walsh's involvement. |
| Grabbed the scarab amulet | Powerful weapon, but creature actively hunts whoever has it |
| Grabbed the shabti figurine | Invisibility to creature (major advantage) |
| Reached dying Bronson | Dying clue about binding method ("the knot of Isis") |
| Grabbed the journalist's camera | Control the photo development; don't need permission |
| Grabbed jar fragments | Have a container already blessed by ancient rites (advantage in binding) |
| Helped bystanders | Grateful witnesses provide information freely later |
| Attacked the creature | It recognizes you as a specific threat; targeted attacks later |

## ACT 2: THE INVESTIGATION {#act-2:-the-investigation}

### The Detective Problem (Solved) {#the-detective-problem-(solved)}

First, two uniformed officers arrive—Officer Hannigan and Officer Nilsen—to secure the scene and take preliminary statements. Approximately thirty minutes later, Detective Benjamin Drummond from homicide enters. He's irritated at being pulled from a card game, more irritated at the circus of wealthy witnesses demanding attention, and deeply suspicious of the inexplicable corpse.

The Mayor, embarrassed that "Merriwether's special guests" experienced this horror, pressures Drummond to accept their help. This happens publicly, which Drummond resents. He doesn't have to like the investigators, but he can't refuse without creating a political problem. He can:

- Grant access to crime scenes (hotel cooperation follows his lead)  
- Share official findings (autopsy results, witness statements)  
- Provide police resources within reason (patrol backup, warrant requests)  
- Make introductions that open doors (coroner, precinct records)  
- Be lost as an ally if investigators do something illegal or stupid *in front of him*

The investigators don't need to charm him—they need to not alienate him. Every time they involve him in something that makes his job easier (a solid lead, evidence that holds up, a confession), his disposition improves. Every time they make him look bad or waste his time, it deteriorates.

**Drummond's Breaking Point:** If the investigators are caught breaking and entering, assault a witness, or otherwise commit a crime he cannot ignore, Drummond must arrest them or lose his badge. He'll give them one warning first: "I don't know what you think you're doing, but if I see it again, I won't have a choice."

### Investigation Hub Structure {#investigation-hub-structure}

The investigators can pursue these nodes in any order. Some unlock others; some provide redundant paths to the same information (so no single failure blocks progress). Each node includes:

- What's there  
- What skill checks reveal information  
- What items can be obtained  
- How clues connect to other nodes  
- What dangers exist

#### NODE 1: Crime Scene (17th Floor Ballroom) {#node-1:-crime-scene-(17th-floor-ballroom)}

**Available:** Immediately after heist, with Drummond's permission (automatic if deputized)

**The Scene:** The ballroom is in chaos. Shattered display cases scatter glass across the marble floor. The central case—which held the Ibnhotep jar—is destroyed, with fragments spread in a roughly twelve-foot radius. Several artifacts are missing (taken by the gangsters), but many smaller pieces remain: damaged shabti figures, scattered beads, torn papyrus scraps.

Dr. Bronson's body lies near the shattered jar case. Her corpse is deeply wrong—desiccated like a mummy, skin drawn tight against the skull, eye sockets hollow. There is no blood. The guests have been moved to the lobby, but their abandoned drinks and dropped belongings remain.

**Contains:**

| Item | Location | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Jar fragments | Scattered near central case | Container component for binding; some pieces retain faint warmth |
| Hieroglyphic tablet | Under Bronson's body (she fell on it) | Translation source; requires Archaeology or finding a translator |
| Scarab amulet | Secondary display case, if not grabbed | Creature desperately wants this; holding it makes you a target |
| Shabti figurine | Knocked under a chair | Provides invisibility to creature when held; creature cannot perceive holder |
| Bronson's notes | Fallen clipboard near her body | Partial translations, cross-references to her room's complete files |
| Bullet casings | Scattered near east entrance | .38 caliber; match Walsh gang's preferred weapons |
| Strange residue | Trail from jar fragments to vent | Wet, slightly phosphorescent; fades if not collected within an hour |

**Skill Checks:**

| Skill | DC | Result |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Spot Hidden | Regular | Notice the vent grate is askew; something pushed through from inside |
| Spot Hidden (Hard) | Hard | Find the shabti under the chair |
| Track | Regular | Follow the wet residue trail from the jar to the vent |
| Archaeology | Regular | Recognize the tablet describes a containment ritual; partial translation |
| Archaeology (Hard) | Hard | Full translation of tablet (Handout Providence 1\) |
| Natural World | Regular | The residue is organic, similar to mucus or protoplasm |
| Medicine | Regular | Bronson's desiccation was instantaneous; she died in seconds |
| Mechanical Repair | Regular | The vent grate was forced from inside; whatever went through was roughly person-sized but malleable |

**Connections:**

- Tablet translation → Reveals binding method (unlocks ritual knowledge)  
- Residue trail → Points to vent system (leads to NODE 7\)  
- Jar fragments → If collected, provides blessed container component  
- Bronson's notes → Reference her room for complete files (leads to NODE 4\)

**Danger:** Low. The creature is hiding and regenerating. It will not attack here unless investigators remain for more than three hours, at which point it sends a proto-shoggoth through the vents to test their defenses.

#### NODE 2: Witness Interviews {#node-2:-witness-interviews}

**Available:** Immediately (witnesses are being held in the lobby per the Mayor's order)

The lobby of the Biltmore is now an impromptu detention center. Wealthy guests cluster in uncomfortable groups, demanding to be released. The Mayor is trying to calm them while Drummond takes statements. The investigators can interview witnesses freely if deputized; otherwise, they must do so subtly while Drummond is occupied elsewhere.

*See the separate INTERVIEWS section for complete NPC information.*

**Key Witnesses for Main Plot:**

| Witness | What They Know | How to Get It |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Caroline Thornwood | Walsh connection, gang structure, that the jar was specifically targeted | Psychology reveals she's lying; Intimidate or offering protection breaks her |
| Margaret Pemberton | Translation capability, Ibnhotep legend, significance of the scarab | Willing to share freely if approached respectfully as a colleague |
| Edmund Blackwood | Saw creature enter vents, direction of travel | Persuade or simply ask; he's rattled and wants to talk |
| Mary Mazars | Has photographs of the scene, including the creature | Already friendly if pre-heist connection established; otherwise needs Persuade |
| Paul Mazars | Knows social connections between guests, noticed Thornwood talking to gangsters before the heist | Charm or offer exclusive story |

**Connections:**

- Caroline Thornwood → Walsh connection (unlocks NODE 8\)  
- Margaret Pemberton → Translation capability (can translate tablet, unlocks ritual knowledge)  
- Edmund Blackwood → Vent direction (helps map creature's movement)  
- Mary Mazars → Photos (leads to NODE 3\)  
- Waiters (Gerald/Jeanette) → Noticed gangsters casing earlier, know staff areas (helps with NODE 7\)

**Danger:** None, unless investigators intimidate witnesses in front of Drummond or the Mayor.

#### NODE 3: The Darkroom {#node-3:-the-darkroom}

**Available:**

- Immediately if investigators grabbed the camera during the heist  
- Immediately if investigators have the photographer connection from pre-heist social interaction  
- Requires Persuade (Regular) if approaching Mary Mazars cold  
- Requires Stealth and Locksmith if breaking into the newspaper's darkroom

**The Location:** Mary Mazars uses a small darkroom in the Providence Journal building on Fountain Street, about a fifteen-minute walk from the Biltmore. The room is cramped, chemical-smelling, and cluttered with drying prints from various assignments. She's willing to develop the heist photos if asked, but the process takes approximately one hour.

**Contains:**

| Item | Notes |
| :---- | :---- |
| Developed photo: The creature | Shows the proto-shoggoth form during the flash; amorphous, translucent, with visible internal structures. Looking at it costs 0/1 Sanity but provides "hardening"—future encounters with the creature have reduced Sanity costs |
| Developed photo: Eddie Mars with jar | Shows him grabbing for it, proves he was the one who broke it |
| Developed photo: Crowd panic | Caroline Thornwood visible near Danny Walsh, suggesting association |
| Developed photo: Bronson's death | Partially obscured but shows the draining process; 1/1d4 Sanity, but reveals the creature's feeding method |
| Developing chemicals | Could be used as improvised weapons (silver nitrate causes burns) |

**Skill Checks:**

| Skill | DC | Result |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Photography or Art | Regular | Enhance details in photos, revealing more information |
| Cthulhu Mythos | Regular | Recognize creature as proto-shoggoth or shoggoth variant |
| Spot Hidden | Regular | Notice Caroline Thornwood's position near Walsh in crowd photo |
| Law | Regular | Realize the photos are evidence; copies should be made |

**The Attack:** Approximately forty-five minutes into the development process, the creature attacks. It has tracked the investigators (drawn by residue from the crime scene on their clothes, or by the scarab if anyone carries it). A proto-shoggoth emerges from the building's ventilation.

**Combat:** One proto-shoggoth (stats below). The darkroom is cramped (fighting penalty) but the chemicals provide improvised weapons. Mary Mazars will flee if possible; if not, she hides and screams.

**After the Attack:**

- Mary is terrified but grateful if saved  
- She becomes a firm ally, willing to help further  
- The attack confirms the creature is intelligent and aware of the investigation  
- Drummond must be told about the attack; his opinion of the investigators improves if they protected a civilian

**Connections:**

- Photo of creature → Confirms nature (Mythos knowledge)  
- Photo of Thornwood/Walsh → Evidence of her criminal association (leverage for NODE 2\)  
- Attack itself → Proves creature is active, intelligent, and tracking them

**Danger:** HIGH. This is likely the first real combat encounter. The attack is triggered by time spent here, making it predictable for the Keeper but surprising for players.

#### NODE 4: Dr. Bronson's Room (\#808) {#node-4:-dr.-bronson's-room-(#808)}

**Available:**

- Deputized investigators receive a key from the hotel manager  
- Others must sneak (Stealth) or pick the lock (Locksmith, Regular DC)  
- Drummond will accompany if asked, but his presence limits what investigators can pocket

**The Room:** Dr. Bronson's room is neat, academic, and impersonal. A traveling trunk sits open at the foot of the bed, half-unpacked. Her desk is covered with papers, translations, and correspondence. A small jewelry box sits in the desk's top drawer. The window overlooks an airshaft connected to the hotel's ventilation system.

**Contains:**

| Item | Location | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Tyet necklace** | Desk drawer, jewelry box | REQUIRED for binding ritual; ancient, blue faience, warm to touch |
| Personal diary | Nightstand | Her research, worries about the jar's warnings, references to the British Museum's restricted archive |
| Translations folder | Desk | Complete translations of all artifacts, including binding ritual (Handout Providence 1\) |
| Correspondence | Desk | Letters from British Museum warning about "the Ibnhotep collection's documented dangers" |
| Traveling trunk | Foot of bed | Could serve as improvised container for binding |
| Hat pin, straight razor | Vanity table | Improvised weapons |
| Photograph of Bronson with Egyptian dig team | Desk | One figure circled: "M. Pemberton \- knows the old stories" |

**Skill Checks:**

| Skill | DC | Result |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Spot Hidden | Regular | Find the jewelry box (tyet necklace) |
| Library Use | Regular | Organize papers, find the translation folder quickly |
| Archaeology | Regular | Recognize the tyet's significance (knot of Isis, symbol of protection) |
| Psychology | Regular | Reading the diary reveals Bronson was afraid, not just academic—she believed the warnings |
| Occult | Hard | The tyet is genuine ancient artifact with actual power; it feels *alive* |

**The Diary Entries (Key Excerpts):**

*March 15th:* "The British Museum's restricted archive confirms my fears. The Ibnhotep jar is not a funerary vessel—it is a prison. The priest trapped something inside, something he could not destroy. The museum refused to send the full text of the binding ritual, but Margaret's grandmother's stories align with the fragments I have. I must be careful."

*March 22nd:* "I should not have brought the collection to America. But the exhibition was arranged, the tickets sold. Perhaps I am being foolish. The jar has remained sealed for three thousand years. What could disturb it now?"

*March 28th (day of the heist):* "I dreamed of drowning in sand. Of eyes opening in the dark. I will speak to Margaret tonight—she knows the old rituals better than any academic. If something goes wrong, she will know what to do."

**Connections:**

- Tyet necklace → Required for binding ritual  
- Translations → Complete ritual knowledge  
- Diary → Points to Margaret Pemberton's knowledge (NODE 2\)  
- Correspondence → British Museum as backup research source (NODE 9 variant)

**Danger:** HIGH. The creature considers this room a threat—it contains information about binding it. A proto-shoggoth attack occurs if investigators spend more than thirty minutes here, or immediately if they find the tyet and speak aloud about its significance.

#### NODE 5: Coroner's Office {#node-5:-coroner's-office}

**Available:**

- With Drummond's introduction (automatic if deputized)  
- Sneaking into Rhode Island Hospital morgue (Stealth, Hard DC; multiple guards)  
- The coroner, Dr. Marcus Webb, is a methodical man who respects professional curiosity

**The Location:** The Rhode Island Hospital morgue is in the basement, cold and clinical. Dr. Webb has been examining Bronson's body for three hours and is increasingly disturbed. He has no explanation for what he's seeing and is receptive to unconventional theories—though he won't admit this to Drummond.

**Contains:**

| Item | Location | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Bronson's body | Examination table | Desiccated husk; weighs approximately forty pounds |
| The tablet | Evidence locker | Was clutched in her hand; released upon death |
| Coroner's notes | Webb's desk | Detailed observations of impossible desiccation |
| Hydrochloric acid | Chemical cabinet | Medical-grade; effective against creature |
| Surgical tools | Throughout | Scalpels, bone saws—improvised weapons |
| Rubber gloves and aprons | Storage | Protection against acid, creature contact |

**Dr. Webb's Observations:** "The body shows complete moisture extraction—and I mean complete. Every cell is dehydrated. The process was instantaneous; there's no progression, no partial desiccation of outer tissues first. It's as if something simply... removed all the water at once. This isn't possible. I've seen bodies in deserts, bodies in fires. This isn't that."

If pressed: "There's something else. The cellular damage pattern suggests the water wasn't pulled out aerobically. It was... I don't know how to say this professionally. It was *extracted. Drained*. Something fed on her."

**Skill Checks:**

| Skill | DC | Result |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Medicine | Regular | Confirm Webb's analysis; the feeding was life-force, not just water |
| Medicine (Hard) | Hard | Notice trace elements in the cells that match the residue from the crime scene |
| Spot Hidden | Regular | Notice the acid bottles; Webb mentions they're used for cleaning but investigators recognize offensive potential |
| Persuade | Regular | Convince Webb to share his notes officially (helps with Drummond) |
| Psychology | Regular | Webb is frightened but professional; he suspects something supernatural but won't say it first |

**Connections:**

- Webb's observations → Confirm creature's feeding method  
- Acid bottles → Weapon acquisition  
- Tablet (if not already obtained) → Translation source  
- Webb as ally → Can provide medical supplies, testimony, safe harbor

**Danger:** Low. The creature is focused on the hotel. However, if investigators bring items from the crime scene (residue samples, jar fragments), the creature might track them here after several hours.

#### NODE 6: Geology Convention (Same Floor as Exhibition) {#node-6:-geology-convention-(same-floor-as-exhibition)}

**Available:** Immediately (just down the hall from the ballroom)

**The Location:** The geology convention occupies three smaller conference rooms on the 17th floor. Despite the chaos of the heist, several geologists remained, including Dr. Harold Rikards, who is both fascinated and irritated by the interruption to his presentation on uranium deposits.

**Contains:**

| Item | Location | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Torbernite samples** | Display cases | Radioactive mineral (the creature's weakness) |
| Hydrochloric acid | Chemistry demonstration table | Dissolves organic matter, effective against creature |
| Bunsen burners | Same | Fire source |
| Lead-lined sample boxes | Storage | Could contain radioactive material safely |
| Geiger counter | Dr. Rikards' equipment | Detects radiation; useful for tracking if creature leaves traces |

**The Torbernite Connection:** Torbernite is a green, radioactive mineral containing uranium. The radiation disrupts the creature's ability to maintain cohesion. In the presence of torbernite:

- Creature cannot regenerate  
- Normal physical attacks deal full damage  
- Creature attempts to flee rather than fight  
- Proto-shoggoths dissolve within 1d6 rounds of exposure

## ACT 3: THE INVESTIGATION DEEPENS {#act-3:-the-investigation-deepens}

*Three Active Investigation Scenes are underway*

### SCENE 1: Brown University — John Hay Library {#scene-1:-brown-university-—-john-hay-library}

**Characters:** Till, Dr. Elgin, Gale, Margaret Pemberton (NPC guide)

**Travel and Setup:** The group takes a cab from the Biltmore to Brown's campus on College Hill, about fifteen minutes. Pemberton is quiet during the ride, staring out the window. She's grieving Bronson but channeling it into purpose. When they arrive at the John Hay Library, it's after hours, but Pemberton meets Brown University colleague, Professor Worthington.

The library is dark, stone-floored, and cold. Emergency gas lamps cast uneven light. Pemberton leads them to the third-floor reading room where the Egyptology and religious studies collections overlap with a small restricted section behind a locked cage.

**Scene Structure:** Four sequential challenges, each gating an advantage. Pemberton provides the guaranteed lore drop about the tyet at the end regardless of other outcomes, but the players need to earn everything else.

---

### Beat 1: Pemberton's Trust {#beat-1:-pemberton's-trust}

Before Pemberton opens up fully, she needs to gauge whether these people will treat the knowledge seriously. She's spent her career having her grandmother's traditions dismissed by European academics. She won't waste her time on skeptics.

**The Test:** As they settle into the reading room, Pemberton sets out Bronson's notes (she has her own copies of some correspondence) and asks, bluntly: "Before we begin — do you believe that what killed Caitlin was real? Not a gas leak, not hysteria. Something that should not exist."

**Skill Options (one character must succeed):**

| Skill | DC | Approach |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Occult | Regular | Demonstrate genuine knowledge of binding traditions, protective magic, or Egyptian metaphysics |
| Psychology | Regular | Read that Pemberton needs validation, not persuasion — affirm her experience honestly |
| Anthropology | Regular | Frame her grandmother's knowledge as legitimate oral tradition with evidentiary weight (Till's specialty) |
| Persuade | Hard | Convince her through sheer sincerity (harder because words are cheap) |

**Success:** Pemberton relaxes visibly. "Good. Then we won't waste time pretending." She opens her personal notebook — not Bronson's academic files, but her own family records, written in a mix of English and Arabic. This notebook remains available for the rest of the scene, providing a bonus die on all subsequent Library Use and Occult checks in this location.

**Failure:** Pemberton remains guarded. She'll still help, but she withholds the family notebook. She answers direct questions but doesn't volunteer information. All subsequent checks in this scene are made at normal difficulty (no bonus dice), and Beat 4's advanced lore is unavailable — Pemberton simply won't share it with people she doesn't trust.

---

### Beat 2: The Binding Ritual {#beat-2:-the-binding-ritual}

The group digs into the stacks. Pemberton directs them toward the religious studies section — comparative binding traditions across Egyptian temple practice. The goal is to reconstruct the full ritual procedure from the academic sources cross-referenced with Bronson's partial translations.

**What They're Looking For:** The mechanical steps of the binding — container preparation, sealing materials, the prayer, and the sequence.

**Primary Check — Library Use (Regular):** Any character. Locating the relevant texts among the stacks, cross-referencing Bronson's notes with the library's holdings.

**Success:** They find a 1903 monograph by Elisabeth Hartley (the British Museum curator who rebound the creature in 1879\) titled *Anomalous Containment Practices in Late Period Egyptian Temple Ritual*. It's dry, academic, and buried in footnotes, but it contains the complete binding procedure:

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Binding Ritual Knowledge:**  
> 

> 1. Reduce the creature to helplessness (unconscious or physically restrained)  
> 2. Place in a prepared vessel (clay blessed in Isis's name, or any container holding fragments of the original vessel)  
> 3. Seal with honey (symbol of preservation; prevents the hunger from escaping)  
> 4. Affix the tyet to the seal (the knot of Isis holds the binding)  
> 5. Speak the prayer to Isis (costs 15 MP total, divisible among participants; takes 3 full rounds)

The monograph also contains the prayer text in hieratic with Hartley's English translation. *Provide Handout Providence 2\.*

**Failure:** They can't locate the monograph in the stacks. Pemberton can reconstruct the ritual from memory and her family notebook (if they earned her trust in Beat 1), but without the academic source, the knowledge is incomplete — they know the steps but not the prayer text. They'll need Pemberton physically present at the binding to speak the words, making her a required participant in the final confrontation rather than an optional advantage.

**Secondary Check — Archaeology or History (Regular):** Any character. While searching, they can attempt to understand the *why* behind each ritual step.

**Success:** Understanding the logic of the binding provides tactical flexibility. They learn that the container doesn't need to be the original jar — any vessel that holds fragments of the original works because the blessing transfers. They also learn that honey isn't symbolic; it's a literal sealant that the creature cannot pass through. The creature's protoplasmic form reacts to honey the way flesh reacts to acid — it rigidifies and cannot flow.

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Honey as Tactical Weapon:** Honey can be used defensively during the fight. Splashing honey on the creature or on surfaces creates barriers it cannot cross or flow through. A honey-coated threshold blocks proto-shoggoths. Honey applied directly to the creature deals no damage but immobilizes the affected portion for 1d3 rounds.

**Failure:** They understand the ritual steps but not the underlying principles. Honey is just a sealing material to them — they won't think to use it tactically.

---

### Beat 3: The 1879 Incident {#beat-3:-the-1879-incident}

This requires accessing the restricted collection behind the cage. Pemberton has general library access but not restricted access. The restricted section holds rare and fragile texts, including Hartley's personal correspondence with the British Museum (donated to Brown in 1911).

**Access Options:**

| Approach | Skill | DC | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Find the librarian's key | Spot Hidden | Regular | Spare key in the front desk drawer |
| Pick the lock | Locksmith | Regular | Old lock, simple mechanism |
| Force the cage | STR | Hard | Noisy; damages the cage; may trigger consequences later |
| Pemberton's credentials | Persuade (Pemberton) | Regular | She can talk her way in if someone coaches her on what to say to the night watchman (requires finding the watchman first) |

**Inside the Restricted Collection:** Hartley's correspondence is in an archival box, filed under "British Museum — Anomalous Incidents." It contains letters between Hartley and the museum director describing the 1879 event.

**Primary Check — Library Use (Regular):** Locate the relevant letters among several boxes of correspondence.

**Success:** The letters describe the 1879 incident in clinical detail. The jar was opened by a cleaning woman who knocked it from a shelf. Three museum employees died before Hartley performed the binding. Key tactical intelligence:

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Tactical Precedent (The Hartley Method):**  
> 

> - Hartley used museum display cases as improvised barriers, herding the creature into a prepared space  
> - She noted that the creature was "drawn irresistibly" to the fragments of its original vessel — it *wants* to return to the jar. Placing jar fragments in a container and leaving it exposed will eventually lure the creature toward it  
> - The creature cannot perceive or attack anyone holding a shabti figure consecrated to Isis (this confirms the shabti's function if they have it, or tells them to look for one if they don't)  
> - Hartley wrote: "The binding required four of us speaking in unison. The creature fought violently during the second round of the prayer. We lost Dr. Marsh during the third round. The binding completed despite his death — the prayer requires belief, not survival."

This last point is mechanically significant: the prayer's MP cost can be paid by someone who dies during the ritual. Their contribution still counts as long as they were speaking when they fell.

**Failure:** They can't find the right box, or the letters are too fragile/damaged to read completely. They miss the tactical intelligence. The creature's attraction to jar fragments, the shabti confirmation, and the "belief not survival" principle remain unknown.

**Bonus Check — Psychology (Hard) (Elgin's specialty):** Reading Hartley's letters closely.

**Success:** Elgin recognizes the psychological pattern in Hartley's account. Hartley was terrified. She didn't believe the ritual would work. She performed it anyway because there was no alternative — and it worked because in the moment of speaking, with the creature bearing down on her, her desperate *need* for it to work became belief.

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — The Belief Shortcut:** The ritual's belief requirement doesn't demand faith in Isis or Egyptian religion. It demands genuine belief that the words have power — that *something* will answer. Terror and desperation can substitute for religious conviction. Any investigator with 40+ POW can attempt the binding without an Occult skill, making a standard POW roll rather than the POW×3 roll normally required for non-believers. This significantly opens up who can perform the ritual.

**Failure:** The psychological insight is missed. The belief requirement remains as written in the adventure — POW×3 for non-believers, or Pemberton must perform it.

---

### Beat 4: Deeper Knowledge (Requires Pemberton's Trust) {#beat-4:-deeper-knowledge-(requires-pemberton's-trust)}

*This beat is only available if the group succeeded in Beat 1\.*

Pemberton closes Hartley's letters and sits quietly for a moment. Then she opens her family notebook to a page she's clearly been debating whether to share.

"My grandmother told me something that isn't in any academic text. The temple guardians didn't just bind Ibnhotep. They understood what he became. He's not a demon or a god. He's a hunger — a human soul that ate so many others it lost its shape. The ka he consumed didn't die. They're inside him. Screaming."

**No check required for the lore.** Pemberton shares this freely:

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — The Consumed Ka:** The creature contains the life-force of every person it has ever drained. These consumed souls are in agony and would escape if they could. During the binding ritual, if the speaker addresses the consumed souls directly — calling them to rest, promising them peace — the creature's resistance is weakened. Mechanically: the creature's STR for purposes of the escape contest during the ritual is halved (from 150 to 75\) if the speaker incorporates an appeal to the consumed dead.

**Optional Check — Occult (Hard):** Any character. Studying the notebook's diagrams of the tyet's properties.

**Success:** The tyet isn't just a seal component. It's an active ward. While worn, it provides partial protection against the creature's drain attack — the drain deals half CON damage (round down) to the wearer. It also allows the wearer to sense the creature's proximity within approximately 100 feet (a feeling of cold pressure).

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Tyet as Active Ward:** Half drain damage while worn. Proximity sense (100 feet). These functions are passive and constant while the necklace is in contact with skin.

**Failure:** They understand the tyet is important for the binding but don't unlock its protective properties. It functions only as a ritual component.

---

### Guaranteed Lore Drop: The Tyet {#guaranteed-lore-drop:-the-tyet}

Regardless of all other outcomes, before the group leaves Brown, Pemberton explains the tyet necklace's role in the binding. This is non-negotiable — she watched Bronson die and she will make sure these people know at minimum what the necklace does in the ritual context.

She confirms:

- The tyet is the knot of Isis, a genuine artifact of power  
- It is the irreplaceable component of the binding — without it, the ritual cannot work  
- It must touch both the container and the honey seal simultaneously to activate the binding  
- Bronson kept it in her room specifically because she feared this scenario

This ensures the party understands the tyet's critical importance even if every skill check in the scene fails.

**Potential Interruption:** If any character brought jar fragments, residue-stained clothing, or (especially) the scarab to Brown, there's a 25% chance per hour that the creature sends a proto-shoggoth. It arrives through the library's basement vents. This forces a fight in the stacks — narrow aisles, fragile books, Pemberton screaming that the Hartley letters must be protected. The proto-shoggoth prioritizes destroying the restricted collection (the creature is intelligent and knows what's in there).

### SCENE 2: Thornwood Interrogation {#scene-2:-thornwood-interrogation}

**Characters:** Velvet (PC), Detective Drummond (NPC)

**Location:** A small office off the Biltmore lobby, commandeered by Drummond. Caroline Thornwood sits in a wooden chair, her leg bandaged (glass shrapnel, not a bullet). She's pale, shaking, and cycling between defiance and terror.

**The Core Problem:** How does cracking the mob connection yield advantages against the creature?

**The Answer:** Three paths forward from Thornwood, each yielding a different advantage:

1. **Drummond's resources** — weapons that can be used in the final fight, maybe Drummond will help  
2. **The New York buyer** — someone who specifically wanted the jar, meaning someone who *knows what Ibnhotep is* and may have counter-measures or control methods  
3. **Eddie Mars's testimony** — eyewitness intelligence about the creature's behavior in its first moments of freedom, revealing tactical weaknesses

Velvet doesn't know which path will open. The interrogation structure determines what Thornwood gives up and in what order.

### Beat 1: Breaking Thornwood {#beat-1:-breaking-thornwood}

Thornwood's default state is defensive lying. She claims she's an innocent bystander, hurt in the crossfire, doesn't know any criminals. This is transparent — Drummond already suspects her because she didn't try to leave with the other guests, and her "bullet wound" is glass shrapnel from the display cases (she was close to the action, not fleeing from it).

**Drummond's Role:** He leads the interrogation conventionally. He's competent but limited — he's working a robbery-homicide, not a monster hunt. He'll establish the criminal facts but won't ask the questions that matter for fighting the creature. Velvet needs to steer the conversation or take over.

**Phase 1 — Drummond's Opening (Automatic):** Drummond presents the facts: Thornwood was seen near the gunmen before the robbery (witness testimony from Paul Mazars and Edmund Blackwood). Her wound is shrapnel, not a bullet. She didn't flee. He gives her the choice: cooperate or face charges as an accessory to armed robbery and murder.

Thornwood denies everything. She's scared but holding.

**Phase 2 — Velvet's Opportunity:** Drummond looks at Velvet, grudgingly offering the floor. "Your turn. The Mayor seems to think you people have some special insight."

**Skill Options (Velvet chooses approach):**

| Skill | DC | Approach | Result on Success |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Psychology | Regular | Read Thornwood's fear — she's not afraid of prison, she's afraid of *Walsh* and whatever killed Bronson. Name the real fears. | Thornwood's composure cracks. She admits she was working for "someone" but won't name Walsh yet. Opens Phase 3A. |
| Intimidate | Regular | Lean on the creature angle — "You saw what killed Dr. Bronson. You saw what it did to her body. That thing is still in this building. Right now. And it's getting stronger. The only people who can stop it are in this room, and you're wasting our time." | Thornwood breaks fast. She gives up Walsh immediately but is hostile — provides information minimally. Opens Phase 3B. |
| Persuade | Hard | Offer genuine protection — not just from the law, but from Walsh and from the creature. Frame cooperation as her only path to safety. | Thornwood becomes cautiously cooperative. She trusts Velvet enough to share more than the minimum. Opens Phase 3A with bonus. |
| Charm | Hard | Connect personally — Thornwood is a desperate woman who made bad choices. Recognize that without judgment. | Same as Persuade but also establishes ongoing rapport. Thornwood becomes a willing asset, not just a squeezed informant. Opens Phase 3A with bonus. |

**Failure (any approach):** Thornwood clams up. Drummond is frustrated. The interrogation stalls. Velvet can try a different approach (one retry with a different skill, at Hard difficulty regardless of original DC), or accept the failure.

**Complete Failure (both attempts fail):** Thornwood refuses to talk. Drummond has her booked as an accessory. She's taken to the precinct. The Walsh connection isn't severed — it just has to come through another route later (following the gangsters' trail, Nicky's criminal contacts). No advantage gained from this scene. Drummond's opinion of Velvet drops: "Waste of my time."

### Phase 2 Pushed roll consequences on failures {#phase-2-pushed-roll-consequences-on-failures}

**Pushing Psychology:** Velvet leans harder into reading Thornwood, naming her fears aloud — Walsh, the creature, prison, the shame of her family finding out. On the pushed failure, Velvet reads her wrong. She identifies the wrong pressure point and says something that makes Thornwood feel understood by the wrong person for the wrong reasons.

**Pushed Failure Consequence:** Thornwood decides Velvet is working for Walsh — testing her loyalty, seeing if she'll crack. She goes completely silent, not out of defiance but out of genuine terror that she's being set up. Drummond can't break through this either; Thornwood is now convinced that talking to anyone in this room means death. She demands a lawyer.

**Mechanical result:** Thornwood is removed from the scene entirely. She's taken to the precinct for booking. The Walsh connection must come through other channels (Nicky's criminal contacts, following the gangsters' trail, or photograph evidence linking Thornwood to Walsh). Drummond blames Velvet openly: "You spooked her. She was ready to talk and you spooked her." Drummond's disposition drops one full tier. He's not hostile, but he stops including Velvet in his process — he'll share findings if asked directly but won't volunteer anything.

Why it's not catastrophic: The Walsh connection isn't lost, just delayed and harder to reach. Drummond is damaged but not lost. The deeper intelligence (buyer, Eddie Mars testimony, targeting behavior) becomes unavailable through this path but some of it can be reconstructed from other sources. 

**Pushing Intimidate:** Velvet doubles down on the creature angle. She gets graphic — describes the desiccation, Bronson's hollow eye sockets, the thing she saw in the dark. She's trying to scare Thornwood into cooperation by making prison look safe by comparison.

**Pushed Failure Consequence:** Thornwood has a panic attack. Full hyperventilation, sobbing, unable to speak. Drummond physically steps between Velvet and Thornwood: "That's enough. Step outside." He's not asking. This isn't anger — it's professional judgment. A detective who lets a witness be terrorized in his interrogation room loses credibility in court and with his precinct.

**Mechanical result:** Velvet is removed from the interrogation. Drummond continues alone and eventually gets Walsh's name through conventional pressure (takes 2 additional in-game hours). Velvet gets the basic Walsh leverage information secondhand from Drummond later, but no deep dive options — Drummond didn't know to ask those questions. Drummond's disposition drops one full tier and he adds a condition: Velvet doesn't participate in future interrogations. "You can investigate. You don't talk to my witnesses."

Why it's not catastrophic: The Walsh name still surfaces. Velvet still gets the warehouse location. But she loses access to a whole category of investigative tool (Drummond-sanctioned interviews) and the time cost pushes the escalation timeline forward. The deeper intelligence is gone through this path. 

**Pushing Persuade:** Velvet offers protection more emphatically — promises police custody, safe houses, a new start. She's making commitments she may not be able to keep, leaning into Thornwood's desperation.

**Pushed Failure Consequence:** Thornwood calls the bluff. "You can't protect me. You're not even police. You're — what are you? The Mayor's pet psychics?" She laughs, and the laugh has an edge of hysteria. Then she looks at Drummond. "Can she actually promise me anything? Legally?"

Drummond, who is honest to a fault, says: "No. She can't."

**Mechanical result:** Velvet's credibility with Thornwood is destroyed. More importantly, Drummond heard Velvet make promises she couldn't keep in his interrogation room. His disposition doesn't drop a full tier — he understands the impulse — but he adds a mental note: this one overreaches. Future requests for Drummond's resources require a Persuade check that was previously automatic. Thornwood cooperates with Drummond directly (she now trusts the detective more than Velvet), giving up Walsh's name, but she specifically excludes Velvet from the conversation. No deep dive options.

Why it's not catastrophic: Walsh's name surfaces. Drummond isn't alienated, just warier. The cost is subtle but persistent — every future Drummond interaction has slightly more friction. 

**Pushing Charm:** Velvet tries harder to connect personally. She shares something of herself — her own experience with desperate choices, being in over her head, the feeling of the ground shifting. She's being genuine, which is what makes the failure worse.

**Pushed Failure Consequence:** Thornwood connects — and then uses the connection as a weapon. She mirrors Velvet's vulnerability back at her, twisting it. "You think we're alike? You think you understand me? You're sitting on the right side of that table because someone picked you. The Mayor invited you to a party. Nobody invited me to anything."

The words land. Velvet loses 1 Sanity — not from cosmic horror, but from the mundane cruelty of having genuine empathy thrown back in her face. It's a small, personal wound.

Thornwood then turns to Drummond and cooperates with him, pointedly excluding Velvet. Walsh's name comes out. No deep dive options through Velvet.

Mechanical result: 1 Sanity loss. Thornwood cooperates with Drummond only. Walsh leverage is obtained but attributed to Drummond's work, not Velvet's. Drummond's disposition is unchanged — he actually respects that Velvet tried — but Velvet doesn't get credit for the break.

Why it's not catastrophic: 1 Sanity is a pinprick. The Walsh information flows. The cost is personal and narrative rather than mechanical — Velvet tried to be kind and got cut for it. Deep Dive Options (Phase 3A) These assume Thornwood is cooperating (initial break succeeded) and Velvet is pushing for deeper intelligence.

### Phase 3A: Thornwood Cooperates (Psychology, Persuade, or Charm success) {#phase-3a:-thornwood-cooperates-(psychology,-persuade,-or-charm-success)}

More immediately useful: the buyer told Walsh to deliver the jar to a post office box in New York. Worthington has a copy. **Failure:** Thornwood doesn't remember details about the buyer. Dead end on this path.

**Option B — "Tell me about Eddie Mars — what did he see when the jar broke?"**

Check — **Psychology (Regular):**

**Success:** Thornwood wasn't close to Mars during the break, but she talked to him afterward on the phone in the hotel lobby. He was raving.

"Eddie kept saying the jar moved. That it jumped out of his hands. And when it broke, he felt something pull at him — like something was trying to get inside him through his skin. > **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Targeting Intelligence:** The creature prioritizes people who know about it or have handled its artifacts. It recognized Bronson as a specific threat (she knew the binding). It tried to enter Mars because he touched the vessel. This means the creature is intelligent, has awareness of who poses a danger, and will target the binding ritualist first during the confrontation. *Tactical implication:* The person performing the binding must be protected or hidden (shabti figurine). The creature will focus all attacks on whoever begins speaking the prayer. This foreknowledge allows the party to plan accordingly — decoys, defensive positioning, the shabti holder as the primary ritualist.

**Failure:** Thornwood's account of Mars's experience is vague and unhelpful. "He was scared. We all were."

**Option C — "Where's Walsh now? What's he got?"**

Check — **No check required.** Thornwood gives up the warehouse location on Federal Hill freely once she's cooperating — this is the basic information she trades for protection.

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Walsh Leverage:** Velvet knows the warehouse location, crew names, and that Walsh is frightened. This can be used two ways:

His resources are unavailable but the stolen goods are recovered. Drummond's opinion of Velvet improves significantly. Drummond might give Velvet a 12 ga. shotgun that "I'll want that back later."

Walsh's men are unreliable (they'll flee from the creature) but useful as meat shields and for creating diversions.

*Velvet can only pursue one of Option A, B, or C in depth during this scene.* The interrogation has momentum — Thornwood is talking, but she's exhausted and terrified, and Drummond will want to move on to formal booking. If Velvet pushes for a second deep line of questioning, it requires a **Persuade (Hard)** check to keep Thornwood engaged, and Drummond becomes visibly impatient ("We have what we need for the case, let's book her").

### SCENE 3: Geology Convention — Nicky {#scene-3:-geology-convention-—-nicky}

**Characters:** Nicky (PC), various geologists (NPCs)

**Location:** Three conference rooms on the Biltmore's 17th floor, same floor as the ballroom crime scene. **Nicky's Assets:** He's a working magician — a performer. He's charismatic in a gangly, eccentric way. He also has a 50 score in Irish Criminal Underworld (Boston), which won't help directly here but establishes him as someone comfortable navigating unfamiliar social situations through charm and misdirection. His challenge is that he has no science skills — he needs to befriend people who do.

### Beat 1: Getting In {#beat-1:-getting-in}

**Approach Options:**

| Approach | Skill | DC | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

This costs an additional 30 minutes of in-game time.

**Alternative — if Nicky entered by finding the bar:** Rikards isn't a drinker, but his graduate assistant Thomas Yee is. Thomas is twenty-four, nervous, and starstruck by Nicky's stage performer energy. He'll happily introduce Nicky to his advisor.

### Beat 3: Convincing Rikards {#beat-3:-convincing-rikards}

Rikards is the key NPC. He's forty-five, socially awkward, brilliant in his field, and completely materialist. He doesn't believe in the supernatural and never will. But he believes in radiation, and he believes in solving problems.

**The Challenge:** Nicky needs to convince Rikards to part with torbernite samples (valuable, radioactive, and not easily replaced) and to provide his scientific expertise. Rikards won't respond to supernatural framing. Nicky needs to describe the problem in terms Rikards can engage with.

**Approach determines what Nicky gets:**

**Approach A — The Scientific Frame:**

"There's something in this building — some kind of organism — that's killing people. It's fluid, amorphous, regenerates damage. We need something that disrupts its cellular cohesion."

**Check — Persuade or Fast Talk (Regular):**

**Success:** Rikards lights up. This is a problem he can solve. He starts thinking aloud:

"Amorphous? Regenerating? If it's organic — truly organic — then ionizing radiation would disrupt cellular repair mechanisms. I have torbernite samples. Uranium-bearing copper phosphate. The radiation is modest but constant. If this organism is sensitive to it..."

He offers samples immediately.

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Torbernite Samples (x3):** Three chunks of green, crystalline mineral, each about the size of a fist. Each creates a 5-foot radius of creature-disrupting effect. Within this radius: creature cannot regenerate, normal weapons deal full damage, proto-shoggoths dissolve in 1d6 rounds. Samples can be placed statically (area denial) or carried (personal protection, but the carrier is exposed to low-level radiation — no mechanical effect for short-term use).

**Failure:** Rikards is skeptical. "An amorphous regenerating organism? In a hotel? I think you've been reading too many pulp magazines, Mr..." He won't give up samples without further convincing. Nicky must succeed on a follow-up check to get the torbernite (see below).

**Approach B — The Showman's Frame:**

Nicky plays to his strengths. He's a magician — he knows how to hook an audience. Instead of explaining the problem abstractly, he shows Rikards *evidence*. This requires Nicky to have something physical: residue from the crime scene, ash from the proto-shoggoth fight, or even just a vivid description of what he personally witnessed.

**Check — Fast Talk (Regular) or Persuade (Regular):** Frame the evidence compellingly.

**Success:** Rikards examines the evidence with scientific intensity. If Nicky has residue or ash, Rikards tests it with his equipment and becomes genuinely fascinated — "This is organic but the cellular structure is... I've never seen anything like this." He's now personally invested. He provides torbernite samples AND offers to help further.

> **ADVANTAGE GAINED — Torbernite Samples (x3)** plus **Rikards as Active Ally:** Rikards will work on the radiation device (see Beat 4). Without this level of investment, he hands over samples but goes back to his conference.

**Failure:** Rikards is unconvinced. The evidence isn't compelling enough. Same follow-up requirement as Approach A failure.

**Follow-up for any failure — Science (Chemistry or Physics) or INT (Hard):**

Nicky probably can't make this check on his own. But if Thomas Yee is present (from Beat 2), Thomas can make the check instead at Regular difficulty, providing the scientific vocabulary Nicky lacks. This is the mechanical payoff for befriending the graduate student.

**Success on follow-up:** Rikards provides torbernite samples (x3) but doesn't invest further. No radiation device.

**Failure on follow-up:** Rikards refuses to give up his samples. Nicky can steal them later (Stealth and Sleight of Hand, both Regular, while Rikards is out of the room), but theft yields only 1 sample instead of 3, and Rikards becomes hostile if he discovers the theft.

## Convergence: Bringing the Threads Together {#convergence:-bringing-the-threads-together}

After the three scenes resolve, the party reconvenes. Here's how to drive toward the confrontation without sprawl.

**The Forcing Mechanism:** The escalation timeline. By the time all three groups finish their scenes, approximately 4-6 in-game hours have passed since the heist. The creature is at Hour 5-7 on the timeline. Proto-shoggoths are active. A hotel guest is dead. Power is flickering. Drummond is getting pressure from the Mayor.

**The Convergence Scene:** The party meets in the geology convention rooms (the safe zone Nicky discovered). They share what they've learned. This is a planning session — the players lay out their advantages, identify gaps, and choose their approach to the final confrontation.

Give them 30 minutes of real time to plan. Then the creature forces their hand: the lights go out across the entire hotel. Screaming from lower floors. The Mayor orders evacuation. The creature is manifesting fully.

**What each thread contributed to the confrontation:**

| Thread | Contribution |
| :---- | :---- |
| Brown University | How to bind the creature (ritual knowledge, prayer, tyet usage, possible tactical insights) |
| Thornwood Interrogation | Why the creature behaves as it does \+ either human resources (Walsh's muscle/weapons) or tactical intelligence (targeting behavior, emergency containment) |
| Geology Convention | What hurts the creature (torbernite, acid, radiation device) \+ where to stage from (safe zone) |

The three threads are complementary by design. Brown provides the *method*. The interrogation provides *intelligence*. The convention provides *weapons*. A party that succeeds across all three enters the confrontation well-prepared. A party that partially succeeds has gaps they'll feel during the fight. A party that mostly failed has the tyet, the guaranteed ritual basics from Pemberton, and whatever they can improvise — which is survivable but costly.

# ACT 4: THE CONFRONTATION — Revised and Extended

## Ibnhotep as Antagonist, Not Just Monster

The adventure as written treats Ibnhotep primarily as a creature — a stat block with weaknesses. For the final fight to be memorable, Ibnhotep needs to be a *person* who became a monster. He was a priest, intelligent, desperate to survive, willing to consume others to persist. Three thousand years in the jar didn't kill those traits. It sharpened them.

**Ibnhotep can communicate.** Not speech — he has no mouth in any conventional sense. But he can project emotion, images, and fragmented words directly into the minds of anyone within 30 feet. This manifests as a pressure behind the eyes, a taste of copper on the tongue, and then meaning — not language exactly, but understanding. Characters experience this as intrusive thought that they know isn't their own.

He can also speak through the consumed ka. When he does this, the voice is recognizably the consumed person's — Dr. Bronson's measured academic tone, the hotel guest's frightened stammering, the panicked whisper of a museum worker who died in 1879\. He uses their memories, their mannerisms, their knowledge. This is not possession or resurrection. It's puppetry of the dead.

**Ibnhotep's goals during the fight, in priority order:**

1. Destroy the tyet necklace (without it, the binding is impossible; he knows this)  
2. Kill or drain whoever is performing the binding ritual  
3. Escape the radiation field  
4. Survive  
5. If all else fails, negotiate

He is not mindless. He will retreat from losing positions. He will feint. He will target the most dangerous threat, not the nearest one. He will try to separate the party. He has had three thousand years to think, and the minds he consumed include scholars, priests, and soldiers.

## Ibnhotep — Revised Full Form

The stat block from the adventure is functional but flat. Here's a revised version that creates a more dynamic, phase-shifting fight.

### Core Stats (Unchanged)

| Attribute | Value |
| :---- | :---- |
| STR | 150 |
| CON | 200 |
| SIZ | 90 |
| DEX | 70 |
| INT | 80 |
| POW | 100 |
| HP | 29 |
| Damage Bonus | \+2D6 |
| Build | 3 |
| Move | 10 (flowing) |
| Armor | 5 (regenerates 5 HP/round unless exposed to radiation or acid) |
| Sanity Loss | 1D6/1D20 |

### Ibnhotep Attack Modes (Revised — Context Dependent)

Ibnhotep doesn't cycle through attacks mechanically. His form shifts based on tactical need. He has access to all of these but uses them situationally:

**Pseudopod Lash (Standard Combat)** 60%, 2D6+DB, reach 15 feet. Up to 3 per round against different targets. This is his suppressive attack — keeping people back, punishing anyone who approaches without protection.

**Engulf (Targeted Kill)** 40%, must be within 5 feet. On success, target is pulled inside the creature's mass. Automatic drain: 3D6 CON per round. Target can attempt STR vs. creature's STR to escape each round. Other investigators can attack the creature to force a release (dealing 10+ damage in a single hit forces the creature to eject the victim). The engulfed person can still act — scream, use items, even attack from inside (attacks from within bypass Armor).

**Drain Tendril (Surgical Strike)** 70%, reach 20 feet, 1D6 CON drain (no physical damage). This is how Ibnhotep feeds at range. It's a thin, almost invisible filament of protoplasm — Spot Hidden (Hard) to notice before it connects. He uses this to weaken a target over multiple rounds before closing for the engulf. Characters being drained feel sudden exhaustion, cold, and a brief flash of Ibnhotep's hunger — an involuntary empathic connection. 0/1 SAN per drain.

**Spawn Proto-Shoggoth** Costs 5 HP. Spawns at end of round. Proto-shoggoths act on the following round. Ibnhotep uses these tactically — to flank, to pursue fleeing targets, to threaten the container/ritual components while he engages the main threat.

**Psychic Assault (New)** Opposed POW roll, range 30 feet, once per round as a free action (doesn't count against attack limit). On success, target experiences a burst of consumed memory — drowning in sand, suffocating in darkness, the moment of their own death as experienced by one of Ibnhotep's victims. 1/1D4 SAN and the target loses their next action (stunned). On failure, the target feels pressure but resists. Characters who have seen the creature before (photos, prior encounters) get a bonus die on this resistance.

### Ibnhotep Injury Level Behavior

**Phase 1: Above 20 HP — Predator** Ibnhotep is confident and aggressive. He uses pseudopod lashes to keep the party separated, drain tendrils to weaken isolated targets, and psychic assault to stun the most dangerous threat. He spawns proto-shoggoths to guard his flanks and pursue anyone trying to set up the binding ritual. He focuses attacks on whoever holds the tyet necklace — he knows what it does and he wants it destroyed.

*Keeper behavior:* Target the tyet-holder. Spawn one proto-shoggoth per round for the first two rounds. Use psychic assault on whoever seems to be directing the group's tactics. Punish clustering with area pseudopod sweeps. Reward spreading out by picking off isolated characters with drain tendrils.

**Phase 2: 10-20 HP — Desperate** Ibnhotep shifts to more extreme tactics. He attempts to engulf the most weakened character (lowest current CON). He stops spawning proto-shoggoths to preserve HP. His psychic assaults become more personal — using consumed memories of Bronson to address the investigators by name, reference things Bronson knew about them (Bronson had dinner with them at the exhibition, she observed them).

*Keeper behavior:* One engulf attempt per round on the weakest target. Drain tendrils on anyone approaching with the container. Retreat from torbernite aggressively — he'll flow through vents, across ceilings, behind walls to avoid the radiation field. Force the party to chase him. Use Bronson's voice: "Please — it hurts, it's always hungry, I can feel everyone it's ever consumed —" This is real. Bronson's ka IS in there. Whether this is Ibnhotep manipulating her memories or Bronson genuinely crying out is unknowable.

**Phase 3: Below 10 HP — Cornered** Ibnhotep knows he's losing. Three possible behaviors depending on circumstances:

*If radiation is suppressing him:* He concentrates all attacks on the radiation source — Rikards' device or whoever carries the torbernite. He will sacrifice HP to spawn proto-shoggoths specifically to destroy or knock away the radiation source. If the device is destroyed or dropped, he immediately regenerates and the fight resets.

*If the binding ritual has started:* All attacks focus on the ritualist. Every pseudopod, every drain tendril, every psychic assault targets whoever is speaking the prayer. He will ignore all other threats. He will accept hits without retaliating against anyone except the speaker. This is pure survival instinct.

*If neither:* He tries to negotiate. See "The Negotiation" below.

**Phase 4: 0 HP — The Binding Window** At 0 HP, Ibnhotep collapses into a quivering mass of translucent protoplasm. He is not dead — he's stunned. Without torbernite suppression, he regenerates 5 HP per round and will be back to Phase 3 in two rounds. With torbernite suppression, he remains at 0 HP but is still conscious and can use psychic assault (only). The party has a limited window to complete the binding.

At 0 HP, his psychic projection becomes constant and omnidirectional. Everyone within 30 feet hears the consumed ka — dozens of voices, layered, some screaming, some weeping, some begging. This costs 1/1D6 SAN for everyone in range, once, when it begins.

## The Negotiation

When Ibnhotep drops below 10 HP and isn't being actively bound, he tries to talk. This isn't a trick — or rather, it's not *only* a trick. He's genuinely desperate. He's been imprisoned for three thousand years. He doesn't want to go back.

He communicates through psychic projection and through Bronson's voice simultaneously — the projection carries intent and emotion, Bronson's voice provides words. The effect is deeply unsettling: Bronson's calm academic tone speaking on behalf of the thing that killed her.

**Ibnhotep's Offers (escalating desperation):**

These aren't sequential. He leads with whatever he calculates will work on whoever is present. He's consumed enough minds to be a decent judge of character, and he has Bronson's memories of the evening — she observed the investigators at the exhibition.

**To Dr. Elgin (the psychologist):** "I have consumed seventeen minds since the temple. Priests, scholars, a British curator. Their understanding of consciousness — of what lies beneath thought — you could spend a lifetime studying and never learn what I could show you in an instant. The architecture of the soul, Dr. Elgin. Carl Jung scratches at the surface. I have seen the foundation."

*Mechanically:* If Elgin accepts, she makes an opposed POW roll. Success: she receives a permanent \+20 to Cthulhu Mythos and loses an equal amount of Sanity, but gains genuine insight into the relationship between consciousness and the Mythos — functionally, a permanent bonus die on all Psychology rolls related to the supernatural. This takes 1 full round, during which Ibnhotep regenerates (if not suppressed). Failure: Elgin loses 1D10 SAN and gains nothing — the consumed minds are too fragmented to communicate coherently, and the experience of contact is overwhelming.

**To Till (the anthropologist with occult interest):** "The temple guardians knew the old rituals. Not the fragments your universities preserve. The complete practices. I consumed three of them. Their knowledge is mine. Paleolithic binding sites. Neolithic astronomical alignments your discipline has never identified. Sites of power across the American continent that your European scholars haven't found because they don't know how to look. I can give you a map."

*Mechanically:* Same structure as Elgin's offer — opposed POW roll, 1 full round. Success: permanent \+15 to Archaeology and \+10 to Occult, with corresponding Sanity loss, plus specific knowledge of three genuine sites of Mythos significance in New England (campaign hooks). Failure: 1D10 SAN, nothing gained.

**To Nicky (the magician who believes):** "You believe in magic. You're right to. The words your grandmother whispered, the signs your people carved — they work. I am proof. I can teach you a working. One real spell. Not a binding. Something for you. A ward that will save your life someday."

*Mechanically:* If Nicky accepts, he learns Elder Sign (the actual spell, not the symbol). This is a genuine, powerful protective magic. The cost is the standard SAN loss for learning a Mythos spell, plus 1 full round of regeneration time for Ibnhotep. There's no opposed POW roll here — Ibnhotep genuinely wants to trade this, because a magician who knows the Elder Sign might be convinced to use it to protect Ibnhotep rather than bind him. It's a bribe, and it's a real one.

**To Gale (the biologist):** "I was human once. What I became — your biology can explain it. The cellular transformation. The mechanism of the drain. You study life. I am life taken past its natural limit. Study me. Contain me, if you must, but not in a jar. A laboratory. Observation. Understanding. You could publish findings that would redefine your field."

*This one has no mechanical payoff.* It's pure manipulation — Ibnhotep reads Gale as modest and academic and tries to frame imprisonment-with-access as a reasonable middle ground. There is no version of "studying" Ibnhotep that doesn't end in death. But the pitch is designed to sound reasonable for exactly one dangerous moment.

**To Velvet (wearing the tyet):** Ibnhotep doesn't offer Velvet anything. He's afraid of the tyet. Instead, through Bronson's voice, he says: "Caitlin knew what the necklace does. She wore it because she was afraid of me. She was right to be afraid. But she was wrong about one thing — she thought the binding was permanent. It isn't. It held for three thousand years because no one opened the jar. How long before someone opens the next one? A hundred years? Fifty? You're sealing me away for someone else to deal with. And the next time, there won't be a Pemberton. There won't be a tyet. There will just be me, and whoever is standing nearby."

*This isn't an offer. It's a threat dressed as pragmatism.* And it's true — the binding is containment, not destruction. Whether this matters to the characters is their choice.

### The Dilemma Structure of the Negotiation

The negotiation creates a specific kind of tension for self-interested characters: **the creature is offering them things they genuinely want, and the cost is time.** Each round spent listening is a round Ibnhotep regenerates (if torbernite isn't active) or a round the binding isn't happening. The offers are real — the knowledge, the spell, the insights are genuine. The creature is not lying about what it can give.

The question isn't "is this worth it?" (it might be) but "can we afford the time, and do we trust that the situation stays stable?" If the torbernite is active and Ibnhotep can't regenerate, a character could theoretically accept an offer with relatively low risk — one round of Ibnhotep at 0 HP, suppressed, isn't going to change the fight's outcome. But if the torbernite is flickering, if the device is damaged, if someone is bleeding out — that same round could be catastrophic.

**The characters choose the risk calculus.** There is no "right" answer. A character who delays the binding to learn a spell and then watches a party member die during the extra round of fighting will carry that weight. A character who refuses the knowledge and completes the binding quickly will always wonder what they missed. Both are valid outcomes.

## The Fight: Phase Structure

### Phase 1: First Contact (15-20 minutes real time)

**Regardless of approach, the first moment of contact plays out the same way:**

The creature *speaks.* Not immediately attacking. Bronson's voice, projected through the walls, reverberating in the pipes:

"I know what you're carrying. The necklace. The stone. The jar fragments. I can feel them. I've felt them for three thousand years."

A pause. The phosphorescent glow intensifies at the far end of whatever space they're in. The creature is visible — enormous, filling the space like a tide of translucent flesh, internal structures pulsing with stolen life. Shapes writhe inside it. A hand presses against the surface from within, fingers splayed, then dissolves back into the mass.

"The woman who wore the necklace — Bronson — she tried to keep me sealed. She failed. The women at the British Museum tried. They failed. You will fail. But I am not unreasonable."

**This is the negotiation attempt from Phase 3 behavior, triggered early.** Ibnhotep is strong enough to fight, but he's also been imprisoned for three millennia and he's *intelligent.* He'd rather not risk a fight against people carrying torbernite and the tyet. He opens with conversation.

*What he wants:* The tyet destroyed. Without it, no binding is possible. If the party destroys the tyet, he'll leave. He'll flow out through the sewers, disperse into the river, and eventually reform somewhere else. This is genuinely bad for the world but not immediately the party's problem.

*What he offers:* See the negotiation offers above, adjusted for the moment. He leads with whichever character he reads as most susceptible. He has Bronson's memories of the evening — she watched the investigators, spoke with some of them, formed impressions. He uses those impressions.

**The party decides how to respond.** They can:

1. **Attack immediately.** The fight begins at full intensity. Proceed to the assault section below. Ibnhotep is at full HP and retaliates with everything.  
     
2. **Listen.** Each round of conversation is a round of status quo. Ibnhotep doesn't attack while talking (genuinely — he'd rather negotiate). But he's also studying them, identifying the threat hierarchy, and positioning proto-shoggoths behind the party through vents they haven't noticed. If the conversation extends past 3 rounds, a proto-shoggoth attacks the most vulnerable party member from behind (the one not holding torbernite or the tyet) as a "demonstration" — Ibnhotep claims it was involuntary ("Forgive that. My children are hungry. I cannot always control them.") This is a lie. It's a test of their defenses.  
     
3. **Accept an offer.** See the negotiation section above for mechanics. This is genuinely possible and has real consequences. The party might gain something valuable. But every round spent on the exchange is a round they're exposed.  
     
4. **Pretend to listen while positioning.** Fast Talk or Stealth (Regular per character) to move into tactical positions during the conversation. If Ibnhotep notices (opposed roll: his INT 80 vs. their skill), he attacks immediately and has a bonus die on his first round's attacks ("You think I survived three thousand years without recognizing a trap?").

**The Shabti Factor:** Till is invisible to the creature. During the conversation, she can move freely — approach, position the container, splash honey — without Ibnhotep's awareness. However, if she attacks or touches the creature, the invisibility breaks and Ibnhotep immediately identifies her as the most dangerous threat (someone he couldn't see is more frightening than someone he could). She gets one free action — make it count.

### THE ASSAULT

Once combat begins (however it starts), the fight has a rhythm:

**Round 1-2: Ibnhotep's Opening**

Ibnhotep targets the tyet first. Pseudopod lashes at Velvet — not to kill her, but to tear the necklace from her. If he gets it, he dissolves it in his mass. The tyet resists (it's a genuine artifact of power) but he can suppress it for 1D6 rounds. If the party doesn't recover the tyet within that window, it's destroyed.

*Decision point for Velvet:* The tyet protects her from the drain. If she removes it (to prevent it from being a target, or to begin preparing the binding), she's fully vulnerable. If she keeps wearing it, every attack Ibnhotep makes targets her specifically. She's the lightning rod. Does she accept that role?

Simultaneously, any proto-shoggoths (1-3 depending on how the approach went) attack other party members. Their targets: Nicky's radiation device (they try to knock it from his hands or damage it), and Pemberton (she's the backup ritualist; killing her removes a safety net).

**Round 3-4: The Radiation Question**

If Nicky deploys the torbernite device, the fight changes dramatically. Within the 15-foot cone, Ibnhotep loses regeneration, takes 1D3 automatic damage per round, and proto-shoggoths dissolve in 1 round. He will do everything possible to escape the cone or destroy the device.

*Decision point for Nicky:* The device is two-handed and fragile. While aiming it, Nicky can't fight, can't dodge (penalty die on Dodge rolls), and can't help with the binding. He's a weapon platform, nothing else. If he drops it to do something else — fight, help restrain the creature, protect an ally — the radiation field vanishes and Ibnhotep regenerates.

The creature's response to radiation: it flows away, fast, trying to get behind the cone or above it. In an enclosed space, this means it climbs walls, flows across ceilings, squeezes through spaces that seem too small. Nicky has to track it, pivoting the heavy device. DEX rolls each round to keep the cone on target if the creature is actively evading.

*The radiation is also affecting the party.* Anyone within the 15-foot cone for more than 3 consecutive rounds takes 1 temporary CON damage (radiation sickness — nausea, dizziness, metallic taste). This builds: 1 CON per round after the third. The torbernite doesn't distinguish between creature and human. In a confined space, staying out of the cone while fighting a creature that's trying to escape the cone is a spatial puzzle with no clean solution.

**Round 5-6: Ibnhotep Adapts**

After 4 rounds of combat, Ibnhotep changes tactics based on what's hurting him most:

*If radiation is the main threat:* He splits. A section of his mass detaches — not a proto-shoggoth, but a substantial portion of himself (reduce his HP by 8; the detached portion has 8 HP and the same attack capabilities as a proto-shoggoth but at double the damage dice). The detached portion circles behind the party to attack Nicky from outside the cone while the main body retreats. Nicky now faces a choice: turn the device to defend himself (exposing the main body) or hold position and take the hits.

*If physical damage is the main threat (shotgun, acid):* He disengages to maximum distance and uses drain tendrils exclusively — 20-foot reach, 70% accuracy, picking off CON point by point. He fights like a sniper, refusing to close. The party must chase him into spaces where his superior mobility (flowing through vents, across ceilings) gives him the advantage.

*If the binding is being prepared:* He goes all-in on the ritualist. See Phase 3\.

### Phase 2: The Binding (20-30 minutes real time)

The binding is where the adventure either resolves or falls apart. It requires:

1. Reduce creature to 0 HP OR physically restrain it  
2. Place in prepared container with jar fragments  
3. Seal with honey  
4. Attach tyet to seal  
5. Speak the prayer (15 MP total, 3 full rounds)

Each step is a potential failure point. Each step has an associated decision.

**Step 1: Subduing Ibnhotep**

*Option A — Reduce to 0 HP:* This is the "cleanest" approach. Beat it down with shotgun, acid, and torbernite until it collapses. The problem: even suppressed by radiation, Ibnhotep has 29 HP and 5 Armor. Without radiation, he regenerates 5 HP/round. The party needs sustained, focused damage output, and they need to protect the torbernite operator the entire time.

**Approximate math for the players' benefit:** Elgin's shotgun does 4D6/2D6/1D6 (close/medium/long). At close range, average 14 damage minus 5 armor \= 9 damage. If she hits two of three rounds (realistic at close range), that's \~18 damage. Acid adds 1D6 per application (average 3.5, no armor). The torbernite device adds 1D3 per round (average 2). Call it 5-7 rounds to bring Ibnhotep from full HP to 0, assuming consistent radiation suppression and nobody dies. That's 5-7 combat rounds — significant real-time investment with all the associated checks, reactions, and complications.

*Option B — Physical Restraint:* Multiple investigators combine STR for an opposed roll against the creature's STR 150\. Each investigator contributes their full STR; the highest roller adds their full value, each additional person adds half their STR. With three people (Till, Velvet, Pemberton — designated in the game notes), they might have combined effective STR of... maybe 100-120? Not enough against 150 unless the creature is weakened.

However: honey applied to the creature immobilizes portions of its mass for 1D3 rounds (if the party learned this from the library research). Gale is designated as the honey-mopper. If Gale immobilizes a significant portion of the creature's mass with honey *before* the restraint attempt, the creature's effective STR for the contest drops by 50 (to 100). Now the three-person grapple is feasible.

*Decision point:* The honey supplies are finite. Gale has a bucket and a mop. Realistically, they can make 3-4 significant honey applications before running out. Each application immobilizes a portion of the creature for 1D3 rounds. The honey is also needed for the seal (Step 3). Using it all on restraint means they need to find more for the seal. The hotel kitchen has more — but someone has to go get it during the fight.

So: does Gale use the honey aggressively (better chance of restraining the creature but may not have enough for the seal) or conservatively (save honey for the seal but the restraint is harder)?

*Option C — Combination:* Damage it to Phase 3 behavior (\~10 HP), then restrain. This is the most realistic approach — a wounded, panicked creature is easier to grapple than a healthy one. The risk is that at low HP, Ibnhotep is desperate and uses his most dangerous attacks (engulf, all-in on the ritualist).

**Step 2: The Container**

The container (with jar fragments inside) must be open and ready. Someone needs to be holding it, positioned to receive the creature's mass once it's subdued. The creature is SIZ 90 — it's large, but protoplasmic and compressible. It doesn't need to fit in the container physically; the jar fragments inside create a spiritual anchor, and the creature's mass is drawn into the vessel during the binding like water into a drain. But the container must be within 5 feet of the creature's mass, open, and stable.

*Decision point:* Whoever holds the container can't fight, can't dodge easily, and must stay close to the creature. They're a sitting duck. The shabti figurine would make this role much safer (invisible to the creature), but Till is the designated shabti-holder and she's also designated as one of the restraint team. She can't do both.

**Till's choice:** Hold the shabti and position the container (safe but she can't help restrain), or drop the shabti and help restrain (the grapple team is stronger but she's now visible and the container-holder must be someone else — Pemberton, most likely, who is more fragile and doesn't have the shabti's protection).

**Step 3: The Honey Seal**

Once the creature's mass begins entering the container (first round of the prayer), honey must be applied to the container's opening. This is a DEX roll (Regular) — not difficult mechanically, but it must be done by someone who has honey and is next to the container. If Gale used most of the honey for restraint, there might not be enough for a proper seal. A thin seal (insufficient honey) means the binding is weaker — the creature gets a bonus die on escape contests during the prayer rounds.

**Step 4: The Tyet**

Velvet must remove the tyet necklace and place it touching both the container and the honey seal. The moment she removes it, she loses its protective properties (half drain damage, proximity sense). If Ibnhotep is not fully subdued when she removes it — if he has any HP remaining, if a proto-shoggoth is still active, if anything goes wrong — she's fully exposed.

*Decision point for Velvet:* When does she remove the tyet? Early (get it in position while the fight is still going, accept the vulnerability) or late (keep the protection as long as possible, risk fumbling the placement under pressure)? Early placement means someone must guard the container assembly (open container \+ honey \+ tyet) for potentially several rounds before the prayer begins. The creature WILL target this assembly if it recognizes what's happening.

**Step 5: The Prayer**

Three full rounds. 15 MP total, divisible among speakers. Each speaker must be within 10 feet of the container and must spend their entire round speaking — no other actions, no dodging, no fighting.

*MP Budget:* Average investigator has 10-12 MP. Spending 15 MP among the group is feasible but not trivial. If split between two people, each spends 7-8 (leaving them nearly drained — 2-4 MP remaining, barely functional). Three people makes the cost manageable (5 MP each). Four people is comfortable (3-4 MP each) but that's four people who can't fight for three rounds.

*The Belief Check:* Each speaker makes a POW roll (standard if they have Occult or the belief shortcut from the library research; POW×3 if they don't). This roll is made once, at the beginning of the first prayer round. Failure doesn't prevent the prayer — it means the speaker's MP contribution is halved (rounded down). They're speaking the words but not channeling power. The prayer can still complete if other speakers pick up the slack, but the total MP cost must still be met.

*Per-Round Escape Contest:* Each round of the prayer, the creature attempts to escape the binding. Opposed STR: creature's STR (150, or 75 if the consumed ka appeal is used) vs. the restraining force (grappling investigators' combined STR, or the container's spiritual anchor if the creature is at 0 HP inside it — treat the jar fragments as STR 80 for this purpose, \+30 if the tyet is properly placed, \+20 if honey seal is strong).

If the creature wins an escape contest, it pulls partially free. The prayer is interrupted. The speakers must make a POW roll (Regular) to maintain concentration and continue; failure means they must restart from round 1\. The creature doesn't fully escape unless it wins two consecutive contests.

### THE CONSUMED KA EVENT

During the second round of the prayer (always the second round — this is narratively timed for maximum tension), the consumed souls inside Ibnhotep react to the binding. They feel Isis's power reaching for them, and they respond. This manifests as:

**Every character within 30 feet makes a POW roll (Regular).**

*Success:* The character feels a rush of warmth and foreign memory — fragments of lives lived centuries ago, temple incense, the sound of the Nile, a child's laughter. It's beautiful and heartbreaking and costs 0/1 SAN (the loss is from the understanding that these are real people, trapped and suffering, and the binding will seal them inside again).

*Failure:* A consumed ka attempts to escape *into* the character. The dying soul isn't malicious — it's desperate, drowning, reaching for any lifeline. The character experiences a full sensory overlay: they are simultaneously themselves and someone else, standing in two places, thinking two sets of thoughts. This costs 1/1D6 SAN.

More importantly, the character must make a second POW roll to reject the intruding ka. Success: the ka is pushed back into Ibnhotep's mass. Failure: the ka lodges. It doesn't possess the character — it nests, like a seed. It will fade over the following weeks, but during that time, the character experiences intrusive memories, speaks words in ancient Egyptian during sleep, and periodically feels an overwhelming hunger that isn't their own. This is a long-term consequence — mechanically, the character loses 1D3 SAN per week for 1D4 weeks as the ka slowly dissipates, and gains a permanent \+5 to Occult from the absorbed knowledge.

**The dilemma:** The characters can prevent this event entirely by incorporating the consumed ka appeal into the prayer (if they learned it from Pemberton's deeper knowledge). Speaking directly to the consumed dead, calling them to rest, promising peace — this calms the ka and prevents the escape attempt. But the appeal adds an additional round to the prayer (4 rounds total instead of 3), extending the time the ritualists are vulnerable and adding one more escape contest for the creature.

**Quick or merciful?** Three rounds, fast, efficient, but the consumed souls suffer and may intrude. Four rounds, more dangerous, more MP, but the dead are given peace and the creature's resistance is halved. Characters who prioritize their own safety choose three rounds. Characters who care about the dead (or who fear the ka intrusion) choose four.

Since this group skews self-interested, the framing should emphasize the personal risk of the ka intrusion (SAN loss, weeks of symptoms) rather than the moral dimension. The appeal isn't about compassion — it's about self-protection. "Do you want a dead Egyptian priest's memories rattling around in your head for a month? No? Then add the extra round."

### Phase 3: Aftermath and Complications (5-10 minutes real time)

**If the binding succeeds:**

The creature's mass contracts violently — a sudden, powerful implosion as Ibnhotep is drawn into the container. The room fills with a sound like a deep exhalation, the pressure change popping ears. The honey seal hardens into something that resembles amber. The tyet glows briefly — a deep blue that seems to come from inside the stone rather than reflecting light — then goes still.

The container is warm. It vibrates faintly if touched. If anyone presses their ear to it, they can hear something — not a voice, exactly, but a presence. A hum of compressed rage and hunger, muffled by three thousand years of binding magic renewed.

It's done. The creature is sealed.

But the aftermath has teeth:

**The Container Problem:** What do they do with it? The jar fragments are inside an improvised container — not the original blessed vessel. Pemberton believes the binding will hold, but she's not certain for how long. Years, probably. Decades, maybe. Three thousand years? "I wouldn't count on it."

Options:

- Give it to Pemberton / Brown University (academic custody; relatively safe; Pemberton will guard it)  
- Give it to the British Museum (most experience with the creature; but they lost it once already)  
- Give it to Merriwether (their patron; he has resources but no expertise)  
- Destroy it? (Not clear this is possible. The creature is bound, not dead. Breaking the container releases it. Extreme measures — dropping it into a volcano, sealing it in concrete, sinking it in the ocean — might work but are logistically difficult and uncertain.)  
- Keep it (most dangerous option; the creature is aware of its captors and will work to escape)

There is no right answer. This is a seed for future adventures, not a puzzle with a solution.

**The Drummond Reckoning:** If Drummond survived (and if the party maintained their relationship), he arrives in the aftermath, surveys the scene, and says nothing for a long time. Then: "I'm putting in my report that Dr. Bronson was killed by an unidentified chemical agent released during the robbery. The other deaths are attributed to building collapse from structural damage. The gangsters are the perpetrators. This is now a closed case."

He looks at the container. "I don't want to know what's in that. I don't want to see it again. And I want you people out of my city by morning."

If the party's relationship with Drummond is poor, he's not this cooperative. He demands the container as evidence, which creates a bureaucratic nightmare that takes weeks to resolve and may result in someone at the precinct opening it.

**The Walsh Loose End:** If the party involved Walsh, he wants payment — not money, but the stolen artifacts returned to him so he can complete the New York sale. If the party promised him immunity, Drummond will not honor that promise unless the party specifically negotiated it. Walsh may become a recurring antagonist if he feels betrayed.

**The Pemberton Farewell:** If Pemberton survived, she takes the party aside after the officials leave. She's exhausted, grieving, and quietly proud of them.

"Caitlin would have been grateful. She spent her career studying the dead, and in the end, the dead studied her." A pause. "If you find other things like this — sealed places, old warnings, objects that feel wrong — you can write to me at Radcliffe. I may not be able to help. But I'll know someone who can."

She is a permanent contact for future adventures. If the party earned her trust in Scene 1, she's also a friend — someone who will take their calls, vouch for them to other academics, and warn them about dangerous collections that are being moved or exhibited.

**The Personal Costs:**

Tally the damage:

- HP loss (physical injuries from the fight)  
- CON loss (temporary, from drain attacks — recovers at 1 point per day of rest)  
- SAN loss (cumulative from the entire adventure — probably 8-20 points depending on choices and luck)  
- MP drain (recovers normally)  
- Temporary CON loss from radiation exposure (recovers in 1D6 days)  
- Ka intrusion, if applicable (1D3 SAN/week for 1D4 weeks; \+5 permanent Occult)  
- Knowledge gained from Ibnhotep's offers, if any accepted (permanent skill boosts with corresponding permanent SAN loss)  
- Whatever promises they made to NPCs that they now have to keep or break

**If the binding fails:**

The creature escapes the container during the prayer. This means the party is exhausted (MP spent, HP reduced, resources used) and the creature is free. The fight continues but the binding path is closed unless they can re-prepare the container (honey, tyet placement — probably 2-3 rounds of work while fighting).

If the party cannot rebind the creature, fall back to alternative victory conditions:

**Destruction** is possible if they still have torbernite and acid. It requires reducing the creature to \-29 HP (total dissolution) while regeneration is suppressed. This is a war of attrition that the party will probably lose unless they're very lucky or very well-supplied.

**The Scarab Command** (if anyone has the scarab) is the desperation play. Opposed POW roll, instant death on failure, one command on success. "Return to the container and sleep" is the simplest command. But the creature's POW is 100 — this is a long-odds gamble.

**Retreat** is always available. The creature won't chase them into the radiation safe zone. They can fall back, rest, and try again — but the creature will be at full HP when they return, and it will have prepared for their tactics.

## Pacing Notes for the Keeper

**The 1-2 hour window:** The convergence/planning scene is 15-20 minutes. The descent is 10-15 minutes. The first contact/negotiation is 5-10 minutes. The assault is 15-20 minutes. The binding is 15-20 minutes. The aftermath is 5-10 minutes. Total: 65-95 minutes. This fits the target.

**If the fight runs long:** Skip the descent encounters (proto-shoggoth sentry, body discovery). Start the party at the basement level with the creature already visible. Cut the negotiation short — Ibnhotep says one thing, the party responds, combat begins.

**If the fight runs short:** Extend the descent with additional environmental encounters. Add a second wave of proto-shoggoths during the binding. Have Ibnhotep escape the first binding attempt, requiring a second try.

**When to use the negotiation offers:** Only if the fight has brought Ibnhotep below 10 HP and the party has a clear path to victory. The offers should feel like a temptation from a position of strength, not a delay when things are desperate. If the party is losing, skip the negotiation — Ibnhotep attacks relentlessly when he's winning.

**The golden rule of player agency in this fight:** Every round, at least one character should face a meaningful choice beyond "I attack." The choices built into the structure — Velvet's tyet timing, Nicky's device commitment, Till's shabti-vs-grapple decision, Gale's honey allocation, the prayer length — should generate enough decision points to fill the fight. If a round passes where everyone just rolls attacks, introduce a complication: proto-shoggoth from a vent, Pemberton gets hit and drops the container, the torbernite device flickers (Luck roll or it shuts down for a round), a section of ceiling collapses between the party and the creature.

## KEEPER'S NOTES {#keeper's-notes}

### Pacing {#pacing}

The adventure should feel like a pressure cooker. The escalation timeline creates urgency—every hour the investigators spend investigating, the creature grows stronger. Balance this against the need for preparation: rushing into the confrontation unprepared is suicidal.

Ideal pacing: 2-3 hours of investigation (game time, not real time), covering 4-6 in-game hours. This leaves investigators partially prepared when the creature's attacks force action.

### Information Flow {#information-flow}

Multiple paths exist to each key piece of information. Don't punish players for missing one path—let them find another. But DO reward thorough investigation with advantages. The players who talk to everyone, check every room, and think carefully should have significantly better odds in the final confrontation.

### NPC Relationships {#npc-relationships}

Track investigator relationships with key NPCs:

- **Drummond:** Starts neutral. Each useful lead improves him; each embarrassment worsens.  
- **Margaret Pemberton:** Starts neutral-positive. Academic respect builds alliance quickly.  
- **Mary Mazars:** Starts positive (if pre-heist connection) or neutral. Easy to ally.  
- **Walsh:** Starts hostile. Can become reluctant ally with negotiation.  
- **The Mayor:** Starts positive. Deteriorates if scandal spreads.

### Sanity Management {#sanity-management}

The adventure has multiple Sanity loss points:

- Heist (seeing the creature, Bronson's death): 0/1D4  
- Photos (seeing creature clearly): 0/1  
- Proto-shoggoth encounter: 0/1D6  
- Full creature encounter: 1D6/1D20  
- Bronson's body (if examined closely): 0/1D3  
- Civilian deaths (if evacuation fails): 1/1D4

Expect investigators to lose 5-15 Sanity over the adventure. Hard Sanity checks should be rare—the goal is dread, not madness.

### If Things Go Wrong {#if-things-go-wrong}

- **TPK in early proto-shoggoth attack:** The creature consumes them but is weakened. Introduce new investigators (police backup, hotel guests, Walsh's people forced to help) with some of the deceased's discovered information.  
- **Investigators never find the tyet:** Margaret Pemberton eventually seeks them out, having remembered Bronson mentioning it. Or: Agnes Blackwood provides Bronson correspondence that mentions the necklace's location.  
- **Investigators ally fully with Walsh:** This works, technically. Walsh's muscle dies horribly, but they can distract the creature. Drummond may arrest the investigators afterward.  
- **Creature escapes the hotel:** The adventure expands into Providence. The creature establishes multiple lairs. This becomes a longer campaign rather than a one-shot.
