# The Deedbox at Durgan's Rest
### Durgan's Rest — Tsendur's homestead
*Adapted from "A Mishap of Ill Portent" (Vengeance of the Shunned Part 1, Slarecian Vault),

> ## ⚑ Coming here from Sia — prep for the session after the tomb
>
> **Where the party is coming from.** Out of the Tomb of the Sphinx at the Oasis of Sia
> (sessions 4–6, `scenarios/ashes-at-the-sphinx.md`). Five PCs at level 2 or 3, plus Jiyloo and
> Orkideh's dog. They carry Elmingwed's sealed letter (Tath'Shar has it), the obsidian serpent ring,
> the unidentified white-metal spider bracelet, three unreadable Bokkaran scrolls, and whatever
> they brought out of 9p.
>
> **Why they are coming.** In session 4 Tath'Shar argued for Tsendur first, lost the vote to Sia,
> and accepted it. The party promised that Tsendur comes next.
>
> **The route.** Sia to Tel A'bib, then up into the mountains to Durgan's Rest. On the Roll20 hex
> map that is hex 1213 back to Tel A'bib, then to hex 2012. In game 1 the trip took several days
> (the party was there on days 10–14) and included trading a Bokkard book with kobolds on the way.
>
> **The letter's delay — decided.** `thread:tsendur-letter` asked whether weeks in a pocket helped
> or hurt Tsendur. **Neither.** Maghiel is not hunting Tsendur. She is following the courier, to
> learn what the sealed letters say (timing fork A). So the raid comes on the night the letter is
> delivered, whenever that is. It also means she has been shadowing the party all along,
> including at Sia. Show that once on the road (below).
>
> ### The plan: arrive, sleep one night, stop
>
> Get the party to Durgan's Rest and let them **sleep there one night and get comfortable.** The
> session will probably end by then. **Phoenix is back for the next one, and Tath'Shar delivers the
> letter themselves.**
>
> - **They arrive late in the day.** Troilan, Tsendur's husband, meets them at the gate: the master
>   is deep in his work and will see visitors in the morning. The party camps in the travellers'
>   field across the road, which is exactly where the raid will find them later.
> - **Nothing happens the first night.** Maghiel is following the courier, and the courier hasn't
>   delivered anything yet. Let them be comfortable. That is what the fire takes away.
> - **Use the evening to make the place matter.** The town: Sheriff Isi, the farmer-merchants, the
>   watering hole (source PDF). Tsendur's dwarf assistants Kada and Kayta, carrying supplies up to the
>   house. Lamplight in the study window late into the night. The players should know the house and the
>   people before they have to go into it on fire.
> - **Tath'Shar** is a quiet presence and keeps the letter. If the session runs long and someone wants
>   to push the letter through without them, Troilan's answer holds: morning.
>
> ### The audience with Elmingwed — the negotiation
>
> This happens in Tel A'bib on the way, unless the party goes into the desert after the Keeper's
> reward instead. Keep the stop to a day or two. The usual six weeks of downtime belongs after
> Durgan's Rest: the party is in the middle of a promise.
>
> **1 — The road first.** Restoring the transit lane is the thing he actually wanted. He opens with a
> merchant's questions and listens hard to the answers:
> - Is the water clean?
> - Is the cult gone, or coming back, and when?
> - Who holds the oasis tonight?
> - Can a caravan leave next week, and what will it find when it gets there?
>
> Honest, specific answers set a good mood for everything after. Vague ones make him ask again,
> politely, until they are specific.
>
> **2 — The monk.** The party hands over the captive from the sphinx. **To Elmingwed this is the
> treasure.** A living member of the Sia raid gives him proof, for the Governor and the merchant
> houses, that the Black Water Cult raids within reach of Tel A'bib. It also gives him names, and
> where the column went. Abet's militia takes the prisoner. If the party asks, Elmingwed shares what
> Abet gets out of him, which is a feed of intelligence toward Ayatorrah.
>
> **3 — The treasure they left.** He asks what they brought out of the tomb, and it is a test.
> - **Nothing, or nothing but the ring:** he is pleased, and says why. *"Anyone can carry gold out of
>   a hole. It takes judgment to leave it there."* A robbed tomb makes a cursed oasis and hostile
>   priests, and caravans stop where the dead are quiet. **They saw the bigger picture**, which is the
>   quality he has been testing for since they arrived at his gate with Jeff.
> - **Something the Keeper gave them freely:** he respects a gift, and wants to see it.
> - **Something taken:** he cools, and asks pointed questions about who else knows.
>
> **4 — What he gives: cashing the reputation.** They chose standing over coin, so make them name
> what they want. He grants what is reasonable. Things they could ask for:
> - **Public credit.** He says it in front of the merchant houses: the party reopened Sia and took no
>   fee. Feltan's standing reward for the caravans gets paid, and Elmingwed sees to it.
> - **Outfitting for the mountains.** Gear at cost from Al-Esellin (tents, cold-weather kit), mounts,
>   and supplies.
> - **Identification.** He knows the obsidian ring on sight: a Black Water token, and wearing it in
>   Tel A'bib will get you killed. The spider bracelet is Bokkaran white metal and beyond him; that is
>   Tsendur's field.
> - **Abet's militia to hold Sia** until the caravans are running. It is a real cost to him. He
>   agrees if the party makes the bigger-picture argument: guards at the well protect every caravan
>   he has on that road.
> - **Help finding a priest for Temple al Sia,** if the party made a covenant with the Keeper.
>   Elmingwed trades with Doornian pilgrims and knows who to ask.
>
> What he **won't** do is pay them for the job. Coin now would make them hirelings, and he would
> consider it an insult to them. If they haggle for gold, he pays it without argument and quietly
> files them as hirelings from then on.
>
> **5 — The letter.** He notices it: *"And Tsendur?"* He isn't angry, but he is cooler. His word has
> been travelling in a pocket, and the bearer represents him. He wants it delivered now. **He does
> not write to the Bridged City.** Introductions there are Tsendur's to make, not his.
>
> **What moves him:** discretion, useful information, thinking two steps ahead, and honouring
> obligations.
>
> **What costs:**
> - haggling for gold;
> - boasting;
> - loose talk in public about what is under the sphinx (tomb robbers listen);
> - **Orkideh** being careless. Her old deal with him over Jeff, and Jeff's papers with the
>   Boetharch, make Tel A'bib a place to tread carefully.
>
> **Closing line, if you want one:** *"Deliver my word. Then come back and tell me what Tsendur is
> guarding."* His letter already suspects there is an artifact.
>
> ### The road into the mountains — pick one or two
>
> - **Kobolds who trade.** This is the game-1 precedent. They want the Bokkaran scrolls, which are
>   worth more at Tsendur's. That makes it a small choice rather than a fight.
> - **A construct out of place** in the foothills, wandering away from Kantaron. Ren spotted the
>   first one, so it is Ren's hook coming back around.
> - **Maghiel's shadow.**
>   - One night the dog growls at nothing, and a narrow bootprint is in the frost by the picket line.
>   - Or a **drow mercenary** is seen at the edge of the firelight and is gone when anyone moves.
>   - Noticing it is the foreshadowing that makes the raid feel earned rather than sprung.
>
> ### What Tsendur can do for them — why the trip pays before the fire
>
> - **He reads Bokkaran.** The three scrolls (*Mending*, *Encode Thoughts*, *Alarm*) become usable,
>   and he will start teaching anyone who asks.
> - **He knows white metal.** He can say something true but partial about the spider bracelet: a
>   Bokkaran ward against water you cannot see. Keep the Sea of Souls name back, per the old-school
>   identification rule. If they mention the Keeper's language engine, he adds the useful half: *"Wear
>   it when you touch one. It takes half of what the machine gives — once, for one wearer, at each
>   machine."*
> - **He makes the introductions onward,** to his friend **Dain Ironheart** (beat 5).
>
> ### Maghiel and her crew — how they run, all campaign
>
> - **Maghiel is always ahead of the party in level, and very hard to beat.** She is the one
>   deliberate rail. She gets away.
> - **Her named crew stay at or near the party's level the whole way along,** so the fights against
>   them are fair. Those are **Gotrin, Tolovin and Kyorgar**, and they gain levels as the party does.
> - **When the fight turns against them, Maghiel often teleports out with her team:** whichever named
>   members are standing near her. **Anyone downed, grappled or cut off stays behind.** Downing or
>   capturing a crew member is a real, reachable win; the game-1 party did it.
> - **Every encounter brings lesser minions meant to drop:** drow mercenaries, the red shirts.
>
> **At Durgan's Rest, with the party at level 2 or 3:**
> - **Tolovin** (Rogue 3) and **Kyorgar** (Cleric 3) are already at party level. Keep them as written.
> - **Gotrin**'s source block is Monk 6. He is scaled here to **Monk 3**: one weapon attack plus a
>   bonus-action strike, and no Stunning Strike or Shadow Step yet. He gets those when the party is
>   ready for them.
> - **The three escorts are drow mercenaries,** the red shirts.
> - **Tolovin's Assassinate** still makes round 1 dangerous for anyone surprised at 3 AM. Settle the
>   watch order before the session.

## GM-ONLY TRUTH — read this before you read anything else

This session is not Maghiel's first move. **It's her origin.** Do not let this leak to the
players until it's earned.

- **Maghiel is not Thorn-cursed yet.** In this reset timeline, that hasn't happened. She is
  simply a capable, ambitious drow wizard, and right now she is looking to advance her ambition to move up in her family's power structure by finding riches, treasure and potentially ancient magic to extend her wizard studies — Tath'Shar isn't wrong that Maghiel might yet be findable and reachable. That window closes tonight.
- **The Clasp of Vergren is not what Tsendur thinks it is.** His family lore (Mesos, Enkili, 
  the prank, the Divine War — see the source PDF's Background) is real as far as it goes,
  but incomplete, possibly even a cover story laid over the real thing. **The Clasp is (or
  contains) a splinter of the Black Thorn** — a corruption vector broken off the world-tree
  El Faiz ([[artifact:al-faiz-tree]], thread:the-thorn). Drendari's Deedbox wasn't just
  hiding a relic from scrying; it was **sealing away a corruption artifact**, which is
  exactly why generations of Tsendur's family were told never to open it. Tsendur has no
  idea. He thinks he's protecting a wizard's trinket.
- **Maghiel steals the Clasp and studies it.** Away from Durgan's Rest, alone with it, she
  is pierced by the Thorn — this is the actual moment `npc:maghiel`'s prior-campaign
  "Black-Thorn-cursed, only beginning her path" status comes into being. **It happens after
  this session, off-screen**, the seed of everything downstream (Bridged City, Crystal
  Towers, the eventual Al-Faiz Springs confrontation) growing from a scene the party stood
  in and couldn't stop.
- **This is a hard rail, and that's deliberate.** Maghiel must escape with the Clasp. The
  party can fight, capture, or kill as many of her escort as they like — the source material
  already hands you this exactly: seven cloaked figures go in, one elven woman comes out
  alone. Let that stand. Everything else about this session is open; this one beat isn't.
  If it helps, the in-fiction reason is simple and needs no more engineering than the source
  already gives you: she has a prepared escape (a scroll, a bonded item, a plane-touched
  trick — your call) that the party's timing doesn't allow them to counter. Don't telegraph
  it as unfair; telegraph it as *she was already leaving when the fighting started.*
- **The party is complicit, and that's the point.** Elmingwed's letter (see below) is what
  put Tath'Shar in front of Tsendur with a warning about "prohibited artifacts" — and it's
  entirely fair to let the players wonder, later, whether that letter (or Tath'Shar's own
  inquiries along the way) is what put Maghiel on Durgan's Rest's map in the first place.
  You don't have to resolve which it was. The responsibility to stop what happens next
  belongs to the whole table, not just Tath'Shar's player, because the whole party carried
  the message that (maybe) brought her here.

## The letter

Give the players the actual text, or close to it, when Tath'Shar presents it to Tsendur —
this is not a MacGuffin, it's information that matters:

> *Tsendur — the bearer is Tath'Shar, of family Tath, searching for a sister
> they have reason to think is active in this region: a drow, newly wizarded, dangerous, 
> driven, and — I am reliably told — hunting artifacts that ought to stay buried. 
> Guard your artifacts and guard yourself. Warn your scholarly friends.
> I would take it as a personal favor if you gave them what help you can. 
> — Elmingwed*

**Why Elmingwed knows this much:** Tath'Shar has brought him sealed letters which they cannot read without breaking seals. Elmingwed reads them and is in some way obligated to the bearer of the letters as a representative of the sender.

**The dramatic irony:** Tsendur reads a warning about someone hunting a prohibited artifact
while standing in the room next to one, with no idea it's the same object. If a player
makes that connection before Tsendur does, let them try to act on it — that's the session
working.

## Timing fork — pick one live at the table

**A. Timed to arrival (recommended).** The explosion happens the same day the party walks
into Durgan's Rest with the letter. Maximum stakes: Tath'Shar is present for the exact
moment their sister crosses the line, and the party is the only witness with any context for
what just happened. The reason the explosions happen after the party arrived is that Maghiel was following the party, and following Tath'Shar in particular, to gain information about what was in the sealed letters they were carrying to Tel A'bib.

## Scene beats

**1 — Arrival & the letter.** Durgan's Rest is a small, cosmopolitan farming market town at
the crest of the Kelder Mountains (full stats/NPCs: source PDF, pop. 220, Sheriff Isi,
farmer-merchants). Tath'Shar presents Elmingwed's letter to Tsendur. Play this scene for
what it is: a stranger asking an old scholar for help finding a woman he's never heard of,
carrying a warning that means nothing to him yet.

**2 — The Blast.** A thunderous boom rises from Tsendur's homestead at the north edge of
town — moments after the letter, or the next morning, your call.

**3 — The Burning Homestead.** Full room-by-room detail, fire-spread mechanics, and
minute-by-minute maps are in the source (pages 7-10) — use them directly. **Scorch
zombies** (stat block, page 13) are the threat; a bystander named Jasper gets grabbed and
needs rescuing. Notable loot: a *wand of magic missiles*, a spare copy of Tsendur's
spellbook, and a couple of comedic minor-magic bathroom fixtures (a self-heating washtub, a
self-cleaning commode) — good comic relief in an otherwise tense scene. **Seven cloaked
figures** (Maghiel + six escorts) hit the house hard and fast, ahead of the party — the
scorch zombies are what's left doing damage control, not Maghiel's party itself.

**4 — Tsendur's Account.** If he survives (condition table, source page 10 — his HP/
exhaustion depends on how many rounds the party took to reach him), Tsendur explains: an
elven woman he'd glimpsed once before, at Bridged City's Academy, tortured the location of
the Clasp out of him and fled into the woods alone — pursuit fails automatically (see
GM-ONLY TRUTH above; this is the hard rail). **Play the reveal without naming her.** Let the
description — elven, robed, moved with terrible speed and purpose — be the only clue on the
table. If Tath'Shar's player wants a personal gut-punch, let them notice one specific detail
nobody else would clock as the moment they privately realize it's their sister, without
saying so aloud yet. If the party fought and captured or killed any of the six escorts, they can be
interrogated/looted for a name, a faction hint, or nothing at all — your call on how much to
give away this early.

**5 — Aftermath & the hook onward.** **Tsendur makes the introductions, not Elmingwed.**
He sends the party to his friend **Dain Ironheart**, a dwarf forge-cleric and explosives expert.
Dain is an NPC in the *Scholarly Schism* module. In game 1, Tsendur gave the party letters of
introduction to Dain and to **Savila** (`npc:savila`, head of the College); give them the Savila
letter as well if you like.

What waits in the Bridged City:
- **The College of Engineering and Magic is closed, and its main tower is sealed because of
  contamination.** In this run **the seal is Maghiel's.** That replaces the module's version, where
  the faculty sealed the Academy themselves against wild magic.
- **Breaking the seal takes Dain's help.** He brings his barrel of Bokkaran-inscribed powder on a
  cart. It is the module's breach scene (VoS-2, chapter two), and game 1's notes have Dain arguing
  his barrel through the gate.
- **Below the tower:** the caverns down to the **Sea of Souls**, the ancient lizard **Yormon
  Therdasiroth**, and a **second language engine**. That one does 10d6+10 psychic damage to whoever
  touches it, and leaves a lasting mark (`assets/_raw/Bridged City.../Bridged City Lore.md`).
- **The spider bracelet** from the strongbox (session 3) finally pays off there, twice over:
  - It wards the wearer against the Sea of Souls' sleep.
  - It halves the engine's damage, for one wearer per bracelet at that engine. A bracelet already used
    at the Keeper's engine in Sia still works at Yormon's, because the limit is per engine.

Let this scene also plant **Tsendur as a standing contact**. He is grateful now, guilty once he
learns what he was really protecting, and a door to other Bokkaran-magic scholars. The arc is
`world/adventures.md` → *Bridged City — Scholarly Schism*, with Maghiel's crew (Gotrin, Tolovin and
Kyorgar, levelled to match the party).

## Combat reference — the fire

*Stat block conventions (tracker table + reference card, single-die damage encoding, and the

stat blocks." Paste flow confirmed working: paste this section into Google Docs as markdown,

### Round-by-round GM guide — the ambush

Timing fork A (recommended, per above): the party is camped across the road from Tsendur's
homestead, in the open field travelers use for tents. Maghiel's crew hits the house **on the
night the letter is delivered**, not the party's first night in town (see *The plan*, above).

**Setup — 3 AM.** A flash and a boom from the house. Anyone on watch is not surprised;
everyone else is surprised for round 1.
**Your move:** roll/assign watch order before this if you haven't already; don't let the
party choose who's "on watch" retroactively.

**Round 1 — Breach.** Maghiel's escort is already inside, having blown a hole in the wall
moments before — they're not arriving, they're already fighting their way to Tsendur.
**Your move:** run the surprise round for everyone not on watch, then drop Gotrin, Tolovin,
Kyorgar, and the three drow mercenaries into the breach (A1/A4-ish, GM's call on exact
placement) already engaged. Tolovin's Assassinate (see his card) makes this round nasty for
anyone still surprised.

**Round 2 — Fire and zombies.** The fire spreads while the party fights and tries to push
into the building; the scorch zombies (above) start appearing as the party breaches rooms.
**Your move:** introduce zombies as the party enters A1/A4, not all at once — and start
narrating the fire as a ticking hazard (see the source's room-by-room fire rules, p.7-10),
not just set dressing.

**Round 3 — The getaway.** Tath'Shar sees Maghiel slipping away down the road — narrate this
to their player directly, no check required. She's Hasted; pursuit is hopeless (this is the
hard rail from the GM-only truth above — see her card's "when things go bad" line).
**Your move:** if anyone chases Maghiel, describe her impossible speed and let it fail
narratively — don't roll a chase you can't let them win. Running down her escort instead is
a real, winnable fight if the party commits to it. **If her crew is already losing when she
goes,** she doesn't run alone: she teleports out with whichever of Gotrin, Tolovin and Kyorgar
are standing near her. Anyone downed, grappled or cut off is left behind for the party to take. Around now, Tsendur's husband **Troilan** appears outside, panicked, pleading for someone to go in after Tsendur.
**Your move:** if no one's already moving toward Tsendur, use the husband's plea as the nudge.

**Round 4+ — The rescue.** Combat and exploration of the house continue: the goal is
reaching Tsendur (upstairs, per the source's floor plan) and any recoverable artifacts
before the zombies/fire finish him. He has some magical defenses of his own (Mage Armor,
Aggressive) but they're already spent or failing by the time the party gets there.
**Your move:** track rounds from first entry to reaching Tsendur and apply the source's
condition table (p.10) — the longer it takes, the worse his HP/exhaustion/burns.

- **Slam** +3 to hit, reach 5 ft: 1d6+1 bludgeoning + 1d4 fire.
- **Scorching touch (passive):** anyone who hits it in melee, or touches it, while within

**Reference cards**

**Maghiel the Shunned** — drow wizard, CR 10 — *the rail, not a fair fight*
- Default attack: **Light Crossbow +3**, +7 to hit, 1d8+4 piercing. She'd rather cast.
- **Sign of the Juggernaut (action, assume already on):** resistant to nonmagical
  bludgeoning/piercing/slashing for 10 min — nonmagical weapons do half.
- **Spell Absorption (reaction, ioun stone):** no-sells one spell of 4th level or lower
  targeting only her, no save needed by the caster — she has 20 spell-levels banked in the
  stone before it burns out.
- **Battlefield Magic:** when her Evocation spell (Fireball, etc.) hits multiple creatures,
  she can auto-succeed + zero-damage a number of them equal to 1 + the spell's level —
  expect her own escort to walk out of her Fireball unscathed even if they're in the blast.
- **Battlemage's Fortitude:** +3 (beyond proficiency) to Concentration saves — hard to knock
  her spell off with damage.
- **Fey Ancestry:** advantage vs. charm, can't be magically put to sleep.
- **Tactics, in order:** (1) Slow the party's front line, (2) Haste on Gotrin, (3) Fireball,
  (4) Chill Wind if nothing's on fire yet, (5) Animate Dead, (6) Adamantine Undead.
  **Reaction: Counterspell.** **When it goes bad: Haste on herself + Darkness, then gone** —
  this is the hard rail (GM-only truth, above). **If her crew is losing, she teleports out with
  whichever of them are standing near her**; anyone downed, grappled or cut off stays. Never run her to be beaten, only survived —
  Gotrin/Tolovin/Kyorgar and the escorts are the actual fight.

**Gotrin** — drow captain, Monk 3 (Way of Shadow) — *scaled down from the source's Monk 6 to
stay near the party's level (crew rule, above); he levels up as they do*
- Default attack: **Shortsword** +6 to hit, 1d6+4 piercing.
- **Martial Arts (bonus action):** one unarmed strike, +6 to hit, 1d4+4 bludgeoning. Roll the
  tracker row again and treat it as a d4.
- **Flurry of Blows (1 ki, bonus action):** two unarmed strikes instead of one. He has **3 ki**
  and spends them fast.
- **Shadow Arts (2 ki):** casts *Darkness*, *Silence* or *Pass without Trace*. He fights inside
  his own darkness, and drow see in it better than most of the party.
- **Deflect Missiles (reaction):** reduces one ranged weapon hit by 1d10+7. Ranged attackers
  underperform against him.
- **Not yet:** no Extra Attack, no Stunning Strike, no Shadow Step. Those come at Monk 5 and 6,
  when the party meets him again.
- If Maghiel **Hastes** him, he gets one extra weapon attack that round.
- **Bodyguard.** If Maghiel teleports out, he is the one most likely to be standing next to her.

**Tolovin** — drow assassin, Rogue 3 (Assassin)
- Default attack: **Rapier** +5 to hit, 1d8+3 piercing. Alt: **Shortbow** +5, 1d6+3
  (range 80/320).
- **Sneak Attack (+2d6, once/turn):** add it to any hit made with advantage, or when an ally
  of his is adjacent to the target and he doesn't have disadvantage — don't bake it into the
  row, add it by hand when it applies.
