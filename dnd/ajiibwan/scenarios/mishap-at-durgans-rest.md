> ## ⚑ Session 7 opens here — the kobold traders on the mountain trail
>
> **Where they are.** Day 2 out of Tel A'bib, on the trail up into the mountains below Durgan's
> Rest. It is late in the day and they have not made camp. Session 6 ended as a **friendly troupe
> of kobolds with a wagon of junk** surprised them on the trail.
>
> **What they carry now** (`campaign/inventory.yaml`):
> - 52 gp, from the merchants' collection at Elmingwed's banquet.
> - Studded leather on Ren and Lia.
> - Elmingwed's sealed letter, still undelivered. Tath'Shar has it.
> - The obsidian serpent ring, the unidentified spider bracelet, and the three Bokkaran scrolls.
>
> **Who is with them.** Jiyloo is now a full party member, hired by the party itself for 1/5 of
> the loot. Ren carries the Keeper's blessing: a faint path north that only she can see, toward the
> desert pyramid the Black Water Cult has made its eastern outpost.
>
> **Level.** The party has **earned level 3 but has not taken it.** They level up when they reach
> Tsendur's: the night they sleep at Durgan's Rest, before the letter and the raid. The crew stat
> blocks below already fit level 3.
>
> **Tath'Shar.** Phoenix is expected back. Last session Steve ran Tath'Shar as an NPC, and they did
> much of the talking with Elmingwed and the merchants, so catch Phoenix up on that. The Keeper's
> hunger never made its private offer to Tath'Shar. If you want it, use the packet's "Hold it"
> fallback: a dream at the trail camp tonight, with the Scepter line (*"The one you are looking for
> collects things like the thing in my hands. She will come for it."*) and *"Come back and open my
> door."* It lands well the night before Maghiel shows up.
>
> ### Read-aloud, to restart the scene
>
> *The trail has been climbing all afternoon, switchbacks of loose shale with the desert going
> blue and hazy below you. Round the last bend comes a racket of tin and wood: a handcart-wagon
> twice too big for the six small figures hauling and pushing it, piled higher than any of them
> with pots, harness, bottles, boots and a birdcage. Scaled snouts, bright eyes, and a lot of
> waving. "Trade! Trade! Good trade, tall-folk, before dark!"*
>
> ### The troupe
>
> - **Six kobolds, led by Grizzik.** She is old, missing half her tail, and wears a dozen keys
>   on a string. She does the talking and the pricing. The others unpack, hold things up, and
>   argue with her in Draconic.
> - **They trade up and down this trail** between the hill farms, the mines, and Durgan's Rest. In
>   game 1 a party traded a Bokkaran book with kobolds on this road; this is that troupe.
> - **They are friendly and they are cowards.** A drawn weapon scatters them under the wagon. Harm
>   one and the rest flee up the trail, and Durgan's Rest hears about it before the party arrives.
>   That costs the party Sheriff Isi's goodwill. The kobolds trade with the town and everyone knows
>   them.
> - **Stats, if you need them:** standard 5e kobolds, AC 12, HP 5, dagger +4 for 1d4+2, Pack
>   Tactics. They should not need them.
> - **Names, so they stick when the troupe comes back:**
>   - **Pip** is the youngest, cheerful, and always tugging a sleeve. The Black Water Cult took
>     his brother **Nib**.
>   - **Tamm** is the appraiser. He bites coins, sniffs bottles, and blurts out what things are
>     really worth, which drives Grizzik mad.
>   - **Sket** is the scout. She walks ahead of the wagon and hears everything, so most of the
>     troupe's news comes through her.
>   - **Ob and Ib** are twins. They haul the wagon, speak no Common, and fight over the birdcage.
>
> ### A troupe that keeps turning up
>
> Play this troupe as a **recurring contact**, not a one-off encounter (`thread:grizziks-wagon`).
> - **They run a circuit.** The route goes from the foothill farms below Tel A'bib, up to Durgan's
>   Rest, out to the mines, over the Haunting Range pass toward the Bridged City, and back again.
>   Whenever the party is on a mountain road, the wagon can come round the bend. Use them every few
>   weeks of in-world time, or whenever the table needs a breather between hard scenes.
> - **Every visit brings three things:** something new on the wagon, something they want to buy,
>   and one piece of news.
> - **Why they know things.** Kobolds go everywhere and sell to everyone, and nobody lowers their
>   voice in front of a kobold. Bandits, cultists, drow, farmers and the town watch all say things
>   over the troupe's heads that they would never say to a stranger. That makes the troupe the
>   cheapest spy network in the mountains, for a party that treats them well.
> - **Their opinion of the party moves in three steps.**
>   - **Strangers** (where they start): fair prices, the wagon goods only, and the free news below.
>   - **Good customers:** earned by a fair deal, a gift, or a small kindness, like fixing the wagon
>     wheel, sharing the fire, or admiring Grizzik's keys. Grizzik opens the box under the wagon bed
>     (below) and starts trading news.
>   - **Friends of the wagon:** earned by a real favour, such as carrying the parcel to Tsendur or
>     bringing Nib home. They come looking for the party to warn them, hold good items back for
>     them, and vouch for them in Durgan's Rest.
>   - **Their opinion drops** if the party bullies them, cheats them or draws steel on them. A
>     cheated troupe still comes back, but it sells the party junk at full price and keeps its news.
>
> ### How they trade
>
> - **They prefer barter to coin.** Gold is fine, but shiny, noisy or strange things get a better
>   price than their weight in gold.
> - **They will not touch the obsidian serpent ring.** If anyone shows it, the troupe goes quiet and
>   Grizzik pushes it back with a stick. *"Black-water people. Bad people. Took Pip's brother for
>   digging."* (The cult takes kobold labour too.)
>
> ### What they'll buy from the party
>
> - **Their tastes.** Keys first: Grizzik collects them, pays double for any key, and throws in a
>   piece of news. After keys come bottles, bells, mirrors, anything that rattles or shines, anything
>   with writing on it, and boots of any size.
> - **What they pay.** Half of book value in coin, or full value in trade goods. They are short of
>   coin and say so.
> - **The Bokkaran scrolls are what they want most.** Grizzik's opening offer is **all three scrolls
>   for the "goat medicine" potion plus the climbing kit**, or 10 gp each in coin. Haggled hard, she
>   goes to 15 gp each.
>   - **Let the party discover that this is a bad deal.** If anyone asks who buys white-metal writing,
>     or makes a DC 10 Insight check while she haggles, Tamm blurts it out: *"Bearded scribe pays
>     fifty! Each!"* Grizzik smacks him with her tail stump.
>   - Fifty each is kobold rumour, not a price Tsendur has promised. The real point is that every
>     scrap of Bokkaran is precious to the scholars who study it, and Tsendur is the man who can
>     tell the party roughly what these three are. He can't make them castable, and he can't teach
>     the language (see *What Tsendur can do for them*, below). Still, a scroll in hand is a
>     bargaining chip with him, and a future spell once someone in the party can read Bokkaran.
>   - **If the party keeps the scrolls,** Grizzik isn't offended. She offers a different deal: the
>     parcel, below.
> - **The spider bracelet.** If anyone shows it, Grizzik offers anything on the wagon for it. Tamm
>   sniffs it and says *"Water-charm. Old water."* That line is the hint that it matters. It pays off
>   at the Sea of Souls, so the party should not sell it, and Tamm's remark gives them the reason.
>
> ### The parcel for the scribe
>
> Grizzik has a parcel for Tsendur: a bundle of good lamp wicks and a sealed packet of ink powder he
> ordered from the farms. The troupe turns off toward the mines next, so without help the parcel
> waits a month. **She asks the party to carry it, and pays in news:** the false toll, below.
> - Tsendur pays the party 5 gp when they deliver it, and he remembers who brought it. That gives
>   him a warm first moment with the party, before the letter goes wrong.
> - This is the easy way to become **good customers**.
>
> ### The wagon — what's for sale
>
> Prices are Grizzik's opening ask. She comes down a third for a good haggle, and more for a good
> swap.
> - **Climbing kit:** pitons, hammer, 50 ft of knotted rope. 15 gp. Useful at Durgan's Rest.
> - **Two fleece-lined cloaks,** cold-weather. 5 gp each. The mountain nights are cold, and the
>   party is dressed for the desert (Gereon especially).
> - **A clay bottle labelled "goat medicine."** It is a real *potion of healing*. 30 gp. A DC 12
>   Medicine or Arcana check tells what it is.
> - **A jar of fire-beetle oil.** It burns bright and long, and it is what Temple al Sia's Ember
>   burns. 3 gp. A small nod for anyone who tended the flame.
> - **A dented brass plate, stamped with gear-marks.** The kobolds say they found it below a "metal
>   man who walked wrong and fell apart" in the foothills. It is a Kantaron construct's plate. This
>   is Ren's old construct hook. 2 gp, or a swap for anything that rattles.
> - **A broken drow hand crossbow,** fine black wood and silver wire. Grizzik bought it three
>   nights ago from **"tall dark elves, no fire, camping above the pass."** That is Maghiel's crew,
>   shadowing the courier. 8 gp. **This is the Maghiel's-shadow beat.** If Tath'Shar is at the
>   table, they recognise the House style. If not, it waits in the pack for Phoenix.
> - **A cracked clay pyramid token,** the kind pilgrims buy at Doornian shrines. 1 gp. *Optional:*
>   when Ren holds it, her faint path north flickers brighter for a moment.
> - **Pure junk,** for colour and haggling: one good boot, a birdcage with no door, a tin whistle,
>   a bag of mismatched dice, a cracked hand mirror, a harness with no animal.
>
> ### The box under the wagon bed — for good customers only
>
> These items are cheap, odd and hard to find anywhere else. None of them is valuable, and each is
> useful at least once.
> - **A pair of fire-beetle glands.** Each glows like a torch (bright light 10 ft, dim light 10 ft
>   more) for 1d6 days, with no flame and no smoke. 1 gp each. Nobody in the desert sells them.
>   A light that gives off no fire is worth having with drow camped above the pass.
> - **Waking salts.** A twist of the salts kobold miners sniff in bad air. Held under a sleeper's
>   nose (one action), they wake a creature from magical sleep, such as a *sleep* spell. Three uses,
>   4 gp. They can't beat the Sea of Souls while the sleeper is still in the water's reach, but once
>   someone drags the sleeper clear, the salts wake them at once.
> - **A charcoal rubbing of a Bokkaran inscription,** taken from a boundary stone near the mines.
>   2 gp. It is useless to the party, because nobody can read it. To Tsendur it is a new sample, and
>   new samples are rare and jealously guarded. That makes it a good gift to open a conversation
>   with him, or something to trade him for what he knows.
> - **Boot spikes, two pairs.** Iron spikes that strap onto boots. They give advantage on checks to
>   keep footing on ice and wet rock. 3 gp a pair. Useful on the Haunting Range pass and on the goat
>   track (below).
> - **A jar of "dragon pepper."** Kobolds burn it to drive giant insects out of tunnels. Lit, it
>   fills a 10 ft area with smoke, and any beast with Intelligence 2 or lower must leave the area
>   unless it passes a DC 10 Constitution save. 5 gp.
> - **A bronze toll chit and a cork-stamped receipt.** These go with the false toll, below.
>
> ### Trading in news
>
> Grizzik sells news the way she sells boots: everything has a price, and the price is rarely coin.
> A key buys news. So do a good story, a fair deal, a song, or carrying the parcel. Open with the
> free items so the party sees that the troupe knows things, then make them pay for the good ones.
>
> **Free, to anyone who buys something:**
> - **Tsendur sees visitors in the morning, never at night.** "Busy man. Lamps all night."
> - **The dark elves above the pass.** They camp without a fire, pay in silver, and ask questions
>   about who goes up the trail.
> - **Durgan's Rest is a day's walk,** and "the field across from the scribe's house is where
>   travellers sleep." That is where the raid will find them.
>
> **For a price:**
> - **The false toll on the road to the Bridged City.** This is the big one, and it gets its own
>   section below.
> - **Where Nib went.** Black Water men took Pip's brother "from the mine road, two moons back,"
>   along with four other kobolds, "to dig in the sand north of the water-town." The water-town is
>   Sia. This confirms what the Keeper told Ren: the cult is at the pyramid, and it is digging for
>   something. It also means there are kobold prisoners inside who will help anyone who comes to
>   free them.
>   - If the party promises to look for Nib, Pip gives them his tin whistle. Nib has its twin, and
>     both play the same note.
>   - Making that promise and keeping it makes the party **friends of the wagon.** It also gives
>     Ren's charge to investigate the pyramid a small, personal face.
> - **The metal man who walked wrong.** Sket can say where the brass plate came from: a gully half a
>   day off the trail, east of the pass. There were two metal men. The second one still walks at
>   night, one eye lit like a lantern, picking up stones. This is a Kantaron construct gone astray,
>   and Ren's construct hook.
> - **"Don't drink from the marsh where the moon cries."** This is about somewhere northeast of
>   Durgan's Rest, on the east side of the mountains. *"Water looks fine. Moon in it cries. Kobolds
>   who drink it walk off in the night."* It's only a rumour for now, pointing at the Covent of the
>   Weeping Moon for later.
>
> ### The false toll — what the kobolds know
>
> **The setup.** On the road from Durgan's Rest over the Haunting Range to the Bridged City, a
> "customs post" has gone up at a narrow stone bridge over a gorge. It has a striped barrier pole, a
> hut, an Imperial flag, and eight men in Imperial tabards. Their leader calls himself
> **Under-Collector Hesk**. They charge a "bridge tax" of 10 gp a head. They also "inspect" packs
> for "contraband antiquities," which means anything Bokkaran gets confiscated. They took Grizzik's
> best copper pot as her toll.
>
> **The party won't reach this pass until after Durgan's Rest,** when Tsendur sends them on to Dain
> Ironheart and the Bridged City. Plant the news now, and it pays off a session or two later. If the
> party doesn't buy it now, the troupe can turn up in the Durgan's Rest market the morning after the
> fire.
>
> **How the kobolds know it's a fake** (each detail is one piece of news; sell them one at a time
> or all together):
> - **The Empire doesn't reach that road.** The Bridged City is cut off from the Empire by the
>   Dangerous Road and the river blockade. No real Imperial officer has come up that road in years.
>   A PC can work this out alone with a DC 12 History check. The kobolds just say it plainly.
> - **The tabards are stolen.** They came off an Imperial courier party that went missing on the
>   Dangerous Road. One has a crossbow-bolt hole in the back, darned in the wrong colour thread.
> - **The seal is a cork stamp.** The paper receipt Hesk hands out is stamped with the mark of a
>   Tel A'bib wine merchant. Anyone who drank at Elmingwed's banquet has seen that mark on the
>   bottles. Grizzik kept her receipt and will sell it for 1 gp, or give it to good customers.
> - **A real toll gives bronze, not paper.** A genuine Imperial toll is paid against a bronze chit
>   with a square hole in it. Grizzik has one from a real post on the northern road, and will trade
>   it.
> - **They are the old pass bandits.** Sket recognised Hesk. He is the same man who used to rob
>   caravans on that pass with a sack over his head. Robbing under a flag is safer, because most
>   travellers simply pay.
>
> **How the party can use the news:**
> - **Refuse to pay, and call the bluff.** With two or more of the details above, a DC 10 Persuasion
>   or Intimidation check makes Hesk decide that these travellers know too much and carry too many
>   weapons. He "waives the tax" and waves them through. With only one detail, the DC is 15. These
>   men are robbers, not soldiers, and they don't want a real fight with five adventurers and a drow.
> - **Go round.** The kobolds know a goat track that climbs above the gorge and comes back down to
>   the road a mile further on. It costs half a day and a DC 12 Athletics check per PC; the climbing
>   kit or boot spikes give advantage. Grizzik sells the route, scratched on a scrap of hide, for
>   3 gp.
> - **Turn them in.** The Imperial Guard post in the Bridged City will pay to learn who has been
>   wearing its tabards. That means a reward and a friendly first contact in a city that admits
>   nobody without an introduction.
> - **Fight.** Always possible. At level 3 the party wins, but it costs them hit points and spells
>   they will want at the Bridged City.
> - **Without the news,** the party probably pays. Then "inspection" takes whatever Bokkaran items
>   they carry, including the spider bracelet if it's in a pack and not on a wrist. That is the price
>   of not making friends with the wagon, and it gives them a reason to go after Hesk later.
>

> unchanged.

> - **Under-Collector Hesk (bandit captain).** Makes **three attacks a turn**: two with his scimitar
>   and one with his dagger (the row is the scimitar; the dagger is 1d4+3). **Parry:** once a round,

>   can turn a hit into a miss. He surrenders when he drops below half HP. His men break and run
>   when he does.
> - **Toll bandits.** One scimitar attack a turn. Two carry light crossbows instead (+3, 1d8+1,
>   range 80 ft) and stay behind the barrier pole. They flee once half of them are down.
>
> ### Playing the scene for interest
>
> - **Make haggling a scene, not a roll.** Let the players pitch why an item is worth more: its story,
>   who carried it, what it did. A good story moves Grizzik down a third. A great one makes Tamm
>   want the item more than she does, and the two argue about it in Draconic. Roll only when the
>   table wants to.
> - **Let the kobolds argue out loud.** They squabble in Draconic about prices and about the party.
>   A PC who speaks Draconic hears the real prices and the real gossip, so check character sheets.
>   If nobody does, Pip translates, badly and honestly.
> - **Grizzik's keys.** She has a dozen, and a story for each one. If anyone asks, she tells one,
>   and it ends on something useful: *"This one from a black-water man who drowned in the sand. Door
>   it opened is still out there."* Asking about her keys is the fastest way into her good books.
> - **One interruption.** Mid-haggle, Sket comes running down from upslope. Either it's the drow
>   crossbow moment, or simply *"Tall-folk with no fire, watching from the rocks."* That turns a
>   shopping scene into a warning without starting a fight.
> - **End with a gift.** As the wagon rattles off, Pip runs back with something small and free:
>   the cracked pyramid token if nobody bought it, or the tin whistle. It tells the players these
>   kobolds will be back.
>

> ## ⚑ Coming here from Sia — prep for the session after the tomb
>
> **Where the party is coming from.** Out of the Tomb of the Sphinx at the Oasis of Sia
> (sessions 4–6, `scenarios/ashes-at-the-sphinx.md`). Five PCs at level 2 or 3, plus Jiyloo and
> Orkideh's dog. They carry Elmingwed's sealed letter (Tath'Shar has it), the obsidian serpent ring,
> the unidentified white-metal spider bracelet, three unreadable Bokkaran scrolls, and whatever
> they brought out of 9p.
>
> **Why they are coming.** In session 4 Tath'Shar argued for Tsendur first, lost the vote to Sia,
> and accepted it. The party promised that Tsendur comes next.
>
> **The route.** Sia to Tel A'bib, then up into the mountains to Durgan's Rest. On the Roll20 hex
> map that is hex 1213 back to Tel A'bib, then to hex 2012. In game 1 the trip took several days
> (the party was there on days 10–14) and included trading a Bokkard book with kobolds on the way.
>
> **The letter's delay — decided.** `thread:tsendur-letter` asked whether weeks in a pocket helped
> or hurt Tsendur. **Neither.** Maghiel is not hunting Tsendur. She is following the courier, to
> learn what the sealed letters say (timing fork A). So the raid comes on the night the letter is
> delivered, whenever that is. It also means she has been shadowing the party all along,
> including at Sia. Show that once on the road (below).
>
> ### The plan: arrive, sleep one night, stop
>
> Get the party to Durgan's Rest and let them **sleep there one night and get comfortable.** The
> session will probably end by then. **Phoenix is back for the next one, and Tath'Shar delivers the
> letter themselves.**
>
> - **They arrive late in the day.** Troilan, Tsendur's husband, meets them at the gate: the master
>   is deep in his work and will see visitors in the morning. The party camps in the travellers'
>   field across the road, which is exactly where the raid will find them later.
> - **Nothing happens the first night.** Maghiel is following the courier, and the courier hasn't
>   delivered anything yet. Let them be comfortable. That is what the fire takes away.
> - **Use the evening to make the place matter.** The town: Sheriff Isi, the farmer-merchants, the
>   watering hole (source PDF). Tsendur's dwarf assistants Kada and Kayta, carrying supplies up to the
>   house. Lamplight in the study window late into the night. The players should know the house and the
>   people before they have to go into it on fire.
> - **Tath'Shar** is a quiet presence and keeps the letter. If the session runs long and someone wants
>   to push the letter through without them, Troilan's answer holds: morning.
>
> ### The audience with Elmingwed — the negotiation
>
> *Played in session 6 (2026-10-24): the party handed over the captive monk and took public credit
> with the merchants instead of a fee. Elmingwed threw a banquet, passed a collection plate, and
> gave Ren and Lia studded leather. Kept here for reference.*
>
> This happens in Tel A'bib on the way, unless the party goes into the desert after the Keeper's
> reward instead. Keep the stop to a day or two. The usual six weeks of downtime belongs after
> Durgan's Rest: the party is in the middle of a promise.
>
> **1 — The road first.** Restoring the transit lane is the thing he actually wanted. He opens with a
> merchant's questions and listens hard to the answers:
> - Is the water clean?
> - Is the cult gone, or coming back, and when?
> - Who holds the oasis tonight?
> - Can a caravan leave next week, and what will it find when it gets there?
>
> Honest, specific answers set a good mood for everything after. Vague ones make him ask again,
> politely, until they are specific.
>
> **2 — The monk.** The party hands over the captive from the sphinx. **To Elmingwed this is the
> treasure.** A living member of the Sia raid gives him proof, for the Governor and the merchant
> houses, that the Black Water Cult raids within reach of Tel A'bib. It also gives him names, and
> where the column went. Abet's militia takes the prisoner. If the party asks, Elmingwed shares what
> Abet gets out of him, which is a feed of intelligence toward Ayatorrah.
>
> **3 — The treasure they left.** He asks what they brought out of the tomb, and it is a test.
> - **Nothing, or nothing but the ring:** he is pleased, and says why. *"Anyone can carry gold out of
>   a hole. It takes judgment to leave it there."* A robbed tomb makes a cursed oasis and hostile
>   priests, and caravans stop where the dead are quiet. **They saw the bigger picture**, which is the
>   quality he has been testing for since they arrived at his gate with Jeff.
> - **Something the Keeper gave them freely:** he respects a gift, and wants to see it.
> - **Something taken:** he cools, and asks pointed questions about who else knows.
>
> **4 — What he gives: cashing the reputation.** They chose standing over coin, so make them name
> what they want. He grants what is reasonable. Things they could ask for:
> - **Public credit.** He says it in front of the merchant houses: the party reopened Sia and took no
>   fee. Feltan's standing reward for the caravans gets paid, and Elmingwed sees to it.
> - **Outfitting for the mountains.** Gear at cost from Al-Esellin (tents, cold-weather kit), mounts,
>   and supplies.
> - **Identification.** He knows the obsidian ring on sight: a Black Water token, and wearing it in
>   Tel A'bib will get you killed. The spider bracelet is Bokkaran white metal and beyond him; that is
>   Tsendur's field.
> - **Abet's militia to hold Sia** until the caravans are running. It is a real cost to him. He
>   agrees if the party makes the bigger-picture argument: guards at the well protect every caravan
>   he has on that road.
> - **Help finding a priest for Temple al Sia,** if the party made a covenant with the Keeper.
>   Elmingwed trades with Doornian pilgrims and knows who to ask.
>
> What he **won't** do is pay them for the job. Coin now would make them hirelings, and he would
> consider it an insult to them. If they haggle for gold, he pays it without argument and quietly

## GM-ONLY TRUTH — read this before you read anything else

This session is not Maghiel's first move. **It's her origin.** Do not let this leak to the
players until it's earned.

- **Maghiel is not Thorn-cursed yet.** In this reset timeline, that hasn't happened. She is
  simply a capable, ambitious drow wizard, and right now she is looking to advance her ambition to move up in her family's power structure by finding riches, treasure and potentially ancient magic to extend her wizard studies — Tath'Shar isn't wrong that Maghiel might yet be findable and reachable. That window closes tonight.
- **The Clasp of Vergren is not what Tsendur thinks it is.** His family lore (Mesos, Enkili, 
  the prank, the Divine War — see the source PDF's Background) is real as far as it goes,
  but incomplete, possibly even a cover story laid over the real thing. **The Clasp is (or
  contains) a splinter of the Black Thorn** — a corruption vector broken off the world-tree
  El Faiz ([[artifact:al-faiz-tree]], thread:the-thorn). Drendari's Deedbox wasn't just
  hiding a relic from scrying; it was **sealing away a corruption artifact**, which is
  exactly why generations of Tsendur's family were told never to open it. Tsendur has no
  idea. He thinks he's protecting a wizard's trinket.
- **Maghiel steals the Clasp and studies it.** Away from Durgan's Rest, alone with it, she
  is pierced by the Thorn — this is the actual moment `npc:maghiel`'s prior-campaign
  "Black-Thorn-cursed, only beginning her path" status comes into being. **It happens after
  this session, off-screen**, the seed of everything downstream (Bridged City, Crystal
  Towers, the eventual Al-Faiz Springs confrontation) growing from a scene the party stood
  in and couldn't stop.
- **This is a hard rail, and that's deliberate.** Maghiel must escape with the Clasp. The
  party can fight, capture, or kill as many of her escort as they like — the source material
  already hands you this exactly: seven cloaked figures go in, one elven woman comes out
  alone. Let that stand. Everything else about this session is open; this one beat isn't.
  If it helps, the in-fiction reason is simple and needs no more engineering than the source
  already gives you: she has a prepared escape (a scroll, a bonded item, a plane-touched
  trick — your call) that the party's timing doesn't allow them to counter. Don't telegraph
  it as unfair; telegraph it as *she was already leaving when the fighting started.*
- **The party is complicit, and that's the point.** Elmingwed's letter (see below) is what
  put Tath'Shar in front of Tsendur with a warning about "prohibited artifacts" — and it's
  entirely fair to let the players wonder, later, whether that letter (or Tath'Shar's own
  inquiries along the way) is what put Maghiel on Durgan's Rest's map in the first place.
  You don't have to resolve which it was. The responsibility to stop what happens next
  belongs to the whole table, not just Tath'Shar's player, because the whole party carried
  the message that (maybe) brought her here.

## The letter

Give the players the actual text, or close to it, when Tath'Shar presents it to Tsendur —
this is not a MacGuffin, it's information that matters:

> *Tsendur — the bearer is Tath'Shar, of family Tath, searching for a sister
> they have reason to think is active in this region: a drow, newly wizarded, dangerous, 
> driven, and — I am reliably told — hunting artifacts that ought to stay buried. 
> Guard your artifacts and guard yourself. Warn your scholarly friends.
> I would take it as a personal favor if you gave them what help you can. 
> — Elmingwed*

**Why Elmingwed knows this much:** Tath'Shar has brought him sealed letters which they cannot read without breaking seals. Elmingwed reads them and is in some way obligated to the bearer of the letters as a representative of the sender.

**The dramatic irony:** Tsendur reads a warning about someone hunting a prohibited artifact
while standing in the room next to one, with no idea it's the same object. If a player
makes that connection before Tsendur does, let them try to act on it — that's the session
working.

**A. Timed to arrival (recommended).** The explosion happens the same day the party walks

## Scene beats

**1 — Arrival & the letter.** Durgan's Rest is a small, cosmopolitan farming market town at
the crest of the Kelder Mountains (full stats/NPCs: source PDF, pop. 220, Sheriff Isi,
farmer-merchants). Tath'Shar presents Elmingwed's letter to Tsendur. Play this scene for
what it is: a stranger asking an old scholar for help finding a woman he's never heard of,
carrying a warning that means nothing to him yet.

**2 — The Blast.** A thunderous boom rises from Tsendur's homestead at the north edge of
town — moments after the letter, or the next morning, your call.

**3 — The Burning Homestead.** Full room-by-room detail, fire-spread mechanics, and
minute-by-minute maps are in the source (pages 7-10) — use them directly. **Scorch
zombies** (stat block, page 13) are the threat; a bystander named Jasper gets grabbed and
needs rescuing. Notable loot: a *wand of magic missiles*, a spare copy of Tsendur's
spellbook, and a couple of comedic minor-magic bathroom fixtures (a self-heating washtub, a
self-cleaning commode) — good comic relief in an otherwise tense scene. **Seven cloaked
figures** (Maghiel + six escorts) hit the house hard and fast, ahead of the party — the
scorch zombies are what's left doing damage control, not Maghiel's party itself.

**4 — Tsendur's Account.** If he survives (condition table, source page 10 — his HP/
exhaustion depends on how many rounds the party took to reach him), Tsendur explains: an
elven woman he'd glimpsed once before, at Bridged City's Academy, tortured the location of
the Clasp out of him and fled into the woods alone — pursuit fails automatically (see
GM-ONLY TRUTH above; this is the hard rail). **Play the reveal without naming her.** Let the
description — elven, robed, moved with terrible speed and purpose — be the only clue on the
table. If Tath'Shar's player wants a personal gut-punch, let them notice one specific detail
nobody else would clock as the moment they privately realize it's their sister, without
saying so aloud yet. If the party fought and captured or killed any of the six escorts, they can be
interrogated/looted for a name, a faction hint, or nothing at all — your call on how much to
give away this early.

**5 — Aftermath & the hook onward.** **Tsendur makes the introductions, not Elmingwed.**
He sends the party to his friend **Dain Ironheart**, a dwarf forge-cleric and explosives expert.
Dain is an NPC in the *Scholarly Schism* module. In game 1, Tsendur gave the party letters of
introduction to Dain and to **Savila** (`npc:savila`, head of the College); give them the Savila
letter as well if you like.

What waits in the Bridged City:
- **The College of Engineering and Magic is closed, and its main tower is sealed because of
  contamination.** In this run **the seal is Maghiel's.** That replaces the module's version, where
  the faculty sealed the Academy themselves against wild magic.
- **Breaking the seal takes Dain's help.** He brings his barrel of Bokkaran-inscribed powder on a
  cart. It is the module's breach scene (VoS-2, chapter two), and game 1's notes have Dain arguing
  his barrel through the gate.
- **Below the tower:** the caverns down to the **Sea of Souls**, the ancient lizard **Yormon
  Therdasiroth**, and a **second language engine**. That one does 10d6+10 psychic damage to whoever
  touches it, and leaves a lasting mark (`assets/_raw/Bridged City.../Bridged City Lore.md`).
- **The spider bracelet** from the strongbox (session 3) finally pays off there, twice over:
  - It wards the wearer against the Sea of Souls' sleep.
  - It halves the engine's damage, for one wearer per bracelet at that engine. A bracelet already used
    at the Keeper's engine in Sia still works at Yormon's, because the limit is per engine.

Let this scene also plant **Tsendur as a standing contact**. He is grateful now, guilty once he
learns what he was really protecting, and a door to other Bokkaran-magic scholars. The arc is
`world/adventures.md` → *Bridged City — Scholarly Schism*, with Maghiel's crew (Gotrin, Tolovin and
Kyorgar, levelled to match the party).

## Combat reference — the fire

*Stat block conventions (tracker table + reference card, single-die damage encoding, and the

### Round-by-round GM guide — the ambush

Timing fork A (recommended, per above): the party is camped across the road from Tsendur's
homestead, in the open field travelers use for tents. Maghiel's crew hits the house **on the
night the letter is delivered**, not the party's first night in town (see *The plan*, above).

**Setup — 3 AM.** A flash and a boom from the house. Anyone on watch is not surprised;
everyone else is surprised for round 1.
**Your move:** roll/assign watch order before this if you haven't already; don't let the
party choose who's "on watch" retroactively.

**Round 1 — Breach.** Maghiel's escort is already inside, having blown a hole in the wall
moments before — they're not arriving, they're already fighting their way to Tsendur.
**Your move:** run the surprise round for everyone not on watch, then drop Gotrin, Tolovin,
Kyorgar, and the three drow mercenaries into the breach (A1/A4-ish, GM's call on exact
placement) already engaged. Tolovin's Assassinate (see his card) makes this round nasty for
anyone still surprised.

**Round 2 — Fire and zombies.** The fire spreads while the party fights and tries to push
into the building; the scorch zombies (above) start appearing as the party breaches rooms.
**Your move:** introduce zombies as the party enters A1/A4, not all at once — and start
narrating the fire as a ticking hazard (see the source's room-by-room fire rules, p.7-10),
not just set dressing.

**Round 3 — The getaway.** Tath'Shar sees Maghiel slipping away down the road — narrate this
to their player directly, no check required. She's Hasted; pursuit is hopeless (this is the
hard rail from the GM-only truth above — see her card's "when things go bad" line).
**Your move:** if anyone chases Maghiel, describe her impossible speed and let it fail
narratively — don't roll a chase you can't let them win. Running down her escort instead is
a real, winnable fight if the party commits to it. **If her crew is already losing when she
goes,** she doesn't run alone: she teleports out with whichever of Gotrin, Tolovin and Kyorgar
are standing near her. Anyone downed, grappled or cut off is left behind for the party to take. Around now, Tsendur's husband **Troilan** appears outside, panicked, pleading for someone to go in after Tsendur.
**Your move:** if no one's already moving toward Tsendur, use the husband's plea as the nudge.

**Round 4+ — The rescue.** Combat and exploration of the house continue: the goal is
reaching Tsendur (upstairs, per the source's floor plan) and any recoverable artifacts
before the zombies/fire finish him. He has some magical defenses of his own (Mage Armor,
Aggressive) but they're already spent or failing by the time the party gets there.
**Your move:** track rounds from first entry to reaching Tsendur and apply the source's
condition table (p.10) — the longer it takes, the worse his HP/exhaustion/burns.

- Default attack: **Shortsword** +6 to hit, 1d6+4 piercing.

- **Flurry of Blows (1 ki, bonus action):** two unarmed strikes instead of one. He has **3 ki**

- **Shadow Arts (2 ki):** casts *Darkness*, *Silence* or *Pass without Trace*. He fights inside

**Tolovin** — drow assassin, Rogue 3 (Assassin)
- Default attack: **Rapier** +5 to hit, 1d8+3 piercing. Alt: **Shortbow** +5, 1d6+3
  (range 80/320).

  of his is adjacent to the target and he doesn't have disadvantage — don't bake it into the
  row, add it by hand when it applies.
- **Assassinate:** any hit he lands against a creature that hasn't acted yet this combat is
  an automatic critical — this is what makes round 1's surprise round genuinely dangerous.
- **Cunning Action (bonus action):** Dash/Disengage/Hide — he ducks out rather than trades
  blows once he's bloodied.

**Kyorgar** — drow war-priest, Cleric 3 (War Domain)
- Default attack: **Warhammer** +4 to hit, 1d8+2 bludgeoning. Alt: **Light Crossbow** +2,

- **War Priest (limited uses/day):** on making a weapon attack, he can make one more as a
  bonus action — an occasional second roll of the tracker row, not automatic every round.
- **Sacred Flame (cantrip, ranged):** target DC 12 DEX save or 1d8 radiant — his ranged poke.
- **Prepared spells:** Bless, Shield of Faith, Divine Favor, Cure Wounds (1st); Magic
  Weapon, Spiritual Weapon, Hold Person, Enhance Ability, Lesser Restoration (2nd) — expect
  a buff on an ally or a Hold Person on whoever's hitting hardest, not just melee damage.
- **Channel Divinity — Guided Strike (1/rest):** +10 to one attack roll — he banks this to
  guarantee a hit that matters rather than spending it early.

**Drow Mercenary** (×3, the red shirts — homebrew, no source block)
- Shortsword +3 to hit, 1d6+2 slashing (melee), or Hand Crossbow +3, 1d6+2 piercing (ranged;
  DC 11 CON save or poisoned 1 min, once per escort per day).
- **Sunlight sensitivity** is irrelevant at 3 AM, but darkvision 120 ft. isn't: they fight from

- No special tactics. They fight alongside the named three, and flee or surrender if isolated and

- A captured mercenary knows little: who paid him, that the woman in charge is a wizard of a great
