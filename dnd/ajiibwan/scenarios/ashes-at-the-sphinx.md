## Session 6 opens here — the false tomb, and the priest who stayed

**Dramatic question for tonight:** *A priest gave up his death to keep this oasis safe, and is
losing the fight with what that made him. The party can help him hold, use him, or end him —
and whatever they choose, the oasis has to live with it.*

**Where they are.** At the north end of **9c, the Hall of the Great God**, in front of the
hidden door between the two armoured statues. Behind it is **9o, the false treasure room**; on
9o's far wall is the door into **9p, Sia's tomb**, where the wight is.

**What they are standing in.** 9c is the Black Water Cult's month-long camp, and the party
walked through it without searching it. Everything on the 9c card is still there: the tally
(31 marks, 19 struck through), the dispatch, the mapcase, and **the unsent letter home** — which
complains about *"a man named Tabbat who won't stop praying out loud."* If they search before
opening the door, they will know Tabbat's name before they meet him (9o, option B).

**What they carry into it.**
- **Four of five have taken the oath.** To the wight, they are pilgrims. **Tath'Shar is the
  only unsworn mind in the party** — which matters a great deal in 9p.
- **Gereon has the Acolyte background** — he served in a temple. He is the closest thing the
  party has to a priest, and the wight only truly talks to priests.
- **One obsidian serpent ring**, off a cultist the tomb turned to stone.
- **Two sessions of reading this building.** They know the grammar. Tonight it pays out.

### Check the clock first

The interrogation in session 4 said the cult's main body was due back **within one to two
days.** The party has been below for **about eight hours**. So the column is due, but it is not
overdue. It can arrive at whichever moment serves the session best:
- **While they are in 9o or 9p (strongest).** That is the one part of the tomb the cult has
  already proved it cannot enter (9o, option E). The Keeper's sanctuary means something only if
  something is chasing them.
- **As they climb the stair.** The column is filing into the oasis while the party comes out of
  the sphinx with whatever they took from 9p. That is a fight in the open, or a hide-and-watch.
- **After they leave.** The party rides away and the column is behind them. Then it is a
  consequence instead of a fight: it reaches the tomb with the drowned priest while nobody is
  there, and the Keeper has to face it alone. That works best if the party is heading for
  Durgan's Rest — see *After the tomb — which way?*, Part 2.

What they are bringing: *The clock*, Part 2.

### The grammar, one more time — a search aid

*Everything the party could have learned in the crawl, arranged for the two hidden doors in
front of them. Full reasoning in Part 2, Rule three.*

**The signs carved over doors.** Every sign is built on a pyramid, cut hand-sized into the
**lintel** (the stone beam over the doorway).
- **Clean pyramid** (often with sun, wheat or water added) — *His.* Worship and passage. Safe.
- **Skull on the outer flank, turned outward** (looking at you in the corridor, away from the
  god) — **SEALED.** What is inside is held forever. Do not open. *(9d, 9g, 9h)*
- **Skull set into the face like an inlaid stone, tilted up toward the apex** (looking at the
  god) — **WASHING.** What is inside is being purified toward release. Also do not open — because
  opening interrupts the work. *(9i, 9k)*
- **The road under the pyramid struck through** — **NO PASSAGE.** The route does not go this
  way. *(9b)*
- **An open mouth carved beneath a clean pyramid** — **this threshold answers speech.** A worn
  pendant does nothing. Say one of the four names. *(9e, 9m, and the door into 9p)*
- **Hammered flat** — it was marked and someone destroyed the label. *(9f, An-Zefful)*
- **No mark at all** — not Doorne's work: squatters, objects, or something the god did not put
  there. *(9j, 9n, 9o — and, seen from outside, 9p)*

**The four names** (any one satisfies an open-mouth door; sincerity is not checked):
*Doorne the All-Knowing · Mighty Doorne · Doorne the Omnipotent · Doorne the Creator.*

**The physical tells.**
- **A marked cell has three more tells:** a **raised stone sill** you must step over on purpose,
  a **shallow offering dish in the floor outside**, and **a handle on the corridor side only.**
  You can open these doors. Nothing inside can.
- **The gold-flecked black marble is the priests' road.** It routes around every marked room and
  never turns into one.
- **Marks label rooms, not objects.** The scarab in 9l lay in a clean room and was still cursed.

**Finding the hidden door they are standing at (9c → 9o).** Clues in rising order of
obviousness:
- **The gold road ends at a blank wall.** It runs up between the two armoured statues and stops.
  *A road does not end at a wall.*
- **The floor in front of the wall is worn** into a shallow hollow, the way stone wears where
  people stood and bowed for centuries. Pilgrims were allowed this far and no farther.
- **The matched pair.** Two armoured statues, five feet apart, hands on their sword hilts. The
  source drops the search to CL 5 if anyone notices this mirrors another pair with a door
  between them.
- **The cult found it first.** A curved scrape in the floor dust where a pivoting door has swung,
  chalk route-marks in the cult's script, boot traffic. **Orkideh's dog** works the base of the
  wall without being asked — give it the free find.
- **There is no sign over it.** By the grammar, the room behind is not Doorne's. **Say that out
  loud if nobody else does: the rules are about to change.**

**Finding the door to 9p (inside 9o).** The source makes this easy once the floor has collapsed
(CL 2). The puzzle is *reading* it, not finding it.
- **The floor fell everywhere except a two-foot ledge along the west wall.** Builders who dropped
  a whole floor and spared one strip were telling you where to stand.
- **A second matched pair:** two golden statues on that wall, a warrior and a priest. Same rule,
  said again.
- **The cult's siege:** a fan of soot, chiselled edges, a pick-head snapped off in the top seam,
  acid runnels. Wipe it and the stone underneath is perfect.
- **The lintel: a clean pyramid with an open mouth beneath it.** This door wants words.
- **And the inconsistency, for the readers:** the *door* carries Doorne's sign; the *room* behind
  it carries nothing. The party may read that as "Doorne didn't put whatever is in there." They
  are half right. See 9p — the mark is on the inside.

**Jiyloo's line, if the party stalls on the ledge:** *"It's the same as the statue room. It wants
to hear you say it."* She will not say it first.

**Jiyloo's other line — give it the first time anyone wonders aloud what is behind that door:**
*"My grandmother said that when the empire fell, one priest stayed behind, so the dead would stay
where they were put."* That is all she knows. It is a children's story. It is true.

### Suggested running order

1. **Search 9c's camp** if they want to (Tabbat's name, the dispatch, the mapcase), then **open
   the hidden door** using the clues above.
2. **9o:** pick from options A–E on the 9o card. Give them time with the paintings.
3. **The voice through the door** (option D) once someone reaches the ledge. Talk through the
   door for as long as the table enjoys it.
4. **The column arrives** (option E) when the talk is at its most interesting, or when the table's
   energy dips. It forces the decision about the door.
5. **9p:** the Keeper. Run him with the hunger track (*Running the Keeper*, after the 9p card).
6. **If they make a bargain:** the column meets the tomb, and the session ends on the road north
   (*The road north*, Part 2).

## Doorne's rules — the table card

*The one-page version. Full reasoning and room lists in Part 2.*

### The five rites

What a pilgrim owes this place. Taught in **9a**, the vestibule; the three rules below are how
they cash out at the table.

| | Rite | What it is | Covered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Bear the sign** | Wear a pyramid of Doorne openly | Rule ① — *this is Jiyloo's pendant* |
| **2** | **Speak the praise** | Say one of the four names at a threshold | Rule ① — *the open-mouth doors* |
| **3** | **Arrive clean** | Wash in his water before going deeper | *the foot-washing that settles the ants* |
| **4** | **Depart holy** | Leave as holy as you came — carry none of it out | ★ *this is what prices the loot* |
| **5** | **Remain pure** | The oath, and conduct afterward | Rule ② — *and already broken on the plinth* |

**The four names:** *Doorne the All-Knowing · Mighty Doorne · Doorne the Omnipotent · Doorne the
Creator.* Any one of them satisfies rite 2, and **sincerity is not required** — which is the whole
reason the Black Water Cult is standing outside.

**Rites 4 and 5 are the ones with teeth.** ***Depart holy*** is not a prohibition on theft, it's a
statement about what this building *is*: the gold is in the floor, the water is in the pools, the
fire is in the hearth. You can't take the holiness out, so you can't take the things out.
***Remain pure*** isn't an act you perform once — it's a standing condition, which is why a
killing done on the god's own threshold keeps mattering.

---

**① The pendant.** Only **two** things in sixteen rooms check credentials, and they want
different keys:
- **9a** — an openly worn **pyramid** (this is Jiyloo, and it's why 9a was quiet).
- **9e** — **spoken praise** to one of the four names. *A worn symbol is not enough here.*

Everything else — the dead, the beasts, the traps, An-Zefful — isn't checking anything.
**Anyone can wear a pyramid**; Temple al Sia above ground is covered in them.

**② The blessing (*Unwelcome*).** Taken in **9a**, at one of four doors, per character, once.
Kneel and say *"I take the goodness of Doorne into my heart."*

| Door | Aspect | Boon (until they leave the tomb) |
|---|---|---|
| **East** | the Creator | No food or water needed; **adv. on saves vs poison & disease** |
| **West** | the All-Knowing | **Adv. on Investigation / History / Religion here**; door-marks read as plain language |
| **North** | the Mighty | Gear **weighs half**; **adv. on Athletics** to lift, shove, brace, hold |
| **South** | the Omnipotent | **Adv. on saves vs frightened, charmed, paralysed, forced movement** |

**Declined all four → *Unwelcome*: −1d4 on attacks, saves and checks — but ONLY against
Doorne's own work.** Not the gargoyle, not the Dark Creeper, not the scarab swarm, not the
settled ants, **not the Black Water Cult.** Ask once; it rides. **9m** is the only place to
convert, renounce or re-dedicate. *It is not faith* — no protection from the Scepter.

**③ The doors.** Doorne does not destroy, he **keeps**. Most of this complex is a gaol, and the
mark over each door tells you which kind:

All of them are carved on the **lintel**, hand-sized. Marked rooms also have a **raised sill**, a
**stone offering-dish in the floor outside**, and **a handle on the corridor side only.**

| Mark | Meaning | Rooms |
|---|---|---|
| Clean pyramid | His | 9a, 9c, 9e, 9l, 9m |
| **Skull on the flank, turned OUT** (facing you, away from the god) | **SEALED** — held forever. Don't open. | **9d, 9g, 9h** |
| **Skull set INTO the face, tilted up to the apex** (facing the god) | **WASHING** — being purified toward release. Don't open. | **9i, 9k** |
| Base-road **struck through** | **NO PASSAGE** — the route doesn't go this way | **9b** |
| **An open mouth beneath the pyramid** | ★ **This one wants WORDS, not a worn symbol.** Speak a name. | **9e**, 9m, and **the secret door into 9p** |
| *Defaced — hammered flat* | was marked; can't tell which | **9f** |
| *None at all* | not Doorne's work — squatters, objects, or **evidence** | 9j, 9n, 9o — and **9p from outside** (★ its mark is on the *inside*) |

**★ The open mouth is the sign that wins the adventure.** It's on 9e, where the pendant stops
working — and it's on the secret door into Sia's tomb, which the Black Water Cult beat on for days
with fire, steel, acid and magic. That door has never been locked. It opens for four words, and
nobody down here could read a mouth.

**The gold-flecked pilgrim road routes around every marked room.** Follow the floor and you meet
none of them. Marked doors have **handles on the corridor side only** and a **raised sill**, with
a **stone offering-dish outside** still holding the last offering anyone ever made.

**Jiyloo's free line, whenever it first comes up:** *"You don't go through a skull door."* She
cannot explain why. Her grandmother told her.

### 9a — Hall of Water ⟨clean⟩ · **the vestibule**

**The doors (all four).** Every lintel carries a clean pyramid — no skull, no sill, no offering
dish. Each is cut with one extra element, and **those four elements are the four aspects:**

| | Lintel carving | Aspect |
|---|---|---|
| **East** | water running down both slopes, wheat standing at the base | the **Creator** |
| **West** | a half-sun setting behind the pyramid, a small pair of scales | the **All-Knowing** |
| **North** | a block of dressed stone resting on the pyramid's shoulder | the **Mighty** |
| **South** | sand-lines streaming around the pyramid, which does not move | the **Omnipotent** |

> **Read:** "Ornate to the point of showing off. Frescoes of a green and crowded empire. The floor
> is black marble streaked with gold. Two pools of clear, slowly turning water stand before the
> wyrmwood door in the west wall, and two more before the door in the east. There are doors north
> and south as well. A statue of a woman in desert robes stands in each corner."

**In it:** two of the four statues are **Caryatid Columns** — *dormant, because Jiyloo's pendant is
showing.* They wake for anyone approaching a door with no pyramid on display.

**The walls — this room is a classroom and the party has crossed it twice:**
- **The four statues wear a carved pyramid pendant, identical to Jiyloo's.** *(free)*
  → **rite 1, Bear the sign**
- **A band of script runs beneath the frescoes on all four walls.** **DC 13 History** → the four
  names: *Doorne the All-Knowing, Mighty Doorne, Doorne the Omnipotent, Doorne the Creator.*
  → **rite 2, Speak the praise**
- **Pilgrims are shown seated on these exact pool rims washing their feet**, one kneeling to wash
  an elder's. *(free after the ant scene, otherwise DC 10)* → **rite 3, Arrive clean**
- **Every figure painted inside the building has empty or open hands.** The ones carrying goods
  are all outside it. **DC 14 Investigation/Religion:** the panels show the god's substance being
  *poured into* the place — gold into the floor, water into the pools, fire into the hearth.
  Look down: the marble really is streaked with gold. **You cannot carry the holiness out, so you
  cannot carry the things out.** → **rite 4, Depart holy**

**The five rites, in the order the room teaches them:**
**1. Bear the sign** · **2. Speak the praise** · **3. Arrive clean** · **4. Depart holy** ·
**5. Remain pure.**

The last two are the ones with teeth. ***Depart holy*** means you leave as holy as you came —
which, in a building whose wealth *is* its holiness, means taking none of it. ***Remain pure*** is
not a single act but a standing condition: the oath at the doorframe, and conduct afterward. *(It
is also the one the party has already broken — see Part 2, Rule two, on the prisoner executed on
the plinth.)*

**Do:** wash feet in the pools (**Arrive clean**). Then each PC kneels at one door and takes the
oath (**Remain pure**) — **the door they pick sets their boon.** → rules card ②

**Loot:** none. That's the lesson.

### 9b — Guardian Ants ⟨**NO PASSAGE**⟩

**The door.** Pyramid on the lintel standing on a carved **base-line — a road** — and that road is
**cut clean through by a single deep chisel stroke.** No skull. No sill, no offering dish: this is
not a cell. **It means: the route does not go this way.** The builders were being helpful.

> **Read:** "The walls carry bas-reliefs of giant ants eating people staked out in the sand. Some
> of the ants standing along the north and south walls stop being carvings."

**In it:** **12 Giant Soldier Ants**, two waking per round for six rounds. Fight to the death.

**The walls.** Not decoration and not a threat display — **a picture of the penalty for robbery,
hung in the room that carries it out.** Spread-eagled figures pegged in sand, ants over them.

**Do:** **the way out is the wash, not the fight** (✅ *played in session 5: Jiyloo held the door
while the party washed, then washed hers*). Once the ants settle,
**taking the dead cultist and his route-sketch is safe** — he came from outside. **Prying the door
fittings or chipping the floor wakes all twelve**, because that is robbery and robbery is the one
thing this room exists for. That distinction *is* **rite 4, Depart holy**, delivered survivably.

**Loot:** a dead cultist's kit and a charcoal **route-sketch** — ant room crossed out, two rooms
blank, one corridor drawn wrong.

### 9c — Hall of the Great God ⟨clean⟩ · **the teaching room, and the cult's camp**

**The door.** Clean pyramid, **sun disc on the apex, wheat at the base.** No sill, no dish. The
gold-flecked pilgrim road runs straight down the middle of this hall — **this is the processional,
the spine of the complex.**

> **Read:** "A hall twenty feet wide, frescoed end to end: desert people in lush country
> worshipping a man who wears a pyramid for a hat. A pyramid stands in every panel — struck by
> lightning, with the sun on its point, with wheat growing out of the top. Some of them have a
> skull on the side. At the far end stand two statues of armoured men, hands on their sword hilts."

**In it:** nothing hostile. Twenty-odd cultists lived here for a month.

**The walls — ★ this is where the grammar gets taught.** The source hands you every variation of
the pyramid on one hallway:
- struck by lightning · sun touching the pinnacle · wheat growing from the top → **the clean sign.
  His. Worship, harvest, weather, passage.**
- **and some with a skull on the side** → **the other kind.**

**DC 10 Investigation:** there are two kinds of pyramid on these walls, and the skull ones are
always beside panels of the dead. **DC 13:** the skull sign also appears *over doorways* — and the
gold road never once turns into a room that has one. **DC 16 / Religion:** the skull's orientation
is the word. Facing **out, away from the pyramid** = *sealed.* Set **into the face, tilted up
toward the apex** = *washing.*

**Also in the walls:** a **secret door between the two armoured statues** at the north end. CL 10
→ **CL 7** if someone remarks there are only two statues and they stand five feet apart → **CL 5**
if they've noticed the matching pair in the west hall.

**The camp.** Tally scratched into the plaster (**31 marks, 19 struck through**), the dispatch in
an oilcloth wallet under a bedroll (**DC 13**), an unsent letter home, the mapcase, and a latrine
trench dug under a fresco of the harvest. **DC 10:** bedding rolled, firepits banked, stores
stacked — *nobody who leaves for good tidies up.* **They are coming back here.**
→ Part 2, *The cult's high-water mark*

**Do:** learn the signs here. From this room on, **look at every lintel before touching a door.**

**Loot:** no coin. Bulk water, oil, rations, rope, timber, canvas — logistics, worth more than
coin on a clock.

### 9d — Skeletal Guardians ⟨**SEALED**⟩

**The door. ★ Do not open this one.** Pyramid on the lintel with a **skull in low relief on the
right flank, turned outward** — it faces *you*, standing in the corridor, and faces *away* from the
god. Raised sill. Offering dish in the floor outside with the dust of the last offering still in
it. Handle on the corridor side only. **Sealed: what is in here is held, and will be held.
Nothing about it is going to change.**

> **Read (only if they open it):** "Eight skeletons, two in each corner. All eight heads turn. The
> frescoes show people being sacrificed in front of a pyramid with a skull on its side."

**In it:** **8 Skeletons.** Vulnerable to bludgeoning. **They chase** — the only marked room whose
occupants pursue past the threshold.

**The walls.** Sacrifice, repeated: figures held down before a skull-marked pyramid. **Their hands
are bound and their faces are turned away from the god** — the same direction the skull on the
lintel faces. That is the whole meaning of *sealed*, said twice.

**What it actually is:** the people the state murdered in Doorne's name. **There is no rite for
the murdered** — they did nothing to be purified of — so they are not being washed, only kept,
because they are furious. They have a case, and that is why they chase.

**Do:** walk past. If they open it, fight in the corridor is a mistake and running doesn't work.

**Loot:** none.

### 9e — Statue Guardians ⟨clean, **+ the open mouth**⟩ · **where the cult broke**

**The door. ★ This lintel carries a fifth sign, and it is the one that saves them.** A clean
pyramid — and **beneath it, small and worn, a carved open mouth.** **The mouth means: this one
wants words spoken, not a symbol worn.** A party that has got into the habit of reading lintels
gets this for free and walks through. A party that hasn't, doesn't.

> **Read:** "Statues of men and women in priestly robes line the south wall. Above them, something
> is chiselled into the stone in large letters — gouged at, scraped, attacked with a chisel, and
> still perfectly legible. The plinth in the middle of the room is ringed with bodies."

**In it:** **1 Stone Guardian.** Animates only if someone travels **15 ft in** before praise is
spoken. AC 18; **edged weapons quarter damage, missiles do nothing and are destroyed**, wood hafts
break; immune to mind control; sees invisible. Won't leave the room.

**The walls.** Worship at the feet of the pyramid-hatted man; one panel shows **two sphinxes
genuflecting.** And above the statues, in Doornian script *and* hieroglyphs, large enough to read
across the room:

> ***"Praise Doorne the All-Knowing and live."***

It has been gouged, scraped and chiselled at — and left perfectly legible.

**Do:** **say one of the four names out loud before anyone is 15 ft into the room.** The pendant
does nothing here. Said *mid-fight* it stops the guardian and resets it — **unless it's struck
again afterwards**, which restarts the fight for good.

**The cult, at their high-water mark:** seven or eight mummified cultists ringed at exactly the
guardian's reach, scimitars snapped, arrows in splinters nowhere near it. **DC 10:** they attacked
it on separate days — they came back and tried again. **DC 14 Religion / Jiyloo:** they read the
inscription, tried to destroy it, and went into the desert to buy magic rather than say four
words to the wrong god.

**Loot:** cult gear.

### 9f — An-Zefful, the Minotaur ⟨**DEFACED**⟩

**The door.** The lintel has been **hammered flat.** You can make out the ghost outline of a
pyramid and a rough oval where something was set into it — **and you cannot tell which orientation
the skull had.** The sill is still there and so is the offering dish, kicked half out of its
setting. So it *was* marked, and someone destroyed the label.

> **Read:** "Every fresco in this room has been smashed and scratched off the wall. Blood stains
> the whole north wall, old and black. In the middle stands a minotaur with an axe over his
> shoulder. He turns, lowers his head, and bellows."

**In it:** **An-Zefful.** 35 HP, +7, 1d8+5 (**Battle Axe +1**). **Never Surprised.** Powerful
Charge on round 1 if the room allows it. Cannot be talked down; has a death wish. Can't follow
anyone out; turns to dust if magically removed.

**The walls.** There aren't any left. That is the information.

**What it means:** the party **cannot tell whether he is sealed or washing** — whether killing him
ends a punishment or interrupts a redemption. Best answer, never stated: **he did it himself,**
over four centuries, trying to erase his own sentence. **No blessing is available in this room**,
because there is nothing left to read.

**Do:** walk past. If they fight him, give the kill a line of narration — it's a mercy either way,
and nobody will ever know which.

**Loot:** **Battle Axe +1.**

### 9g — Coffer Corpse ⟨**SEALED** — and the carving is newer⟩

**The door.** Sealed mark: skull on the flank, turned outward. Sill, dish, corridor-side handle.
**DC 12 — and this is the one worth catching:** the carving is **sharper and less weathered than
every other mark in the complex.** It was cut centuries after the rest.

> **Read (only if they open it):** "The walls are painted with weather — sunrises through to floods

**In it:** **Coffer Corpse.** 10 HP — **only harmed by magic weapons** (say this before three

**What it means:** the "statues" are a **petrified bandit** who was caught robbing this tomb and
the **cockatrice** that caught him. *After the priests stopped coming.* **Somebody cut a fresh

**Do:** walk past. If they open it: magic weapons only, and don't turn the cockatrice back to flesh.

### 9h — Ogre Guardian ⟨**SEALED**⟩

**The door.** Sealed mark: skull on the flank, turned outward. Sill, corridor-side handle — and
**the offering dish outside this one is scoured clean and empty,** with no residue at all. Every
other dish in the complex has centuries of dried offerings in it. **Nobody ever left anything for
this one.** Whatever is in there, the priests kept it and did not feed it.

> **Read (only if they open it):** "The walls are painted black, with small yellow lights shining
> on them. On the far wall a large man in a pyramid hat is painted, staring at you. Below him the

**In it:** **Monster Zombie (Ogre).** 20 HP, slam 1d6+4, **Slow** (always acts last). Won't chase.

**The walls.** Black, and the yellow lights are **22 citrines** set into the walls and ceiling,
20 gp each — a night sky, with the god painted staring straight at the door. Half an hour to pry

**What it means:** sealed, and filed without ceremony. Not even a Doornian — just a thing that had

**Do:** walk past. **The citrines are the temptation, and *Depart holy* applies** — they are set
into the fabric of the building. Prying them is the same act the man in the wall in 9j died committing.

**Loot:** up to 440 gp of citrine, at a price.

### 9i — Ghoul Guardians, the dakhma ⟨**WASHING**⟩

**The door.** Pyramid on the lintel with the skull **set into the sloping face like an inlaid
stone, tilted up toward the apex** — it faces *the god*, not you. Sill, corridor-side handle. And
**the offering dish outside this one is the deepest-crusted in the complex** — layer on layer of
grain and oil residue. *Somebody tended this one, every day, for a very long time.* **Washing: the
thing inside is being worked on, and the work was meant to end in release.**

> **Read (only if they open it):** "The room reeks. The reliefs show the man in the pyramid hat —
> a skull on the side of it — standing in the well of a dakhma, bodies laid out in rings around
> him, his hand raised. Four of the bodies in the relief are standing, with long fangs. Four in
> the room begin to shuffle toward you."

**In it:** **4 Ghouls.** Two claws each per turn. **Paralysis on a claw hit — STR save or 1d4+1
minutes.** Call it out loudly the first time it lands; it is the real danger, not the damage.

**The walls.** A **dakhma** — a tower of silence, a structure whose actual purpose is to expose and
purify the dead. The relief shows the rite as it should look: concentric rings of laid-out bodies,
the god in the well with his hand raised. **Four of them are standing up in the picture too.** The
relief is a diagram of a process, and it is a diagram of a process that has gone wrong.

**What it means:** these are people who died accursed of Doorne and are being **cleansed**. Four
hundred years in, and not clean yet, because the fire went out when the Ember did.

**Do:** walk past. **Killing them ends the process rather than completing it**, and Jiyloo will say
so, badly, the way people say things they were raised on and can't argue for.
**Optional — complete the rite:** bodies returned to the circles, **their names** (findable in 9c's
frescoes or a priest's roll in 9m), and **Doorne's own fire** — Sia's Coal, or a brand from the
relit Ember. → Part 2, Rule three, *finishing a washing*

**Loot:** none.

### 9j — Hall of the Forgotten Guardians ⟨**none**⟩ · **the dig**

**The door.** **Plain lintel. No pyramid at all, no sill, no dish.** To a party that has learned to
read, that says something useful: **this is a corridor of the complex, not a cell.** It is also a
reminder that an unmarked room can still kill you — the tomb only labels *its own* work.

> **Read:** "The walls show humans working in mines and building tombs, every overseer in a pyramid
> hat. Two brightly painted statues stand on the east wall. And in the far wall, someone has hacked
> a tunnel."

**In it:** **1 Gargoyle** — one of the two painted statues. **Freeze:** give it a surprise round.
Pursues this hallway, not beyond. *A squatter. Not Doorne's, not marked, not answerable to
anything.*

**The walls — ★ look closely, because the cult didn't.** At a glance: miners cutting stone,
labourers raising tombs, overseers in pyramid hats supervising.

**DC 12:** the miners **are not mining.** Their hands and forearms are already **merged into the
rock face they are cutting** — the stone has grown over them to the wrist, the elbow, the shoulder
— and the pyramid-hatted overseers standing behind them are watching it happen **and smiling.**

**This is not a mark and it is not a curse. It is an instruction manual,** painted four hundred
years ago, in the one room where somebody would eventually be stupid enough to dig.

**The dig.** Eighteen feet of tunnel driven at the sealed wing, shored with wagon timber cut from
the burned shop above ground. **Seven cultists are in the walls** — one sunk to the waist still
holding a pick that is also in the stone, one a shoulder and half a face, one just a hand flush
with the rock at the tunnel face. **DC 15:** absorption runs *against* the dig — the shallowest
body is the **last** man in. They kept sending people.

**Do — telegraph this hard, it is not a gotcha:** the **first tool that cuts stone sticks and is
lost. Nobody is hurt.** That's the warning shot. Persist → **CON 13**, restrained and sinking, one
round for allies to haul them clear; a second failure costs the hand. **Walking, hauling spoil and
carrying bodies out are completely safe** — say so if they ask.

**Loot:** steel mining tools (~40 gp). In the waist-deep man's pocket: a **black obsidian serpent
ring** (Cult membership token — infiltration later) and **~15 gp of gold filings** he had been
scraping out of 9a's floor for weeks, hidden, against orders.

### 9l — Scarab Guardians ⟨**none** — and the door is telling the truth⟩

**The door.** **A clean pyramid with wheat at the base. No skull. No sill. No offering dish.** This
is a *good* room and the lintel says so honestly.

> **Read:** "A bright sun fills the northwest corner. The floor is green stone, inlaid with people
> dancing with their hands joined. In the centre lies a beautifully wrought golden scarab."

**In it:** nothing — **until someone lifts the scarab**, at which point a **Scarab Beetle Swarm**
pours out of the east and west walls onto whoever is holding it.

**The walls and floor.** The high-water mark of the good reign: a sun, joined hands, dancing.
**And not one figure in the inlay is holding anything** — the same empty-handed motif as 9a.

**What it means — ★ this is the room that keeps the whole mark system honest.** The marks label
**rooms, not objects.** This room is clean. The thing lying in the middle of it is not. Let the
system fail here, exactly once, on purpose.

**Do:** admire it. **Don't pick up the scarab.** If someone does: the swarm, and the curse — the
bearer can't sleep well enough to heal or recover spells until it's returned; dropping or selling
it makes it reappear in their path; *Remove Curse* holds only 24 hours unless cast by a 9th-level
priest.

**Loot:** the scarab. Don't.

### 9m — Temple of the Sphynx ⟨**clean, and maximally**⟩

**The door.** Clean pyramid, **sun on the apex, and the open mouth beneath it** — the same sign as
9e: *this place answers people who speak.* And cut into the threshold stone itself, a **shallow
basin**, worn smooth by centuries of hands. Inside, the three doors are inlaid rather than carved:
**west a golden sun, east a mother-of-pearl moon, south a brown soapstone pyramid.**

> **Read:** "A temple, and stunning. An ironwood podium stands at the centre of the south wall with
> a chair behind it, flanked by huge seated statues of **dog-headed men in pyramid hats** — the
> left one's eyes are rubies, the right one's emeralds. Rows of carved benches fill the rest."

**In it:** nothing hostile.

**The walls and statues.** This is the living heart of the complex — the room the priests actually
worked in, between rounds of leaving grain at eleven thresholds. The **dog-headed attendants** are
Doorne's servants; the benches mean this room held *people*, not just rites.

**Do:**
- **★ This is the only place to convert, renounce, or re-dedicate** (rules card ②). The worn basin
  in the threshold is where you kneel. If someone has been eating the −1d4 for six rooms, this is
  their destination — say the basin is worn smooth and let them draw the conclusion.
- **The dog.** Describe the dog-headed statues. Then describe **Orkideh's dog going quiet when it
  sees them.** Say nothing else.
- **A priest's roll** is findable here — the names needed to finish the washing in 9i.

***Depart holy* bites hardest here:** rubies 400 gp each, emeralds 600 gp each, ~17 gp of door
inlay.
Prying the eyes out of a temple in front of Jiyloo is not a loot decision, it's a relationship one.

**Loot:** 2,000 gp of eyes you shouldn't take.

### 9n — Tomb Guardians ⟨**none** — a different vocabulary⟩

**The door.** No pyramid on the lintel. Instead the whole frame is carved with **gryphons in
flight** — military heraldry, not priestly signage. **This isn't a cell and it isn't a shrine. It's
an honour vault**, granted space inside the god's house.

> **Read:** "Five stone coffins rest on catafalques, each carved with the relief of a knight
> holding a sword. Statues of the pyramid-hatted man stand in the northeast and southeast corners,
> and between them the walls carry knights flying on gryphons."

**In it:** **Dark Creeper** in the centre coffin. AC 20 (16 in light), 6 HP. Grabs a light source
and vanishes rather than fighting. **Let him succeed once; don't spend more than two rounds.**

**The walls and coffins.** Each of the five coffin lids names its knight in the relief. Four of the
five hold **Griffon Blades** — an elite order that **still exists and is honoured across the
desert**, and which has lost the location of this tomb entirely.

**Do:** **read the coffin reliefs and write the names down.** Returning one knight's blade and
sigil to the living order costs nothing but the carrying and is worth more than the grave goods.
**This is the reputation payoff, and reputation is exactly what this party chose over gold at
Elmingwed's gate.**

**Loot:** four knights' arms. Spend them on standing, not on a fence.

### 9o — False Treasure Room ⟨**none** — and that should feel wrong⟩ · **the humiliation, and the ledge**

**The door in (from 9c).** Hidden between the two armoured statues. **Plain. No pyramid, no skull,
no sill, no dish.** By now that should bother somebody: *the treasure room of a god's tomb, and
the god didn't sign it?* **Because it isn't the treasure room and it isn't his work. It's Sia's** —
the king's vanity, built by the king's men.

> **Read:** "This must be the treasure room. The paintings show a man whipping slaves, waging war,
> lounging among beautiful women. Two ivory chests stand against the north wall and two against
> the south — all four smashed open, the birch showing through the thin ivory. Three hundred silver
> pieces lie scattered where somebody weighed them. The bronze daggers have been thrown at a wall.
> In the corner is a neat little heap of scraped gold leaf. And most of the floor is gone."

**In it:** no creature (unless you use option B). **The cult already fired the trap** — moved the
golden statues without finding the switch and dropped the floor. **Four mummified cultists** on the
spikes ten feet down (the poison died centuries ago). The **two-foot ledge** in front of the door to
9p survives. The party comes in on the **east** side; the ledge is on the **west** wall, across the pit.

**The walls — ★ the tell.** Whipping, war, concubines. **The pyramid appears in exactly none of
these panels.** Every other room in this complex has the god somewhere in the picture. Here the
king is alone, and everything in the room is counterfeit: ivory over birch, gold leaf over
worm-eaten wood, shaved silver, −1 bronze daggers, pewter "chainmail." Somebody spent a full day
scraping and came away with **10 gp of leaf**. *(Contrast with the miner's private 15 gp in 9j: one
is sanctioned and pointless, the other is a man robbing his own side in secret.)*

**The door out (to 9p), on the ledge.** Look at *its* lintel: **a clean pyramid with the open mouth
beneath it.** The same sign as 9e. **It isn't locked. It has never been locked. It opens for
words.** And it has been **worked on for days**: soot in a fan shape, every edge chiselled, a
**pick-head snapped off in the top seam**, acid runnels down the face. Wipe it clean and **the
stone underneath is perfect.** **DC 13:** fire, steel and acid, over days. **DC 16 / Arcana 14:**
and at least one real spell.

**Do:** cross the pit, **speak one of the four names** on the ledge. A party that learned the
grammar opens in one sentence the door a month of siege engineering could not touch. Give them the
beat — and then give them a reason to hesitate before they say it (options B and D).

**Loot:** four cultists' gear, ~25 gp, and the **Fire Beetle Lantern** in the hand of the one nearest
the ledge, down on the spikes. *(Smokeless, needs no air, ~a week per tank, five Holy Cities etched
on the base in a script nobody can read. Do not explain it.)* → Part 2, *The lamp*

#### 9o — five ways to raise the stakes *(pick two or three; they stack)*

**A. The crossing — the room is a pit.** Between the east doorway and the ledge is a ten-foot drop
onto spikes (**1d10**). The cult's answer is still in place: **two lengths of wagon timber** laid
across the pit, the same burned-shop timber that shores the dig in 9j.
- **One plank is sound; the other is fire-cracked** from the days they burned things against the
  door. **DC 12 Perception or Investigation** spots the crack before anyone steps on it.
- **The sound plank:** DC 10 Acrobatics, or no check if roped. **The cracked one** holds one person,
  then goes — **DC 12 Dexterity save** to catch the ledge.
- **The ledge holds about four people.** That matters if the column arrives (E): not everyone fits.
- **The North-door oath (*the Bearer*)** earns its keep: advantage on Athletics to brace a plank,
  hold a rope, or haul someone off the spikes.
- *What it buys:* physical tension in a room that was otherwise a museum, and a reason to decide
  **who goes first** onto a two-foot ledge in front of a door with something behind it.

**B. The man on the ledge — the plinth again, with the stakes reversed.** One of the cult is still
alive, **sitting on the ledge with his back against the door.** He is **Tabbat** — the man in the
unsent letter in 9c, "who won't stop praying out loud."
- He was on the ledge when the floor dropped. The others could not reach him and did not try very
  hard. He has been there **eight days**, living on the waterskins of the dead men below, fished up
  one at a time with a hooked spear haft. He is down to the last mouthful, praying to Elsrok in a
  cracked whisper.
- **He has spent eight days in front of a door that would open for four words, and he will not say
  them.** He read the mouth on the lintel days ago.
- **And something behind the door has been asking him to come in.** *"Every night. Some nights it's
  kind. Some nights it isn't. It wants me to say the words. I won't."* His refusal to praise Doorne
  is the only reason he is alive. (He is hearing the Keeper — both of him. See 9p.)
- **What he knows** (traded freely for water — he is bitter): the main body went for **"drowned
  spirits in jars, from the old pyramid on the north road"**; Ashiq — "the water-woman" — **worked
  this door herself with a spell and failed**, and left in a fury.
- **The choice.** The party executed exactly this man on the plinth. This one is harmless, useful,
  and will die without help. **Rescue him** (a witness — and a fanatic who may bolt for the door the
  moment it opens, because Ashiq wanted the Scepter and he has nothing else to bring her). **Leave
  him** (he dies; nobody will know; Jiyloo will). **Kill him** (the table will watch who reaches for
  it). **Or give him to what is behind the door**, which will ask for him if it learns he exists. Do
  not suggest that. If a player does, let the room go quiet.

**C. The god is missing from every picture — and the priest is in all of them.** Add one detail to
the paintings:

> **In every panel, a man in a high priest's robes stands at the king's shoulder.** Through the
> whipping, the wars, the women. In the place where every other room puts the god, this room puts
> the priest.

- **The name.** The two golden statues on the west wall are the king and that priest. **The cult's
  gold-leaf scraping exposed a name cut into the priest statue's base**, which the gilding had
  covered. **DC 12 History** (advantage with the West-door *Reader's Eye* oath) reads it: ***Kamenhet,
  Voice of the King.*** The cult's greed uncovered it and none of them could read it.
- **The last panel.** Low on the west wall beside the ledge, under the cult's soot, is one small
  painting in a **plainer, later hand** than the rest. Wipe the soot (**free** to anyone on the ledge
  who wipes the door; otherwise **DC 13 Investigation**): the same priest, alone, **kneeling at a
  door with a sceptre across his knees.** Over the painted door, a skull — **set facing inward, toward
  him.** And behind him, his shadow on the wall has **a skull for a face.**
- *What it buys:* the party walks into 9p knowing **who** is in there, that **he put himself there**,
  and that **he is holding something terrible in himself.** And they have his **name** — which is the
  single most useful thing they can carry through that door (*Running the Keeper*).

**D. A voice through the door — he speaks first.** He has been awake for four hundred years, and for
the last month he has listened to the cult fail outside his door. **He knows the party is in 9o
before they know he exists.** When someone reaches the ledge, a voice comes **through the stone,
aloud** — dry, careful, unused:

> *"Who stands at the door? Are you clean?"*

- That is **the priest**. He asks after their feet, their oath, their god. He addresses whoever
  answers **as a pilgrim**, and asks whether they have a priest with them.
- **Tath'Shar hears a second voice, inside their head, that nobody else hears.** It is warmer than
  the first, and much closer. *"Not you. You didn't kneel. Come here and let me look at you."* That
  is **the hunger**, and the unsworn are the only minds it can reach. → *Running the Keeper, the
  hunger's offer*. **Tell Phoenix privately (a passed note or a DM) — this is theirs to share or not.**
- *Why this is better than a lid flying off:* the conversation starts **with a sealed door between
  them.** The party can talk, learn, lie, and decide whether to open it at all.

**E. The column arrives — and the only sanctuary is the wight's room.** *Use if the clock lands
tonight.* The dog hears it first: boots on the stair, far back. Then voices. Then light in the
corridor behind them. **Ashiq's column is in the tomb.** The party is on a ledge over a pit, with the
cult behind and a door in front that opens to four words the cult will not say. And the priest's
voice says:

> *"Speak, and come in. They cannot follow. Close it behind you."*

- **This is the convergence.** The one room the cult has proved it cannot reach is the wight's.
  Taking sanctuary makes the party **the Keeper's guests** — inside a room with a wight whose hunger
  is rising, while the cult hammers on the other side.
- **Pull the planks** after the last person crosses and the cult is stuck across a pit, outside a
  door — exactly where it has spent the last month. **Or hold the east doorway** and fight in the
  corridor, a fine fight from a worse position.
- If the cult has **the drowned priest** with them (Part 2, *The clock*), the door is not safe for
  long. A mouth that will say the names is walking down the stair.

### 9p — Tomb of Sia ⟨**no mark outside — ★ and one inside**⟩ · **the Keeper**

**The signs — read this carefully, it's the best find in the dungeon.**
- **The way in** (from 9o's ledge) carries a **clean pyramid and an open mouth.** The door is
  Doorne's, and it answers speech — **from the outside only.** There is no mouth and no handle on
  the inside face. Whoever is in here cannot open it, and cannot ask to.
- **From outside, the room is unmarked.** No skull on the lintel, no sill, no offering dish.
- **★ The mark is on the inside.** Once through, look up at the **inner** lintel — the only mark in
  the complex carved to be read from *within* a room. It is a pyramid with a skull, and **the skull
  has been recut, over and over:** turned out (*sealed*), chiselled back and set in (*washing*),
  recut out again. The stone around it is gouged pale from centuries of the same argument. **Whoever
  is in here filed himself, and has never been able to decide which kind he is.**

A party that has read lintels all evening gets that with **no roll**. It is the whole Keeper in one
image: a priest who sealed himself in, hoping he was washing, afraid he was only held.

> **Read:** "Wealth of a kind most people only hear about. Against the west wall, a carved
> sarcophagus inlaid with gold, flanked by two canopic jars. Along the south wall, a rack of gilded
> weapons, a closed ivory chest, a heap of silk pillows, and an altar with a golden pyramid standing
> on it, glowing faintly, like a coal. Against the north, a sealed crate, two open chests spilling
> coins and gems — and at the foot of the sarcophagus, in an ornate chair, a seated figure in a high
> priest's robes, a silver sceptre across his knees, perfectly still. Its eyes are open. Behind
> the chair, half in shadow, stands a box of white metal the size of a small altar, ringed with
> nested metal hoops that do not move."

**In it: the Keeper — Kamenhet, once high priest of Sia, now a wight.** He is **not** Sia. **Sia is
in the sarcophagus**, truly dead, at rest, never raised. The Keeper sits his vigil at the king's feet.
*(This replaces the source's "Sia's high priest poisoned him and raised him as a wight." Nobody
poisoned anyone. The priest's name is Steve's to change; "Kamenhet" was never said at the table.)*

**What he is.** When the empire fell, the priests stopped coming down the stair, and nobody was left
to keep the condemned in their cells or the oasis safe for the king's temples and the travellers who
drank there. **One priest stayed.** He took the **Scepter of Ice** — the keeping-tool, through which
the tomb's guardians hear their orders — and **gave up his death** so the keeping would never end.
He sealed himself into the deepest room with an oath on Doorne's names, behind a door that only
opens to praise from outside.

**Why he sealed himself in.** Becoming a wight put **a terrible evil inside him** — the hunger
every wight has for the warmth of the living. **He bound himself to this room so it could never get
out.** He has held it for four hundred years. He is lucid, reasonable, courteous and very tired —
and **he is a wight, dangerous and unstable.** For two weeks it has been harder: he felt the oasis
go dark when Ashiq smothered the Ember above, and the hunger has been louder ever since. Tabbat
heard both of him.

**The walls.** None — this room is **furnished, not frescoed**: a king's chair, pillows, weapons
packed the way he travelled. Every other room in this complex is *about* something. This one is a
man's belongings and the priest who guards them.

**The language engine.** The white-metal box behind his chair is a **Bokkaran language engine**. Its
nested rings spin and glow when it wakes. Whoever touches it and seeks what it holds comes away able
to **read and speak the ancient tongue**. The temple's priests once used it to learn the language of
the oldest scriptures. It is the Keeper's to give, it is **the reward he offers for service**, and it
is extremely dangerous. Rules and terms: *Running the Keeper* → *The reward*.

**Do:**
- **Talk before you get close.** He will talk — he has been waiting for someone to talk to. Run him
  with the hunger track, below.
- **Close the door behind you.** He will ask. An open door is the thing the hunger wants most.
- **Don't touch the Scepter, the Coal, the sarcophagus or the engine without leave.**
- **The golden pyramid on the altar is Sia's Coal** — the live fragment of the hearth-flame that
  relights Doorne's Ember above ground. It is his to give. → Part 2, *Doorne's Ember*

**Loot:** Sia's grave goods — 3,000 sp · 500 gp · 20 rubies at 100 gp · **Heavy Scimitar +1** ·
**dagger +2** · **Splint Mail +1 with a dog's head for a helm.** The **Scepter of Ice** (30,000 gp;
**4d6 cold to a bearer who isn't faithful**, again next round if not dropped — *the doorframe oath is
not faith*) is in the Keeper's hands, and **taking it ends the keeping.** The king's goods are not
Doorne's holiness, so *Depart holy* does not strictly cover them — but they are a grave, and Jiyloo
knows the difference between a gift and a robbery. **What the Keeper gives freely is clean.**

### Running the Keeper — 9p at the table

#### Protocol — who he talks to

- **Priests.** He *confides* only in priests — in life, priests spoke for people; in death, only the
  faithful can even open his door. His first real question: *"Which of you is the priest?"*
  - **Gereon** (Acolyte — he served in a temple) is the honest answer. The Keeper calls him
    *"Brother"* and talks to him as a colleague. Whether Gereon's temple was Doorne's is the next
    thing he asks, and Mia gets to decide.
  - **A false priest** is a fine, risky scene: **Deception vs. his Insight (+3).** If caught, the
    hunger track rises (below) and he never trusts that person again.
- **Pilgrims** — the four who took the oath. He answers them courteously, as pilgrims, and asks them
  to put their questions through their priest. A small, playable friction; don't make it a wall.
- **The unsworn** — **Tath'Shar**. The priest **will not address them at all**: they did not kneel,
  and they killed a man on his threshold. He felt it. *(Rite 5, Remain pure.)* **The hunger, though,
  speaks to them — inside their head. Its private voice can reach only an unsworn mind.**
- **Jiyloo** knows him from her grandmother's story — *the priest who stayed* — and to her he is
  something close to a saint. **She kneels and cannot speak.** When she sees what he has become, it
  breaks her heart. If the hunger track needs bringing down and nobody else will do it, **she is the
  one who gets up and washes his feet** — the same thing the party did for her at the ants door.
- **The dog.** He sees Orkideh's dog and goes still. Doorne's attendants are dog-headed (9m; the
  helm in this room). *"A hound of the Weigher, in my king's house."* He is gentlest with the dog's
  keeper. The dog sits at his feet, unafraid. Do not explain why.

#### The hunger track — how stable he is right now

Keep a number from **0 to 6** in front of you. **Start at 3** — the Ember above is out. *(If the
party relights the Ember first, start at 2.)*

**Rises by 1** (each trigger once per scene unless noted):
- A living creature comes **within reach** (5 ft) of him — the first time.
- **Blood is drawn** in the room or on the ledge.
- A **weapon is drawn** in his presence.
- Anyone **touches the Scepter, the Coal or the sarcophagus** without his leave.
- He **catches a lie** (Insight +3).
- **The door to 9o is left open** — +1 for every ten minutes it stands open. He can smell the oasis.

**Falls by 1:**
- **His name is spoken** — *"Kamenhet."* **−2 the first time.** Nobody has said it in four hundred
  years. (Option C is where they get it.)
- **A prayer in one of the four names**, led aloud — once per person.
- **The door is closed behind them** — once. *"Thank you."*
- **His feet are washed**, or water from 9a's pools is brought to him — **−2**, and it requires
  coming within reach (+1), so it nets −1 and it is brave. *Arrive clean*, for the one who can't.
- **The Ember above is relit** — the baseline drops by 1 for good.

**What each level looks like:**
- **0–2 — the priest.** Lucid, courteous, precise. He bargains honestly and keeps his word.
- **3–4 — the cracks.** He loses the thread mid-sentence and the other voice slips into his: asks
  someone to come closer, to leave the door open "for air," for the thief on the ledge, for "only your
  hand." **DC 12 Insight** notices he is not himself. **A bargain struck at 3 or above was partly the
  hunger's** — something in its terms is poisoned (*The road north*, Part 2).
- **5 — the warning.** He grips the chair. The priest uses what he has left: *"Go. Go now. Close the
  door."* One round. Take it seriously.
- **6 — the hunger takes him.** He rises and attacks (Combat reference). **But the priest is still in
  there.** At the start of each of his turns, if anyone spoke **his name or one of the four names**
  since his last turn, that speaker rolls **DC 12 Religion or Persuasion**: on a success the track
  drops by 1 and he **loses that turn**, clutching the chair. At 5 or below he stops, horrified. **If
  anyone strikes him after he has stopped, it goes straight back to 6.** *(Same rule as 9e: words stop
  it, a blow after the words restarts it.)*
- **He cannot cross his own threshold.** The oath holds even when he doesn't. Anyone who gets out
  onto the ledge is safe from him — and the door only opens from the outside.

#### What he wants, in the order he asks

1. **News of the oasis.** *"Are the temples tended? Do travellers still drink at Sia?"* The honest
   answer — the temple's priest murdered, the Ember out, the oasis burned, its people taken as slaves
   — is everything he gave his death to prevent, and it failed two weeks ago. He felt it. Telling him
   is a gut-punch. Lying to him is easy and Insight +3.
2. **The fire.** Relight the Ember. It is how Doorne watched the oasis, and it is what has kept the
   hunger quiet for four centuries.
3. **The oasis's people back**, and the people who took them stopped.
4. **A priest for Temple al Sia.** The keeping was always meant to be **a dead priest below and a
   living one above**, tending the Ember. The living one is dead.
5. **Release.** **He will never ask.** Who would keep the cells? A party that offers it unprompted has
   understood him — and the answer to "who would keep the cells" is item 4.

#### Helpful levers — what an ally gives

- **Intelligence.** A month of the cult's talk through the stone: what they went to fetch (*drowned
  spirits in jars, from Ayatorrah*), Ashiq by voice, the dig, the screams when the wall took the men.
- **The tomb's guardians.** Through the Scepter he can turn them against Ashiq's column, or keep them
  still for the party. Stacks with the Ember ward (*the patrol returns*).
- **The Coal**, to relight the Ember.
- **The old script:** he can read the five-city etching on the Fire Beetle Lantern. "The roads between
  the five Holy Cities, as they ran when I was young" is enough.
- **The rites of Ayatorrah.** He was trained at the Temple of Mennuj, the seat of his order. He knows
  how the faithful enter it — right hand raised to ask Doorne to bless Mennuj, left hand raised to ask
  Doorne the Destroyer to leave your family in peace — and **that rite turns off the deadliest gates in
  *Forsaken Pyramid*** (*The road north*).
- **The king's goods.** He is Sia's steward. The **dagger +2** and the **dog-helm splint mail** are his to
  give to those who serve the oasis. *"The king paid his soldiers."*

#### The reward — the language engine

**He promises ancient language.** When the party has done him real service, he points to the machine
behind his chair: *"The priests learned the old tongue from it. You may too — when there are priests
again."*

- **The terms.** For the right service, he will have **the priests, when they return to Sia**, let the
  party use it. It is temple property, not his alone, and using it is a rite, not a favour.
- **The ceremony.** The priests carry the engine out of 9p in procession and set it up **in the great
  hall (9c)**, with a ceremony. It is the one thing that ever leaves his room with his blessing. He
  cannot come; he hears it through the stone.
- **Why it is deferred.** "When the priests return" ties the reward to his other asks: the oasis's
  people home, the Ember lit, and a priest for Temple al Sia. The reward is the reason to take the
  road north, and the reason to come back.
- **The danger — excessive at low level.** Touching it deals **d100 damage, flat.**
  - **If the roll is double the character's HP or more, they are permanently dead.** No death saves,
    no ordinary resurrection. This is the 5e massive-damage rule, read from full health.
  - Otherwise the damage resolves normally: a character dropped to 0 makes death saves as usual.
  - Anyone who survives gains the language: reads and speaks Bokkaran.
- **The odds — tell the players plainly.** A character survives only if the d100 roll is below
  double their HP.
  - At 15 HP, a roll of 30 or more kills: about a **71%** chance of permanent death.
  - At 20 HP: about **61%**.
  - At 30 HP: about **41%**.
  - At 40 HP: about **21%**.
  - At 50 HP or more: only a roll of 100 kills.
  - The Keeper says it his way: *"The young should wait. It was never meant for the young."*
- **The spider bracelet halves the engine's damage.** This is a campaign rule.
  - **One character per bracelet per engine.** A given bracelet protects only one person at a given
    engine. The party can pass a bracelet around, but once it has been used at this engine, it does
    nothing more here. It works again at the next engine. There are more engines in the world.
  - **The party has one bracelet** (strongbox, session 3, still unidentified), so at the Keeper's
    engine exactly one character can use it. Choosing who gets it is a scene in itself.
  - **The odds with the bracelet.** Halved damage only kills at double HP, so the d100 roll has to be
    four times the character's HP or more.
    - At 15 HP, a roll of 60 or more kills: about **41%**.
    - At 20 HP: about **21%**.
    - At 25 HP: only a roll of 100 kills, a **1%** chance.
    - At 26 HP or more: no roll can kill.
  - **How they find out:** someone who wears it near the engine feels the bracelet go cold and tight,
    as if bracing. The Keeper knows the old bands on sight: *"The priests wore those to the rite, when
    they had them."* Tsendur can say the same at Durgan's Rest if someone asks the right question.
- **Another engine waits in the Bridged City arc:** the one guarded by the ancient lizard Yormon on the
  Sea of Souls. Learning how this one works is the party's first lesson in what those machines cost.

#### Harmful levers — what makes him dangerous

- **The hunger**, and the track above. At 6 he is a wight: **energy drain** and **create spawn**.
- **The hunger's offer to Tath'Shar.** It speaks only to the unsworn mind, and it has been reading it.
  It knows Tath'Shar is hunting someone, and it says one true thing: ***"The one you are looking for
  collects things like the thing in my hands. She will come for it."*** (The Scepter is exactly the kind
  of prohibited artifact Maghiel hunts — `thread:tathshar-maghiel`.) Then the lie: **"Leave the door open
  when you go, and it's yours before she can take it."** It cannot deliver — the Scepter burns anyone
  who isn't faithful, and a freed hunger keeps no promises. **The ask can also be "bring me the thief"**
  (Tabbat). Phoenix decides whether Tath'Shar tells the party, bargains, or tells it to go to hell. *Run
  it as an opportunity for the character, never as a penalty for refusing the oath.*
- **If Phoenix is not at the table.** Don't play the offer through a backgrounded Tath'Shar; it only
  works as the player's secret. Two fallbacks:
  - **Hold it.** The hunger notices the unsworn mind and says nothing yet. Send Phoenix the note before
    their next session as a dream or a voice on the road: the Scepter line, and *"Come back and open my
    door."* That keeps a reason to return to Sia alive even if the party heads for Durgan's Rest.
  - **Turn it on the sworn.** With no unsworn mind to reach, the hunger works through the bargain
    instead. Any covenant made at 3 or above gets the poisoned term (*"one of them, alive"*, in *The
    road north*), and nobody hears the second voice at all.
- **His word, and his oath.** At 0–2 his word is good. **An oath on one of Doorne's four names binds him
  absolutely** — that is how he has held the hunger for four centuries. A party that learned the names
  can make any bargain solid by asking him to swear on one.
- **What the hunger asks for, at 3–4:** come closer, the door open, the thief, a hand.

#### How it can end — helpful to harmful

None of these is the right answer. All should stay reachable.

- **Covenant.** A bargain sworn on a name: he gives intelligence, the Coal, the guardians and the
  Ayatorrah rite, and promises the language engine when the priests return. The party takes up his
  vow **outside** the room he can't leave. → *The road north*, Part 2.
- **Steadying.** Relight the Ember with his Coal, close the door, and leave him to his vigil — stronger
  than before. The smallest good ending, and a real one.
- **Release.** A living priest takes up the keeping at Temple al Sia (Gereon? Jiyloo? someone found on
  the road?), the Scepter passes to them, and the Keeper is allowed to die — or the party finishes his
  washing (Part 2, Rule three, *finishing a washing*, with him as the subject). Earned, slow, and the
  best trophy in the dungeon.
- **Mercy by the blade.** They destroy him. **The keeping ends**: the guardians go still and stop
  answering anyone, the tomb stops filing, and the Scepter is lying on the floor for whoever comes next
  — and Ashiq is coming next.
- **The hunger's bargain.** Somebody takes the offer. The door stays open. That is a future session.
- **Walk away.** Close the door, leave him. The cult arrives with a mouth that will say the names (Part
  2, *The clock*) — and the Keeper only talks to priests.
- **Fight.** A table that wants a boss fight at the end of a long crawl gets one: walk in with steel out
  and the track is at 5 before anyone speaks.

### Round-by-round GM guide — the sphinx guard (beat 2a)

**Surprise round.** The guards are dozing in the sphinx's shade, not braced for a fight.
**Your move:** if the party scouts before engaging (no torches, no shouting), they get a full
surprise round automatically. If they announce themselves instead, run one alert cultist
fumbling for his scimitar rather than a clean ambush either way.

**Round 1 — Drop them fast.** HP 5 each, no real defense — two or three solid hits usually
ends this outright.
**Your move:** this isn't meant to be a fair fight, it's meant to be a fast one. Once half are
down, the rest should be looking for a chance to surrender or bolt, not fight to the last —
that's the "feckless individually" flavor paying off. Let the party choose to kill, capture,
or let one run.

**Round 2+ — Interrogation, not combat.** A captured cultist (or two, played off each other)
will talk — they're not devoted enough to die silent for a schedule they only half understand.
**Your move:** hand over both hooks somewhere in the conversation: the retrieval patrol is
due back within about a day (they're sure of that much), and some fuzzy gossip that "the
mystic" did something to the temple before the attack (they don't know what). Don't make the
party roll for this if they're actually roleplaying the interrogation — reward the scene, not
a Persuasion check.

# PART 2 — BEHIND THE SCREEN
*Read this before the session, not during it. Everything above is the playable layer; everything
below is why it works, and the detail to reach for when a player pushes on something.*

## The clock — revised in play (session 4)

**Ignore the source's flat 24 hours.** The party interrogated the sphinx guards and earned
something better, which is now canon:

> The cult's **main body left the oasis about a week ago to obtain magical items needed to
> penetrate the tomb.** They are expected back **within one to two days.**

That single sentence does three jobs the source's timer couldn't:

1. **It gives the party a deadline they own.** They did the work, they asked the question,
   they know. It's information, not GM fiat, and they can plan against it.
2. **It says there is a door the cult could not open.** A whole raiding party sat on this
   site for a week and left empty-handed. Decide what stopped them and put it in front of
   the party, because it is their one structural advantage over the cult. *Recommended:*
   the rite, not a lock — the tomb opens for anyone who will praise Doorne, and no Black
   Water cultist will. See `ashes-at-the-sphinx-depth-concepts.md` → Sketch 1 and 3. The
   party can walk through what the cult had to go shopping for.
3. **It tells them what the endgame is.** Not "some patrol shows up eventually" but
   "Ashiq comes back with the key, and we are on the clock to be done or to be waiting."

**What the cult went to fetch.** Pick one and commit before session 6:
- *Recommended, thematic:* **a vessel of the cult's own black water** — their sacrament, to
  drown the god's fire. It rhymes exactly with what Ashiq already did to Doorne's Ember above
  ground (smothered it in ash to blind the tomb's guardians *outside*); now she is coming to
  do it *inside*. It makes the Ember plot the spine of the whole adventure rather than a
  side quest, and it means the party and the cult are racing for the same object from
  opposite directions.
- *Mundane alternative:* a Chime of Opening, a scroll of *Knock*, a stolen pyramid-reliquary
  looted off some other Doornian shrine. Cheaper, less interesting, perfectly serviceable.
- ★ ***Session-6 recommendation — the drowned priest.*** This keeps the interrogation's "magical
  items needed to penetrate the tomb" exactly true. The cult's problem is that the doors that stop
  them (9e, and the door to 9p) want **spoken praise**, and no Elsrok-sworn fanatic will give it — not the rank and file, who believe, and not
  Ashiq, who would but can't be seen to (Rule one, *Would Ashiq say the words?*).
  Their solution is **a mouth that isn't theirs.** The main body went north to the Temple of Mennuj at
  Ayatorrah (*The road north*, below), where the cult's mystics bottle water spirits — **Essence of
  Hayawiyya** — that animate a corpse and **can speak in any language the body knew.** Ashiq already
  did this to the four Talifan guards at area 7. **The body she means to use is the murdered priest of
  Temple al Sia**, whom she killed the day before the raid and who still lies unburied in the temple.
  A Doornian priest's mouth, a water spirit's will: walk him down the stair and have him say the names.
  **And the Keeper only talks to priests.** The cult was not just planning to open his door; it was
  sending him a priest.
  - **The party can get ahead of it:** find the body in the temple and bury, burn or carry it below
    (Jiyloo will want him buried regardless — this gives that instinct a payoff they discover later);
    intercept the column on the stair, where its whole plan walks in the middle of it; or let the
    Keeper meet it — at 0–2 on his track he knows a drowned mouth when he hears one: *"That is not a
    priest. That is a well with a man in it."* At 3 or above, the hunger doesn't care.
  - Stats: *The Drowned Priest*, Combat reference.

**Running the deadline.** The guardians are ancient and faction-blind — they threaten
cultists exactly as much as the party, which is what keeps "beat the cult back" tense rather
than a simple race to loot first. If the party is still below when the cult arrives, the cult
comes down after them, and the tomb fights *both*. That is the best version of this fight;
don't be shy about letting it happen.

## Doorne's rules at the table — the pendant, the blessing, and the doors

*Three rules, one system, and together they are what turns this from a room-by-room crawl into a
place with a logic the party can learn and exploit.*

- **Rule one — the pendant.** Who the tomb lets past. This is why the party got in, and it is the
  answer waiting for the question they're going to ask about the cult.
- **Rule two — the blessing.** What it costs, per character, to keep walking without buying in.
- **Rule three — the doors.** How to read a room *before* opening it — and the theology that
  finally explains why a holy site is stuffed with undead. (Short version: it isn't infested.
  It's a filing system.)

All three are load-bearing from session 5 on.

## Rule one — Jiyloo's pendant

### The principle

**Doorne's credentials work on things that were built to be shown credentials.** The tomb has
two guardians whose whole purpose is to check who you are, and they can be satisfied. Nothing
else down here is checking anything. Mindless dead can't be presented with a symbol. Beasts
can't read one. A collapsing floor doesn't care. And a thing Doorne *cursed* is serving a
sentence, not standing a post — it has no credentials to check because it isn't a guard.

So: **the pendant is a front-door key, not a skeleton key.** That's what keeps the party's
advantage real and narrow at the same time.

### The table

### Why the cult had to leave and the party walked in

This is the answer to the question the party will eventually ask, and it's better than "because
you're the heroes." All of it is straight out of the source.

**Important: the cult got much further than "stopped at the front door."** They lived down here
for about a month and ranged over most of the tomb — the physical evidence of that is a whole
section below (*The cult's high-water mark*), and finding it is one of the best things in this
adventure. What they never managed was to reach the **tomb wing**: the temple, the knights, and
Sia. That is where the treasure is, and that is the wall they broke on.

1. **The Caryatid Columns are undying.** The source says so in as many words. The cult knocked
   them down and they got back up, every single time. 9a was passable for them, but it was a
   toll gate — it cost bodies on every crossing and bought them nothing permanent. That's a
   month of attrition on the *entrance*.
2. **The Stone Guardian in 9e is effectively immune to how the Black Water Cult fights.** AC 18;
   edged weapons do **quarter** damage; missiles do **zero** and are destroyed on impact; wood
   hafts break; immune to mind control; sees invisible. A column of scimitar-and-bow fanatics
   cannot meaningfully hurt it. They would need magic they didn't bring — which is exactly what
   the captured guards said they went to fetch.
3. **And the door was open the whole time, and it was labelled.** The lintel at 9e carries the
   *open mouth* — the sign that means *this one answers speech* — and the wall above the statues
   spells out the phrase in two scripts. Four words of praise to Doorne and the Stone Guardian
   stands aside. The source doesn't require sincerity — it says anyone who *makes
   praises to any of the names* passes unmolested. **The Black Water Cult is stopped by a door
   that opens if you say a sentence, and they would rather walk back into the desert and go
   shopping for a magic item.**
4. **And when the rite-locked route beat them, they tried to go around it — twice, and both
   attempts failed spectacularly.** They drove an eighteen-foot tunnel through raw rock toward
   the sealed wing, and the tomb's builders had anticipated exactly that (the diggers are *in
   the walls*). Then they found the secret door to Sia's tomb and worked it over with fire,
   steel, acid and at least one real spell, and left it without a scratch on it. Both sites are
   detailed under **The cult's high-water mark**, below, with investigation ladders — because
   this is material the party should get to *discover and enjoy*, not be told.

That third point is the one worth savoring, because it is precisely the cult you've been
building: individually dull, doctrinally rigid, institutionally capable. They serve Elsrok.
Praising the local god is apostasy and they will not do it — and none of the rank and file is
clever enough to notice that the god isn't checking.

### Would Ashiq say the words? ✅ Decided: she would, and she can't

**Ashiq heads the Black Water Cult, and she and the cult's other top ranks would say the four
names in a heartbeat** if it made their lives easier. Four syllables cost a pragmatist nothing.
What stops them is not faith. It is being seen.

- **The leader has to be the purest of them.** A cult holds together because its followers
  believe the people at the top are more than ordinary: closer to Elsrok, beyond fear, beyond
  bargaining. The fanatics' obedience rests on that belief, and nothing else.
- **One overheard prayer to the local god breaks it.** If a fanatic caught Ashiq praising
  Doorne to get through a door, she would stop looking holy and start looking mortal and craven:
  a woman who does whatever is convenient. Followers who see that do not quietly lose
  interest. Their attachment breaks all at once, and a cult built on fear and devotion turns
  on the person who was supposed to embody it. Every cult leader fears that moment.
- **So the cult she built has trapped her.** She has to perform devotion to Elsrok and
  obedience to Black Water doctrine at all times, in front of everyone, including at a door
  that would open for her in a breath. The rank and file won't say the names because they
  believe. The leaders won't say them because they must be seen believing.
- **That is why she went shopping for magic.** A drowned priest saying the names is not
  Ashiq praying to Doorne. It is a puppet doing something unclean on her behalf, which is
  doctrinally fine. *The clock*, below, has the details.

**At the table:**
- **Leverage.** A captured Ashiq, alone, out of sight of her fanatics, might say the names
  to save herself, and might bargain far more freely than her public face suggests. In front of
  even one fanatic, she will die before she does either. **Get her alone and she is a different
  person.**
- **A weapon.** If the party ever makes her say the names where her followers can hear — or
  just convincingly claims she did — they hit the cult where it is weakest. A rumour that
  "Ashiq prayed to Doorne at Sia" is worth more than her head.
- **Her lieutenants are in the same trap,** each watching the others. Any one of them could
  be turned in private and never in public.
- **The obsidian ring** lets someone in the party stand close enough to hear what the leaders
  say when they think only believers are listening.

**So the full answer, when the party asks:** the cult paid in blood for every yard the party is
walking for free, got most of the way, and then hit a door that opens for four words they will
not say. They are not coming back with a battering ram. They are coming back with magic, because
they have decided the problem is the door.

### Operating the pendant at the table

- **The pendant has to be visible and out front.** If Jiyloo is downed, at the back of the
  marching order, sent up the stair with the prisoner, or separated by a closing door, 9a stops
  being safe. Don't announce this; let the marching order matter.
- **Anyone can wear one.** The source gates 9a on the *symbol openly displayed*, not on belief —
  and 9e on *words spoken*, not on meaning them. **Doorne's credentials are cheap, and Jiyloo
  is just the only person who thought to bring them.** Temple al Sia above ground is carved with
  pyramids and the party has never been inside it. A trip back up to equip the whole party is
  a real, smart, available move, and it turns them from dependent on Jiyloo into self-sufficient.
  Let them find that out rather than suggesting it.
- **Don't let the pendant become a skeleton key.** The first time it visibly fails — the ants,
  or better, 9e, where a party that's grown confident strolls in wearing pyramids and gets hit
  by something that wanted a sentence instead — is the beat that teaches the rule properly.
- **Jiyloo watched them execute a prisoner on the plinth** an hour before she vouched for them
  to a god (`thread:the-prisoner-on-the-plinth`). She doesn't have to say anything about it.
  She is the believer who opened the door for them, and she was standing there.

## Rule two — the blessing at the door (*Unwelcome*)

*Steve's mechanic, session 5 onward. The pendant (above) is the tomb's **front door**; this is
how the party keeps walking once they're past it. Don't call it "Bane" at the table — someone
will ask whether it can be counterspelled. Call it **Unwelcome**, or "the god's cold eye."*

#### Where the offer happens

**In 9a, right after the ant scene.** ✅ *Played in session 5: everyone except Tath'Shar knelt
and took the oath. Tath'Shar is Unwelcome.* That's where the oath is taken, that's where the
aspect is picked (the four doors are on the table card above), and that's where a character
decides to go without.

That placement is deliberate: **9a is a vestibule.** Preparing pilgrims is literally what the
room was built to do, and offering the oath anywhere else wastes the architecture. (An earlier
draft of this packet put the offer at 9e; 9a is better and supersedes it. **9e is now where the
choice first *bites*** — a guardian that wants spoken words rather than a worn symbol, standing
over a ring of cult dead who wouldn't say them.)

Jiyloo makes the offer, and she is **worried, not preachy**:

> *"This is a holy room and there are more of them below us. Either we don't go on, or you take
> the blessing first — kneel in a doorframe and say: 'I take the goodness of Doorne into my
> heart.' I can't make him protect you. I can only tell you how to ask."*

Play her reluctant and a little frightened rather than devout-and-certain. If she comes across
as sanctimonious, the holdout player's resentment lands on **her** instead of on the god, and
you lose the friction you actually wanted.

#### The rule

**Per character, not per party.** Each PC kneels or doesn't, in front of everyone.

- **Blessed:** nothing happens. You are simply not being resisted. (No bonus — this is not a
  buff, it's the absence of a penalty. Say so plainly or players will feel cheated.)
- **Blessed, and at a door:** you also carry that door's boon for the whole delve (the four
  aspects are on the table card, above).
- **Unwelcome:** **−1d4** on attack rolls, saving throws, and ability checks — **but only in
  Doornian conflicts and against Doornian obstacles.**

**The scope of that carve-out is the single most important line in this rule.** It applies to:
the guardians, the marked rooms and what's inside them, the wards and traps the priests built,
and the tomb itself resisting a search. It does **not** apply to anything that isn't Doorne's —
not the gargoyle, not the Dark Creeper, not the scarab swarm, not the ants once they've settled,
not a stuck door or a cult-installed lock, and above all **not the Black Water Cult.** When
Ashiq's column comes down that stair, the holdout fights at full strength beside everyone else.

Two reasons this matters more than it looks:

1. **It keeps the holdout playable.** A flat −1d4 on every d20 taxes the rogue two to three times
   harder than anybody else, and your likely holdout **is** the rogue. Narrowed like this, they
   are completely fine in roughly half of what's coming.
2. **It changes what the penalty means.** It reads as *this place doesn't want me* rather than
   *the GM is taxing my character sheet* — which is the whole difference between a grumbling
   player and a resentful one.

**Adjudicate with one question: is the thing resisting you Doorne's work?**

**The choice is made once and then rides.** Ask in 9a. After that, **do not re-offer at every
door** — eleven of the fourteen remaining rooms qualify, and asking eleven times turns the best
beat in the dungeon into a chore. Just apply it, silently, and let the player feel it. Say the
Doornian-only scope out loud once, plainly, the first time it bites, and then never mention it
again.

**One place to change your mind: 9m, the Temple of the Sphynx.** An actual temple with an actual
podium and an altar. A holdout can kneel there and convert; someone blessed can renounce it
there, or **re-dedicate to a different one of the four aspects**, if they want to. Nowhere else. This preserves "no take-backs at the doorframe" while
giving a worn-down player a destination rather than a dead end — which matters, because the
whole point is attrition, and attrition with no exit is just punishment.

#### Escalation — widen the scope, never the number

You want to wear a holdout down, but a flat −1d4 forever is *constant*, not attritional. Keep
the die at 1d4 and escalate what the refusal costs in the fiction:

| After | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Rooms 1–2 of refusal** | Just the −1d4. Nothing is said about it. |
| **Rooms 3–4** | The tomb notices. Carved eyes track them across a room; guardians that roll for a target pick them; they're the one the room orients toward. Narrate it, don't roll for it. |
| **Rooms 5+** | **Jiyloo's pendant stops covering them.** It shielded the whole party at 9a. It now shields only the blessed. The holdout is the one person a credential-checking guardian comes for — alone, while everyone else stands there watching. |

That last step is the squeeze, and it never touches the arithmetic. It also pays off the
pendant rule above: the credential was always *hers*, and the god has finally started asking
who else is actually asking.

#### What refusing buys — and it has to buy something

Declining a foreign desert god is **correct characterization** for a drow noble who kills
prisoners. If refusal is pure cost, the player learns that playing their character is a tax.
Give them these, and let them discover both rather than announcing them:

- **Inside Doornian rooms, the blessed have disadvantage on Deception.** They took the god into
  themselves in a room where he is listening. The unblessed keep their tongue — which a rogue
  will value more than a +2.
- **The hunger in 9p can only reach an unsworn mind.** This is the real payoff, at the climax.
  The Keeper — the priest — will not address the one who refused the oath and killed on his
  threshold. **But the hunger inside him speaks to them, privately, in their head**, and to
  nobody else: it has something true to tell them and something false to offer. Six rooms of
  grumbling cash out as the one character the monster talks to — and whether they tell the
  party is up to them. See *Running the Keeper* → *the hunger's offer to Tath'Shar*.

**The blessing does NOT make you faithful.** It's a courtesy at a doorframe, not conversion —
so it does **not** protect a bearer from the Scepter of Ice's 4d6 (that wants the real thing),
and it does not satisfy An-Zefful, who isn't checking. Keep this line firm or the blessing
quietly solves the whole dungeon.

**Does it persist past the tomb?** Old-school convention for this campaign says don't tell
them. It leaves a mark; Doorne remembers who said the words. Jiyloo is honest that she doesn't
know what that means either. Decide later.

#### Which rooms are holy

| Room | Holy? | Note |
|---|---|---|

| **9d** Skeletal Guardians | ✅ | Sacrifice before a skull-pyramid. Let Jiyloo say it plainly: *this is Doornian, and it is not good.* |

| **9n** Tomb Guardians | ✅ | Pyramid-hatted statues, the Griffon Blades. |

#### The Tath'Shar problem, handled carefully

Tath'Shar executed a bound prisoner on this god's plinth (`thread:the-prisoner-on-the-plinth`),
and one of Doorne's four names is *the All-Knowing*. There is an obvious beat here: **the
blessing doesn't take** the first time they say the words.

It's strong and it's risky. It reads as the GM rigging the mechanic against one player, and at
a table with a brand-new PC that's a bad first impression. If you use it:

- Make it **visible and fixable**, never a permanent lockout. Something concrete and cheap —
  bury the man, or speak his name, or let the surviving prisoner go.
- Make it happen **in front of everyone**, so it's a scene rather than a private penalty.
- Give Phoenix the out of playing it as *interesting* rather than *unfair* — Tath'Shar isn't
  being judged for being drow, or trans, or a rogue. They're being judged for the specific
  thing they chose to do, in the open, an hour ago. That's a story about a character's actions,
  which is the good kind.

If the table's energy isn't there for it, skip it entirely and just run the −1d4. The mechanic
works fine without it.

## Rule three — reading the doors (the skull grammar)

*This is the answer to the question that actually breaks this dungeon's fiction if nobody asks
it: **why is a holy site full of undead?** It is not an infestation. It is the filing system.*

### The theology, in four sentences

Doorne does not destroy. Neither does the desert — leave a thing in the sand and it does not rot
away, it dries, and stays, and waits. A faith grown in that country does not believe in
annihilation; it believes in **keeping**. What is wrong is not burned out of the world, it is
marked, shut, and held — either forever, or until it is fit.

So the priests of Sia were never besieged by the things behind these doors. **They were their
keepers.** They came down the stair every day, walked the gold road, opened the temple, said the
offices, left grain at eleven thresholds, and went home to supper. They no more fought what was
in those rooms than a gaoler fights his prisoners.

And the king is buried here for the same reason the prison is here: **you bury your king where
the god is already looking.** The tomb and the gaol share a building because they share an
audience.

> **The inversion, and it's worth landing on the party when they get it:** they think they are
> robbing a king's tomb. They are walking through **a prison with a king buried in it.**

### The grammar — four signs, and that's the whole language

Every sign is built on the pyramid. Keep it to these four; a symbol system players can actually
hold in their heads is worth ten times one that's complete.

| Sign | What it looks like | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **The clean pyramid** | Plain, whole, often with sun, wheat or water | His. Worship, passage, servants who check credentials (*Rule one*). |
| **The sealed** | Skull on the **outer flank**, facing **away** from the pyramid — i.e. looking at *you* in the corridor | **Containment.** What is in here is held, and will be held. Turned away from the god. Nothing about it is going to change. Do not open. |
| **The washing** | Skull set **into** the face like an inlaid stone, turned **up toward the apex** — looking at *the god* | **Purification.** What is in here is being worked on, and the work is meant to end in release. Also do not open — but for a different reason. |
| **No passage** | Pyramid whose base-road is **struck through** by one deep chisel stroke | Not a curse and not the damned. Simply: *the route does not go this way.* This is what's over the door of **9b**, and the builders meant it kindly. |
| **The open mouth** ★ | A small worn mouth carved **beneath** a clean pyramid | **This threshold answers speech, not display.** A worn pendant does nothing; say one of the four names. It is on **9e**, on **9m's** threshold (which also has a hand-worn basin cut into the stone), and — the one that matters — **on the secret door into 9p.** |

> **The open mouth is the keystone, so don't bury it.** It makes 9e *fair* — a party that has
> formed the habit of reading lintels walks through a fight that killed eight cultists. And it
> makes the last door in the dungeon openable in one sentence, after a month of siege work
> failed on it, because the Black Water Cult could not read a mouth. Five signs is still a
> language a table can hold in its head; this is the fifth.

**Teach it in layers, don't hand it over:**

- **Free — Jiyloo.** She is a lay believer, not a priest. She knows the *rule* and not one word of
  the reason: **"You don't go through a skull door."** Her grandmother told her that. She cannot
  tell them why, cannot read the difference between the two skull marks, and will be honest that
  she can't. Perfect — the safety rule is free, the understanding still has to be earned.
- **DC 10, in 9c (Hall of the Great God).** The source already gives you every variation of the
  pyramid painted on this hallway's walls, *including the ones with a skull on the side.* There
  are two kinds of pyramid here. That's the whole Tier-1 finding.
- **DC 13, once they've seen a marked door.** The skulls aren't only decoration — they're
  **over doorways**, and the gold-flecked pilgrim road never once turns into a room that has one.
  The architecture is the safety system.
- **DC 16 / Religion / a priest.** The two skull orientations are different words. *Sealed* and
  *washing.*
- **No roll at all — just noticing.** See *The mark is on the inside*, below.

### What the priests' procedure looked like, and what's still lying there

These are cheap physical details that make the whole system visible without a single check:

- **The thresholds.** A shallow stone offering-dish is set into the floor **outside** every marked
  door — never inside. Priests left grain, oil, or a coin, and did not cross. **The dishes are
  still there, and so is the last offering ever made in them**, four hundred years dried to
  nothing. Somebody did the rounds on an ordinary morning and then the empire ended.
- **The doors have handles on the outside only.** One line, and it tells the entire story.
- **A raised stone sill** at each marked door. You do not walk into one of these rooms by
  accident; you step over, deliberately, having decided to.
- **The gold road never turns in.** The gold-flecked black marble of 9a's hall is the priests'
  route, and it routes *around* every marked room in the tomb. A party that simply follows the
  floor never meets a single one of them. That is not a trick — it is what the floor is for.

### What this changes

**1. Avoidance becomes a real, legible strategy.** The party can read a door before opening it.
That is agency, and it's the reward for having paid attention to walls.

**2. Every marked door the cult opened, they opened out of ignorance.** They could not read any
of this. Their dead are behind doors that said *no* in a language nobody among them had bothered
to learn — while the Doornian instructions for getting *through* 9e were chiselled on the wall in
plain sight and they tried to destroy them rather than read them. That is the same failure twice,
and the party can spot it.

**3. The marks label rooms, not objects — and this is load-bearing.** **9l** (the golden scarab)
has a *clean* room: sun, dancing figures, joined hands. It is one of the good rooms. The curse in
there is on a **thing**, not on the place. Let the system fail exactly once, exactly here, so the
party learns the marks are a map and not a guarantee. Same for the **gargoyle in 9j** and the
**Dark Creeper in 9n** — unmarked rooms with monsters in them, because those monsters *moved in
later*. The tomb only labels its own work.

**4. Killing what's in a *washing* room is a real moral problem.** The purification is running.
It has been running for four centuries and it was going to end in release. Six seconds of
violence ends the process instead of completing it. **Jiyloo will say so** — not as a lecture,
just badly, the way someone says a thing they were raised on and can't argue for. Let it sit.

**5. An unblessed PC is, to a sealed room, indistinguishable from its contents.** This is the
in-fiction reason *Rule two*'s penalty bites hardest exactly where it's most dangerous. Don't
explain it; it just gives the −1d4 a face.

### Room by room

| Room | Mark over the door | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **9d** Skeletons | **Sealed** | The people the state killed in Doorne's name — the sacrifices in this room's own frescoes. **There is no rite for the murdered.** They can't be purified because they did nothing wrong; they're held because they are *angry*. A theological embarrassment, behind a door. This is also the only marked room whose occupants **chase** — they want out, and they have a case. |
| **9k** Zombies | **Washing** | "Doorne's less faithful followers, serving him better in death than in life." Penance through labor. The work is meant to finish. It has not. |
| **9i** Ghouls | **Washing** | The dakhma — a purification structure, which the source already calls out and which is *literally what a tower of silence is for.* Four accursed dead, being cleansed, four hundred years in and not clean yet. The clearest example of the category. |
| **9h** Ogre | **Sealed** | Not even a Doornian. An outsider that had to go somewhere. Filed without ceremony. |
| **9g** Coffer Corpse | **Sealed — and the mark is newer than the others** | Per the source, the statue in here is **a bandit who was caught looting the tomb.** Which means: **this room is still being filled.** The tomb has gone on filing people for four centuries with no priests, no ceremony and no supervision. See below. |
| **9b** Ants | **No passage** | Not cursed. The builders are telling you the road doesn't go this way, and they were right. |
| **9f** An-Zefful | **Defaced — you can see there *was* a mark, and cannot read which** | Somebody destroyed this room's frescoes, bloodied the north wall, and took the sign with them. **Best answer: he did it himself.** He has a death wish and four hundred years, and he has been trying to erase his own sentence. The party cannot tell whether he is *sealed* or *washing* — whether killing him ends a punishment or interrupts a redemption. Leave that unresolved. It is the whole reason his death is worth a line of narration. |
| **9e** Statue Guardians | **Clean + the open mouth** | The threshold that wants *words*. Also where the cult's dead are piled at exactly the guardian's reach. |
| **9l** Scarab | **Clean pyramid, wheat at the base — no skull, no sill, no dish** | A genuinely good room. The curse is on the *object*. The lesson that marks label rooms, not things. |
| **9m** Temple | **Clean + the open mouth + a hand-worn basin in the threshold** | The only place to convert, renounce or re-dedicate. |
| **secret door → 9p** | **Clean + the open mouth** | Never locked. Opens for four words. The cult spent days on it with fire, steel, acid and a spell. |
| **9j** Gargoyle / **9n** Dark Creeper | **None** | Squatters. Moved in after. |
| **9o** False treasure | **None** | Sia's vanity and Sia's trap, not Doorne's work. |
| **9p** Tomb of Sia | **None outside — a recut skull inside** | ← *read the next section* |

### The mark is on the inside ★

**9p has no skull over its door.** There is a wight in it.

A party that has learned this grammar can walk up to the deepest room in a building whose entire
purpose is marking and holding the damned, and notice that **nobody marked this one from outside.**
Doorne files everything. Did Doorne not file this?

**Let them think that.** It is half right. Then let them through the door and have them look up.

The **inner** lintel — the only mark in the complex carved to be read from *within* a room —
carries a pyramid and a skull, and **the skull has been recut over and over**: turned out
(*sealed*), set back in (*washing*), out again, the stone gouged pale around it. **The priest
inside filed himself.** He bound himself in with an oath on the four names, behind a door that
only answers praise from outside, to keep the hunger that being a wight put in him away from the
oasis he gave his death to protect. For four hundred years he has been arguing with himself, in
stone, about whether he is being purified or only held.

That is the Keeper in one image, and a party that has paid attention all evening gets it with
**no roll at all**. It is a much better door into a conversation than 28 hit points.
→ *Running the Keeper*, after the 9p room card.

### The tomb is still working ★

9g's inmate is a tomb-robber who arrived *after* the priests stopped coming. Somebody cut a new
mark and filed him. **The building did not do that on its own: the Keeper did**, through the
guardians that still answer the Scepter of Ice in his hands.

**Played in session 5:** the party found *the new statue* — a Black Water cultist, petrified
mid-stride in a corridor, a ring of black obsidian on his raised stone finger — and took the
ring. That is the Keeper's most recent filing. Nobody has told them so.

### Optional — finishing a washing

If you want a non-combat resolution with real weight, the *washing* rooms can be **completed**
rather than emptied. This is optional and you don't need it; take it if your table is the kind
that would rather solve than swing.

**9i, the dakhma, is the one to use.** The rite was interrupted, not failed: the bodies are no
longer laid out correctly (they're up and walking), the offices haven't been said in four
centuries, and the fire that should burn at a dakhma's centre went out when the Ember did.
Completing it needs three things the party can actually get:

1. **The bodies returned to the circles** — which means putting the ghouls down without
   destroying them, or waiting them out, or something clever. Hard, and it should be.
2. **Their names.** Written somewhere findable — try the frescoes in 9c, or a priest's roll in
   the temple at 9m.
3. **Fire that is Doorne's.** Not a torch. **Sia's Coal** (depth-concepts doc, Sketch 4) or a
   brand lit from the restored Ember above ground.

That is a genuinely lovely use for the Coal, it gives the Ember plot a second job, and finishing
a four-hundred-year rite that the god started and the world interrupted is a better trophy than
anything in the treasure chests. **Reward it in standing, not in gold** — which is the currency
this party chose.

## The cult's high-water mark — what they did, and where they broke

*New material, session 5 onward. The party is about to walk, for free, through ground that
cost the Black Water Cult a month and nineteen people. **Let them find that out and enjoy it.***

### Tone: play it as a comedy of failure with a body count

This should be **fun to discover**, not grim. The Cult spent four weeks down here being
outwitted by dead architects. Somebody scraped gold leaf off a statue for six hours and got ten
gold pieces of it. Somebody snapped a pick-head off in a door that doesn't have a lock. The
right register is the party finding a rival's abandoned project and slowly realizing how badly
it went.

Underneath that, one cold fact worth landing without comment: **nineteen of them died, and
somebody kept giving the order.** Individually these people were fools. The thing that kept
sending them into the rock was not. (`world/factions.md` → Black Water Cult → *the
institutional contradiction*.)

### How old is it

The expedition camped here **about four weeks**; the main body pulled out **a week ago**
(the party already knows this from the interrogation). So nothing is fresh:

- Firepits **cold to the bottom**, ash packed and grey.
- Blood dried brown-black and flaking, not red.
- Bodies **desiccated, not rotten** — the air down here is bone-dry desert air, so the dead
  are leathery and odorless. No flies, no stink. This is a real and useful detail: it means
  bodies stay *readable* (wounds, gear, expressions) weeks later.
- Rations gone hard, waterskins gone stiff, tunnel dust settled flat over everything.
- **The dog notices what the party doesn't.** Orkideh's dog will work the cold campsite,
  the tunnel mouth, and the sealed door without being asked. Give it one free find per site
  — it is a legitimate reason to hand the party a clue without a roll.

### Site 1 — The staging camp (9c, Hall of the Great God)

The widest defensible space in the tomb, so it's where they lived. Bedrolls in rows, three
cold firepits, crates broken down for fuel, and **a latrine trench dug into the northeast
corner, directly beneath a fresco of Doorne receiving the harvest.** Leave that ambiguous —
deliberate desecration, or four weeks of men who didn't want to walk further? Jiyloo will have
an opinion and it will not be a calm one.

**Free, no roll:** the scale of it. Twenty-plus people lived here for weeks.

**DC 10 Investigation** — *the tally.* Scratched into the plaster of a fresco: **thirty-one
marks in groups of five.** Nineteen of them are struck through. Anyone can work out what the
second number means.

**DC 13 Investigation** — *the dispatch.* A folded orders sheet in an oilcloth wallet, under a
bedroll. In the Cult's own script: needs a reader, a translation, or someone clever with
Elsrokian liturgy. This is the strategic tease — **read it aloud and explain nothing:**

> *"Elsrok's blessing on the work. The Sia stone is wanted before the flood-season councils —
> Hothme first, then the Cities. Azhar writes the tribes will quarrel over the wells again this
> summer with or without us, which is convenience, not luck. Do not let the Vanic at Tel A'bib
> learn there is anything at Sia worth a garrison. Four fires, one hearth. — S."*

Four place-names, no context: **Hothme, the Holy Cities, the tribes and their wells, the Vanic
at Tel A'bib.** That is the whole doctrine in a sentence and the party cannot possibly decode
it yet. Do not help them. If they take the sheet, it is worth real money and real credit to
Elmingwed, to Feltan, or to a Vanic officer — which is a reputation payoff for a party that
chose reputation.

**No roll, if anyone searches bedding** — *the letter home.* Half-finished, never sent, in
plain trade script: somebody's wages, somebody's sister, a complaint about the food and the
digging and a man named Tabbat who won't stop praying out loud. The party executed one of these
people on the plinth yesterday. **Do not editorialize. Read it and move on.**

**There is no money here, and that is itself a clue.** A cult does not leave its pay-chest
lying in a hole — and more to the point, **they are coming back.** The camp isn't abandoned,
it's *left in place*: valuables and personal kit went out with the main body a week ago, and
the bulk of the expedition stayed behind because they expect to be sleeping in it again within
a day or two.

**DC 10 Investigation:** the bedding is rolled, not scattered. The firepits are banked, not
kicked out. The stores are stacked. **Nobody who leaves for good tidies up.** This is the same
clock the interrogation gave them, confirmed from a second direction — and it hands a thinking
party a genuine option: *they know exactly where twenty cultists are going to be, and when.*
Ambush, sabotage, poison the stores, or simply be somewhere else. Let them cook.

**What's actually here to take:** bulk water and oil, hard rations, rope, shoring timber,
serviceable camp gear, a spare tent's worth of canvas — real logistics for a party running a
clock, worth more than coin would be. Plus the documents above and the mapcase below.

**The mapcase.** A charcoal route-sketch of everything the expedition mapped: corridors, room
shapes, the rooms they cleared, **the ant room crossed out with a scrawled word**, two rooms
left blank because they never reached them, and one corridor drawn wrong. Hand this over
happily — a party that can navigate makes better decisions, and the blanks are the interesting
part. (Pairs with the depth-concepts doc, Sketch 6.)

### Site 2 — The dig (9j, Hall of the Forgotten Guardians) ★ the centerpiece

**Why this room.** Read the source's description of 9j again: *"The walls of this hall are
decorated with humans working in mines and building mighty tombs, each leader wearing a pyramid
hat."* The one room in the tomb decorated with **pictures of people cutting stone** is the room
where the Cult decided to cut stone. The builders put the warning in the decor and the Cult dug
straight through it.

**Placement:** drive the tunnel from whichever wall of 9j backs onto the sealed tomb wing on
your Roll20 layout. If your geometry doesn't work, 9k or 9n do the job just as well — but 9j
is worth some map-fudging for the fresco alone.

#### What it looks like

A tunnel mouth hacked through worked stone into raw rock, **about eighteen feet deep**, driven
dead straight on a bearing that aims at the sealed wing. Shoring timber — *wagon timber, cut
down from the burned wagons at the El-Alouph shop above ground.* A spoil heap. Baskets, a
sledge, picks laid down in a neat row by someone who expected to come back.

And **seven people in the walls.**

> *Read-aloud:* "Fifteen feet in, a man stands in the left-hand wall, sunk to the waist,
> mummified, one hand still closed on a pick handle that is also in the stone. Further along,
> only a shoulder and half a face. At the tunnel face, there is a hand — fingers spread, palm
> flat, flush with the rock like something fossilized. The stone around them isn't broken or
> healed. It's just stone, and they're in it."

They are at **increasing depths of absorption the deeper you go** — which tells a story if
anyone thinks about the order.

#### The fix, and the warning that was always there

**DC 12 Investigation (or anyone who looks at the walls near the tunnel mouth):** the fresco
here isn't a scene of mining. The miners are being **taken into** the rock they're cutting —
hands and forearms already merged with the face — and the pyramid-hatted overseers behind them
are watching it happen and **smiling.** The Doornian priests anticipated exactly this and
painted the answer on the wall four hundred years ago.

**DC 15 Investigation:** the absorption depth runs *against* the direction of the dig. The
shallowest body is nearest the tunnel mouth — the **last** man in — and the deepest is at the
face. They didn't all die at once. **They kept sending people in, one after another, watching
each one be eaten by the wall, and the next one went anyway.** That is the Black Water Cult in
one image: the diggers were fools and the woman giving the order was not.

**DC 17 Investigation / any Stonecunning:** Gereon's dwarf is made for this. The rock is not
enchanted *stone* — it is enchanted *against cutting*. Break it and it takes you. Don't, and
it is inert rock you can stand in all day.

#### Live hazard — telegraph it hard, do not gotcha anyone

Anyone who **cuts, chisels, picks, or magically breaks** stone anywhere in the tunnel starts
to be taken. Escalate visibly:

- **First tap of a tool:** the tool sticks. It comes away from the wielder's hand, not the wall.
  Gone forever. *Nobody is hurt.* This is your warning shot and it should be unmistakable.
- **If they persist:** flesh in contact with the cut begins to go. CON save (DC 13); on a fail,
  restrained and sinking, one round for allies to haul them clear. On a second consecutive
  fail, they lose the hand.
- **Never a save-or-die.** The point is a rule the party can see, understand and choose to obey
  — not a trap. If your players are the type to test it once with a dagger, that's the correct
  reaction and they should get the correct result.

Anything that doesn't cut — walking, digging out packed spoil, carrying a body out — is
completely safe. Say so if they ask.

#### Loot

- **Good steel mining tools**, a dozen of them, worth carrying out (~40 gp, and genuinely
  useful if the party ever wants to shift rubble somewhere that *isn't* cursed).
- On the waist-deep man — reachable, he's only half in — **two things in the same pocket.**

  **A black obsidian serpent ring.** The Cult's standard membership token
  (`faction:black-water-cult`; the compendium already records serpent rings and robes being used
  for infiltration in the prior run). Identifiable, clean loot, and the single most useful object
  in this dungeon for a party that ever wants to walk into a Cult operation and be taken for
  staff. Don't suggest it. Let them arrive at it.

  **A twist of cloth holding about 15 gp of gold filings and leaf.** He had been scraping it out
  of the gold-flecked seams of 9a's floor and off bronze door furniture, a little at a time, for
  weeks — **hidden in his own clothing, against orders.** He was stealing from his own
  expedition, in fistfuls of dust, and he died standing in a wall with it on him.

  > That is the whole tomb in one pocket. **Fifteen gold pieces**, in a place where the things
  > nobody can carry out are worth tens of thousands. Don't say any of this out loud — just
  > describe the twist of cloth and let it land. It also pairs against the scraped gold-leaf heap
  > in 9o (below): **that** one is the expedition's sanctioned, pooled, pointless day of work.
  > **This** one is a man robbing them in secret for a third of it.
- A foreman's wax tablet, mostly bearings and depths, with one line in plain script at the
  bottom: *"tell her it is rock and not rock."*

### Site 3 — The humiliation (9o, the False Treasure Room)

They found the treasure room. It was the false one. Then it ate four of them.

**On arrival the floor is already collapsed** — they moved the golden statues without finding
the switch, exactly as the source describes, and dropped the whole floor onto the spikes. Four
mummified cultists are down there, ten feet below, on old poisoned spikes whose poison had
already been dead for centuries. **The two-foot ledge in front of the secret door survives**,
which per the source drops the secret-door check to CL 2 — so the party finds the door almost
for free, and that is fine, because the door is the point.

**Free, no roll — the comedy.** The ivory chests are smashed open and the birch underneath is
showing. Three hundred "silver" pieces lie scattered where somebody weighed them and realized
they were shavings. The bronze throwing daggers have been thrown — at a wall, in disgust. And
in the corner is a **neat heap of gold leaf**, scraped painstakingly off both statues by
someone who spent most of a day on it and came away with about ten gold pieces' worth and a
worm-eaten birch statue.

**DC 10:** a curse scratched into the plinth in the Cult's script. It is not liturgical.

#### The door they could not open

On the ledge, the secret door to 9p. It has been **worked on for days** and it is the best
image in this whole section:

> *Read-aloud:* "The door is black with soot in a fan shape, as though something burned against
> it for hours. Every edge has been chiselled at. The head of a pick is snapped off and still
> wedged in the top seam. There are pale runnels down the face where acid ran. And when you
> wipe the soot away with your sleeve, the stone underneath is perfect. Not scratched. Not
> chipped. Perfect."

**DC 13 Investigation:** fire, steel, and acid, in that order, over several days.
**DC 16 Investigation / DC 14 Arcana:** and at least one genuine spell — there is arcane
scoring around the frame with no corresponding physical damage. Somebody with real training
worked this door personally and got nowhere. (Ashiq. Don't name her; let them meet her.)

**And it isn't locked.** There is no mechanism. Look at its lintel: **a clean pyramid with an
open mouth carved beneath it** — the same sign as 9e. *This threshold answers speech.* It opens
for four words and it does not open for people who will not say them. The cult spent days on a
door whose instructions were carved above it, in a complex where they had already defaced the
identical instructions in 9e rather than read them. **This is the thing the main body went
shopping for** — they left the desert to buy a way through a door that opens if you say four
words, because saying them is apostasy. Everything the party has learned about the pendant and
the blessing converges here (see *Doorne's rules at the table*).

If the party opens it in front of the evidence of a month's failure, that is the payoff for
this entire section. Give them the moment.

**Loot from the spiked pit:** four cultists' gear and personal coin (~25 gp between them), and
the **Fire Beetle Lantern** (below), still in the hand of the one nearest the ledge.

### Site 4 — Where they actually broke (9e, the Stone Guardian) ★ run this first

**This is the next room the party is heading for, and it is the best joy-beat in the dungeon.**

The Stone Guardian's plinth is **ringed with the Cult's dead** — seven or eight of them,
mummified, in a rough arc at exactly the radius of its reach. Scimitars lie **snapped and
notched into uselessness**. Arrows and crossbow bolts are scattered everywhere in splinters,
none of them anywhere near the statue, because per the source missiles do nothing here and are
*destroyed on impact*. Somebody's spear haft is in three pieces.

The statue is back on its plinth, unmarked, exactly as it has stood for four hundred years.

**Free, no roll:** they died here, a lot of them, hitting something that never moved from its
spot.
**DC 10:** the wounds and the weapon damage say the Cult attacked this thing **more than once,
on separate days.** They came back and tried again.
**DC 14 Religion, or Jiyloo, or anyone who reads the inscription:** the words are still chiselled
into the south wall above the statues, in Doornian script and hieroglyphs, and they have been
**deliberately defaced** — gouged at, scraped, attacked with a chisel — and are still perfectly
legible. *"Praise Doorne the All-Knowing and live."*

They read the instructions. They tried to destroy the instructions. Then they went back into the
desert to fetch magic.

**And then Jiyloo makes the blessing offer, standing in that doorway, over those bodies.** That
is the ideal staging for *Rule two* — the party is being asked to kneel and say four words in a
room that is visibly full of people who wouldn't.

### Site 5 — Small signs, scattered anywhere you like

Salt these wherever the party wanders; they cost nothing and they make the tomb feel *used*:

- **Chalk route-marks** at corridor junctions in the Cult's script, with several rooms crossed
  out and one marked with something that is plainly a warning. Free navigation aid.
- **A cairn** of five stacked helmets in a side passage. No bodies — the bodies are in the

- **A dropped waterskin** in a corridor, still half full and still sealed, because the person
  carrying it did not come back for it.
- **One room stripped bare** of everything portable and worth under 5 gp — lamp brackets,
  bronze fittings, door furniture. A month is a long time and they got bored.

### The lamp *(the trinket)* — a B'rath Fire Beetle Lantern

**This is not cult-made and it is not new.** It is an existing piece of Ajiibwan kit —
`world/artifacts.md` already carries it as the **Fire Beetle Lantern ("Cool Fire Lantern")**,
and the **Fire Beetle Oil** that fuels it as B'rath's trade staple. *(An earlier draft of this
packet invented a sinister cult-made "Drowned Lamp." Discard that; the real item is better and
it's already in the world.)*

**What it is.** An expensive brass-and-glass lantern out of **[[loc:brath]]**, the village of
fire beetles, a few days south of Tel A'bib. It burns **fire-beetle oil**, which the B'rathian
children harvest in the caves as their coming-of-age ordeal. The flame is **cool and heatless**,
steady, and it does not gutter.

**Why anyone pays what it costs** — three properties, all of them practical:
- **It doesn't smoke.** Nothing to see, nothing to smell, nothing to follow.
- **It doesn't need air.** It burns in sealed rooms, flooded workings, and — the reason it is
  genuinely prized — **beneath the Sea of Souls**, where an ordinary flame is both useless and
  an advertisement to whatever is out there.
- **It burns for about a week on one tank.** It is *not* inexhaustible. Track the fuel; refills
  are had at B'rath (the village will fill lanterns for a fee) or from a serious outfitter.

**Why the cult had one, and what that tells the party.** This is **issued expedition equipment,
not personal property** — it costs more than a rank-and-file cultist earns in a year. A month of
work underground in a dry tomb is exactly the job it's for. **DC 12 Investigation, or anyone who
recognizes the make:** this expedition was *well funded*, and somebody quartermastered it
properly. There were almost certainly several; the rest walked out with the main body. This one
stayed because the man carrying it went into the pit.

**The etching on the base — do not explain this.** Worked into the brass foot: **five marks in a
rough ring, joined by lines.** The lines are not decorative — they bend and fork exactly the way
real roads do. Beside each mark is a short word in a script **nobody at the table can read**, and
nobody the party currently knows can read either.

It is a map of the **five Holy Cities** and the roads that actually connect them. The party
cannot possibly know that yet. Let it sit as an unexplained curiosity for as long as it takes —
this is a piece that **falls into place** the day they first stand somewhere and recognize the
shape. `world/artifacts.md` already records "engraved oil lamp / seashell maps of the five Holy
Cities" among the campaign's map references; this is one of them.

> **If they show it to Elmingwed:** he identifies the lantern instantly and correctly (make,
> origin, fuel, roughly what it's worth). On the etching he is **confidently wrong** — southern
> decorative work, he says, the sort of thing B'rath puts on export goods. Old-school rule: the
> expert answers the question that was asked.

**And it is the party's second Sea of Souls item.** They already hold the white-metal spider
bracelet (session 3, unidentified) which wards against the Sea's unseen water. Now they have the
lamp that lets you *see* down there. Neither of them knows it. See
`campaign/threads.yaml` → `thread:sea-of-souls-water` — the party is quietly assembling an
expedition kit for a place they have not heard of.

**Clean loot, no complications.** Cult property, nothing to do with Doorne, nothing Jiyloo
objects to, and genuinely useful at the table right now: a light source that doesn't smoke in a
tomb full of things that hunt by sense.

## Doorne's Ember — why the raid worked (new lore)

The source doesn't explain why an active holy site sat right next to the tomb and did
nothing while the oasis burned. Here's the answer, invented for this run:

- **Temple al Sia (area 6) keeps a hearth-altar called Doorne's Ember** — a coal that's
  never gone out, said to be a sliver of the sun the god holds in his statue-hand. While lit,
  it wards the whole oasis, not just the temple: Doorne's guardians (the same lineage as the
  Caryatid Columns in 9a, and whatever else still listens in the tomb) sense anyone who means
  the place harm for greed of the tomb's contents, and are moved to act **outside the tomb's
  walls** — not just within them, which is the only place they can normally respond.
- **Ashiq — "the mystic" — disabled it on purpose.** She's the Black Water Cult priest who
  planned this raid and now leads the retrieval patrol (below); reflavor the source's
  unnamed "mystic" as this one person throughout. A day ahead of the main attack, she slipped
  into the temple alone and killed its priest — the source already gives this exact beat
  ("he knew the Inseni would not kill a priest of Doorne"), so keep it, just note her real
  target wasn't the priest, it was the coal. She smothered it in ash and left the temple
  looking untouched (the silver altar and coin lockbox are both still sitting there,
  undisturbed, per the source). Without the Ember lit, the tomb's own guardians stayed
  exactly what they've always been — ancient, faction-blind, dangerous to everyone equally,
  but blind to anything happening outside their own halls. That blindness is what let the
  raid succeed at all.
- **Restoring it.** Sia was entombed with a live fragment of the original hearth-flame — the
  source's "altar with a golden pyramid upon it" in **9p (Tomb of Sia)** is that relic here:
  reflavor it as **Sia's Coal**, a fist-sized golden reliquary that still holds a lit ember
  after centuries (Doorne doesn't let his own fire die). **The Keeper has tended it for four
  hundred years and it is his to give**; the Ember above going out is part of why his hold on
  the hunger is slipping (*Running the Keeper*). Carrying it back to the temple and
  placing in on the Ember-altar with words spoken over by anyone who genuinely means a prayer
  to Doorne (a cleric, a paladin, Jiyloo, or just someone willing to mean it) will relight it.
- **If it's relit before the patrol returns:** when Ashiq and the retrieval patrol arrive,
  Doorne answers — see the round-by-round guide below. This turns the climax from a second
  dungeon's worth of Fanatics into a real payoff for a side quest, without taking the fight
  away if the party wants one.
- **If it's never restored:** run the patrol straight, full fight, no cavalry — also below.
- **World-state note:** if relit, update `loc:oasis-of-sia`'s status to something like
  "warded — Doorne's Ember restored." That's a real, lasting sandbox change (design
  principle 2) and probably the cleanest payoff available for the reopened trade route —
  Doorne himself is now watching the road.

## Adaptation notes (what's kept, what's changed)

- **The raiders become the Black Water Cult.** The source splits antagonists into Chazim
  slavers (who sacked the oasis) and a separate opportunistic "Cult of Water"/Derivesh
  faction moving in after for the relic. Ajiibwan already has an active, "at full strength"
  antagonist here per the reset (`faction:black-water-cult`) — **consolidate both into one
  Black Water Cult operation**: a raiding party sacked the oasis and took slaves days ago,
  and a stronger retrieval team (with a priest) is due back to collect the relic. It fits
  their name almost too well: reflavor their goal here as one local expression of "the
  Great Design" — reversing desertification by force, in service to Elsrok. Use the
  source's **Derivesh Fanatic/Priest/Subleader stat blocks as-is**, just relabeled.
- **Doorne needs no reskin.** `world/entities.yaml` already has `deity:doorne` (aliases
  include "Doorne") tied directly to `loc:oasis-of-sia` **and to Jiyloo** — Steve had
  already folded this exact module's deity into the compendium. That means **Jiyloo has a
  personal stake in this site** the source doesn't give her: she may be Dorne-faithful, or
  simply raised on "don't rob Doorne's tomb" the way any local would be. Let her push back,
  in-character, if the party starts stripping the temple/tomb for coin.
- **The wight in 9p is not Sia.** The source says Sia's high priest poisoned him and raised him
  as a wight. Discarded. **Sia lies dead and at rest in his sarcophagus. The wight is the high
  priest, Kamenhet, who made himself undead when the empire fell so the tomb would go on being
  kept and the oasis kept safe for the king's temples and travellers** — and who sealed himself
  in because of the evil that becoming a wight put in him. Rational, reasonable, dangerous and
  unstable. See *Running the Keeper*.
- **The Scepter of Ice** — keep the mechanics (Staff of Frost + telepathy + burns the
  unfaithful) but consider its irony: an ice relic guarded in the middle of a desert, wanted
  by a *Black Water* cult. That tension is free flavor — lean into it.
- Everything about the tomb's internal guardians (statues, ants, skeletons, the minotaur
  An-Zefful, ghouls, the gargoyle, the Keeper) is **ancient and faction-blind** — it
  threatens cultists exactly as much as the party. Keep that: it's what makes the "beat the
  cult patrol back" clock genuinely tense rather than a simple race to loot first.

## The shape of the adventure

*The arc the rooms hang on. Room-by-room detail is in the **Room cards**, Part 1.*

**1 — The ruined oasis (areas 1–8).** ⏳ *Partly played.* Burnt tents, bodies, a fouled pond.
Done: the tents, the tavern, the dog. **Still open and worth more than the dungeon: Afara and
Dasara in their hole (area 5), Temple al Sia and the smothered Ember (6), the Talifan camp and
the Water-Claimed (7), the pond and the mud elemental (8), and the slave caravan the dog can
track.** Area 5 is the best scene in the module and it is still sitting there unplayed: no fight,
no loot, just a decision about two frightened survivors with nowhere to go.

**2 — The sphinx and the stair (area 9).** ✅ *Played.* Muddy tracks lowered the check to find the
door between the paws — free foreshadowing that something had already been through.

**2a — The sphinx guard.** ✅ *Played.* Three cultists, one killed in the fight, one captured, one
executed after surrender. Both hooks landed: the cult's real timeline, and the gossip that "the
mystic" did something to the temple before the attack.

**3 — The tomb (9a–9p).** ⏳ *Session 5 crawled all of it but 9o and 9p, fighting almost nothing.*
Run it from the **Room cards**. The spine underneath it is Doorne's three rules: the tomb can be
**read** (the door-marks), **passed** (the rite), and **negotiated with** (the Keeper). This party
worked out the first two and cleared the tomb without a fight. The third is session 6.

**4 — The clock lands.** Probably in session 6. Within 1–2 days the cult's main body returns with whatever they bought to
open the door. Either the party is gone, or they're at the sphinx's mouth when it arrives, or
they're caught below and the tomb fights **both** sides. All three are good; the world doesn't wait
on the party's pace. **→ The clock**, above, and *the patrol returns* in the Combat reference.

## The road north — the Keeper's errands, and *Forsaken Pyramid*

*The adventure that grows out of a covenant with the Keeper. It sends the party into the desert for
reasons that are theirs and a dead priest's, not Elmingwed's — and it walks them into the cult's
workshop.*

### The ask: "Keep my oath where I can't"

He swore to keep the oasis safe for the king's temples and the travellers who drink there. He cannot
leave his room. **The party can.** He asks for three things, and they come in order of distance:

1. **Relight the Ember** (local, this session). His Coal, carried up the stair to Temple al Sia's
   hearth-altar, with a prayer from someone who means it — Jiyloo, or any of the sworn. It steadies
   him for good (hunger baseline −1), and it lets the tomb's guardians act **outside** the tomb when
   the column comes (*the patrol returns*). Also: someone has to bury the temple's priest.
2. **Bring back the oasis's people** (a day or more out). The slaves the raiders coffled and took into
   the desert, the El-Alouph family among them. **Orkideh's dog has their scent.**
3. **End the drowning of the dead, at its source** (a day north). The cult's mystics at Ayatorrah
   bottle water spirits that ride corpses like puppets. To a priest whose whole vocation is keeping the
   dead where they were put, that is the worst blasphemy there is — and it is how the cult means to
   open his door.

### The reward: ancient language

What the Keeper offers in return is **the language engine** behind his chair. When the priests are
back at Sia, they will set it up in the great hall with a ceremony and let the party use it. It is
lethal at low level (d100 flat damage; double your HP or more is permanent death; a spider bracelet
halves it for one wearer), so the reward pulls the party back to Sia later, stronger. Full terms: *Running the Keeper* → *The reward*.

In game 1, the party found and used a language engine in the Forsaken Pyramid. Decide whether that
one exists in this run. If it does, the Temple of Mennuj offers the same prize without the Keeper's
terms, which is a temptation worth keeping. If it doesn't, the Keeper's engine is the only one on offer.

### The place is already written: *Forsaken Pyramid* (ONA-16)

The source module's own sequel, and its trail starts at this oasis.
`assets/_raw/Foresaken Pyramid (21) (Level 3-5) (ONA 16) 1112/Forsaken_Pyramid_(Levels_3_5).pdf`

- **The road.** An old stone road under the sand runs **22 miles** north from the Oasis of Sia to the
  ruined city of **Ayatorrah**, and the **Temple of Mennuj** — the one building still standing. About
  eleven hours on foot, half that by camel. Along it, paired obelisks carved with Doornian instructions:
  the same kind of reading this party has spent two sessions getting good at.
- **Why the Keeper cares about it.** The Temple of Mennuj was **the seat of his order**; he trained
  there. He knows the entry rite, and he knows the temple has a **Conformatory** (module area 22) where
  priests who broke their oaths were punished — and its records.
- **Who is there now: the cult.** The module's occupants are the **Insemi** (white-robed desert fanatics
  who, per the module, *took part in the attack on Sia*), two "mystics" secretly serving a priesthood of
  **Doorne the Destroyer**, and a clan of dog-headed **Anubi.** The mystics are summoning water spirits
  and bottling them. **This is where Ashiq's column went.** In this run, fold the Insemi and their
  mystics into the Black Water Cult the same way the Sia raiders were folded in.
- **The slaves may be on this road too.** Optional: **the dog picks up the El-Alouph trail on the
  stone road**, and at the first pair of obelisks (*Turnings of Fate*, six miles out) the trail and the
  road fork. Or the slaves are labour at the temple. Either way, errands 2 and 3 can become one trip.
- **A successor, if they want one.** The module's area 24 holds **four traditional Doornian priests**
  among the Insemi — "not Water Priests," who per the module talk if the party has no elves and fight if
  it does. **This party has two** (Orkideh and Tath'Shar), so recruiting one is a real social problem. One of them, turned, could be **the living priest Temple al Sia needs** (the Keeper's item 4) — the
  thing that would let the Keeper finally die.
- **Level.** The module is written for levels 3–5. A level-2 or -3 party survives it by doing what this
  party does well: reading the place. **The processional statues and gate guardians are lethal to
  unbelievers** (lightning, *confusion*, a gorgon) **and harmless to the faithful who perform the rite**
  — and the Keeper can teach them the rite. *Tath'Shar is unsworn; the module's statues check "faithful
  of Doorne with the party," so the party as a group passes, but Tath'Shar will feel the statues'
  heads turn to follow them.*
- **Seed only:** Steve's folder for the module holds a map titled *Secret entrance from Sea of Souls.*
  The party is already carrying two pieces of Sea-of-Souls kit without knowing it
  (`thread:sea-of-souls-water`). Leave it there.

### Why this is not Elmingwed's errand

- **Elmingwed wants the trade route open** and the merchants of Tel A'bib in the party's debt. The
  oasis matters to him as a caravan stop.
- **The Keeper wants the oasis's people home, its temple tended, and the dead left alone.** The two
  overlap at the oasis and diverge everywhere else — especially at Ayatorrah, which no merchant has any
  reason to visit and where the party would be walking into the cult's base.
- **The party's motive is their own:** a dead priest's vow they chose to carry, a family the dog wants
  back, and the people who burned this place. Elmingwed gets his road reopened as a side effect, and may
  not love *how* — the Keeper's terms can include a priest posted at the temple and pilgrims' rights at
  the sphinx before any caravan stops there.

### The catch

- **A bargain struck while his hunger track was 3 or above was partly the hunger's.** One term is
  poisoned. Recommended: he asks for **"one of them, alive"** — a cultist brought back from Ayatorrah
  for judgment. The priest means a trial. The hunger means a meal. The party may not know which they
  agreed to until they are standing in 9p with a prisoner — the plinth again, a third time.
- **Release has a price.** If the party learns (from the Conformatory, or from the Keeper at 0–2) how he
  can be allowed to die, they also learn that his death **ends the keeping** unless a living priest takes
  up the Scepter at Temple al Sia. Mercy and the oasis's safety pull against each other until someone
  solves that.

## After the tomb — which way?

*When the party comes up the stair, they choose a direction. There are two real roads: **north
into the desert on the Keeper's errands**, or **back to Tel A'bib and on to Durgan's Rest** with
Elmingwed's letter. The world keeps moving on whichever one they don't take.*

**Expect Durgan's Rest.** The party promised Tath'Shar that Tsendur came next. They voted
Tath'Shar down once in session 4 to come here, and Tath'Shar accepted it without a fight. Breaking
the promise a second time would be a choice the table makes on purpose, not by drift.

### Before they leave Sia — cheap acts that change what happens behind them

Each of these takes an hour or less. Each one decides how the oasis fares while the party is gone.
Don't list them for the players. Let the dog, Jiyloo and the Keeper point at them.

- **Relight the Ember** with the Keeper's Coal. Then the tomb's guardians watch the oasis, and a
  column that arrives after the party has left meets them above ground.
- **Bury or burn the murdered priest** of Temple al Sia. That leaves the drowned-priest plan without
  its body. Jiyloo will want it done regardless. *Seed:* Ashiq then needs another Doornian mouth, and
  the nearest one she knows of is travelling with the party.
- **Find Afara and Dasara** in their covered pit. Ask the dog and it finds them in minutes. They are
  witnesses to the raid, and they are someone to leave in charge of the well, or to take to Tel A'bib.
- **Search the 9c camp papers.** They give Tabbat's name, the dispatch and the mapcase: the cult's
  own description of Ayatorrah. That is useful on either road.
- **The captive monk** from session 4 goes to Elmingwed. A living cultist from the Sia raid is
  treasure in itself to him.
- **Jiyloo may ask to stay.** If the Ember is lit and the temple has no priest, she offers to tend
  it until a real one comes. That costs the party their guide and their front line. It also leaves
  a Doorne-faithful woman alone at Sia when the column comes back. Let the party argue her out of
  it, or not.

### Road A — north into the desert, on the Keeper's errands

The full write-up is *The road north*, above. It is the old stone road, 22 miles to Ayatorrah and the
Temple of Mennuj, which is *Forsaken Pyramid* (ONA-16, levels 3–5). Ways to play it:

- **The slave trail.** The dog finds the El-Alouph family's trail. It is a chase across open sand
  that ends at a coffle camped at a waterhole or the first obelisk pair. That is a rescue with a
  clock on it, and it is the cleanest test of whether the party meant reputation over gold.
  - **Optional twist:** the trail bends **east, toward Tel A'bib.** The slaves are being sold, not
    worked. In game 1, Tel A'bib's Imperial Governor turned out to be complicit in the slave trade.
    That makes the two roads converge, and it puts a dangerous question in Elmingwed's city.
- **The obelisk road.** Paired obelisks carry Doornian instructions. It is the door grammar
  again, outdoors, with lethal statues for anyone who reads it wrong. This party is good at that,
  and it plays to their strength.
- **Ayatorrah from the inside.** One PC wears the obsidian ring and walks in as cult staff. With
  the Ashiq lore (*Would Ashiq say the words?*, Rule one), the prize is hearing what the leaders say
  when they think only believers are listening, or getting one of them alone.
- **The Temple of Mennuj itself.** The Keeper teaches them the rite. The Conformatory records hold
  how he can be released. The traditional priests in area 24 might supply a successor, but they
  fight parties that include elves, and this party has two.
- **What it costs:** Tath'Shar's promise is broken again, the letter stays in a pocket, and
  Elmingwed hears nothing.

### What happens at Sia while they are gone

The cult column comes back whether the party is there or not. What it finds depends on what the
party left behind:

- **Ember relit:** the guardians meet the column above ground. The cult breaks on the oasis and
  pulls back to Ayatorrah. The caravans can start running again.
- **Priest buried, Ember still dark:** the column reaches the tomb with no mouth to say the
  names. It camps in 9c, digs, and loses more men to the wall. Ashiq starts looking for another
  Doornian body.
- **Neither:** the drowned priest walks down the stair and says the names. The Keeper meets him at
  whatever point on the hunger track the party left him.
  - If the Keeper is **steadied or under a covenant**, he holds the door: *"That is not a priest.
    That is a well with a man in it."*
  - If he was left **at 3 or higher**, the hunger opens it. Then the cult has the Scepter, and Sia's
    guardians answer to Ashiq.
- **How the party hears about it:** as caravan news in Tel A'bib or on the mountain road, weeks
  later. Whichever outcome it is, it is the price or the payoff of how they left the oasis. Report
  it plainly and let it land.

## Rewards & consequences
- The Scepter of Ice is worth **30,000 gp** but is far
  too recognizable to sell to any local merchant, and Jiyloo would be absolutely dismayed and adversarial to any attempt to remove it from the temple.
- **Elmingwed reads restraint as judgment.** Restoring the transit lane is what he wanted. Delivering
  the captive monk is treasure enough. Leaving the tomb's wealth where it lies tells him the party
  sees the bigger picture: robbed tombs make cursed roads and hostile priests. That is worth more to
  him than any relic they could carry out, and he says so.
- Resolving this **pays off `thread:missing-caravans`** (Feltan's standing reward for the
  vanished western-route caravans) and satisfies Elmingwed's actual want — the trade route
  reopened.
- **Black Water Cult activity confirmed this close to Tel A'bib** is a genuine escalation
  worth surfacing to Elmingwed and, eventually, the wider setting — this is the same faction
  behind the Kantaroz demon-summoning's larger stakes (per `faction:black-water-cult`'s
  world-level "Great Design"), even though the two threads haven't touched yet.
- **Afara and Dasara Al-Doorne's fate** (found or never found; taken in, sent to Tel A'bib, or
  left to fend for themselves) is a small but real character beat worth tracking if it lands.
- **The El-Alouph family are alive, enslaved, and trackable** — their dog is in the party. Whether
  anyone goes after the slave caravan is the cleanest test of whether this party meant the
  reputation-over-gold choice they made at Elmingwed's gate. **Nobody has mentioned them yet.**
- **The reputation ledger** — this job was taken *unpaid*, so the payoff is standing, not coin:
  Elmingwed and the Tel A'bib merchants in the party's debt, Feltan's caravan reward, a relit
  Ember watching the road, the Griffon Blades' honours returned to a living order, two survivors
  pulled out of a hole, and a caravan of slaves brought home. **Price all of it in reputation and
  the dungeon finally pays in the currency they chose.**
- **If Doorne's Ember is restored:** the oasis gets a real, lasting upgrade — Doorne's own
  guardians now watch the road, not just the tomb. This is arguably a better long-term reward
  than the Scepter for a party that can't easily fence a 30,000 gp relic anyway.
- **Ashiq**, captured or escaped, is a loose thread into the wider Black Water Cult
  operation — she personally orchestrated this raid and knows more about "the Great Design"
  than any rank-and-file cultist would.
