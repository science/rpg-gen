## Where things actually stand (continuity note)

Per `campaign/sessions/03.md`: the party never reached the Kantaroz tower. They intercepted
Jeff at a mercenary camp *near* it, fought and won, and hauled him straight to Tel A'bib.
So: **the tower, Durel, and the summoning are all still ahead of you** — nothing here
resolves them. What's new since session 3, for this session:

- **Jeff's pockets are empty.** He sank the stolen operating money into a speculative
  camel-trade venture — plausibly routed through the **Cascabel "Golden Camel" trading
  house** (an established, ruthless merchant family in the world compendium — a nice, free
  piece of connective tissue if you want a name behind the deal) — now en route to the
  Granite Empire. It won't resolve for **8–9 weeks**. He has nothing to hand over but
  information and labor.
- **The spider bracelet is already in the party's strongbox** (`campaign/inventory.yaml`),
  unidentified — taken off Jeff when he and his mercenaries emerged from the tower. The
  party is walking into Elmingwed's compound, *the* Bokkaran identifier in Tel A'bib, with
  it in hand. `the-tinkerers-strings.md` → *Cashing in the loot* already covers this
  dilemma: showing it to Elmingwed gets it identified as ancient + magical, but he won't
  (old-school rule) reveal what it actually does — and selling or revealing it spends a
  Chekhov's gun the campaign hasn't paid off yet. **Worth a beat in Scene 2 or 3** if the
  party thinks to show him — a live temptation, not mandatory.
- That single fact reframes the whole session: **Elmingwed can't be paid in coin today.**
  He has to decide what he wants *instead* — and that's the hook into the next mission.

## Overall Goals

1. **Introduce Tath'Shar** (they/them) without stalling the Jeff scene — fold their arrival

2. **Resolve Jeff's disposition** (or at least the immediate question of what happens at

## Scene 1 — At the Gate

Cold open picking up exactly where session 3 ended. One small live choice sets the tone:
does the party present themselves and Jeff formally to the gate steward, or does Jiyloo
(who lives here, works for Elmingwed's circle) go in first to smooth the way? Either is
fine — it's a two-minute beat, not a puzzle.

> *Read-aloud:* "The gate of Elmingwed's compound is bronze-banded acacia, cool even in the
> afternoon heat. A steward with ink-stained fingers looks up from a ledger, looks at the
> bound man at your side, and says only: 'He's expected. You're... a surprise. Wait here.'"

## Scene 2 — The Audience Chamber (Tath'Shar arrives)

Elmingwed receives them — but he's not alone. **Tath'Shar (they/them) is already
mid-conversation with him**, presenting a letter of introduction from their family when the
party is shown in. Let the two agendas collide in real time; that collision *is* the
introduction.

**Why Elmingwed brushes them onto the party (his real motive, not just plot convenience):**
hosting a Drow noble directly is a political liability in a Vanic-Athyes town where locals
already eye drow with suspicion — but the letter carries enough weight (their family, their
sponsor) that he can't simply refuse it either. Handing them to the party solves both
problems at once: he honors the letter without the entanglement, and hands the party
capacity he clearly thinks they need (a man in irons and no muscle to spare).

> *Read-aloud (Elmingwed, barely missing a beat):* "Ah — good, you're all here. Tath'Shar,
> these are the people I mentioned. They have a spare seat at their fire and, as of this
> afternoon, a spare debt to work off. I think you'll find each other useful."

**Entrance beats for Tath'Shar (Phoenix's flourish, theirs to play):** the glamoured
studded leather reads as fine, unremarkable travel clothes — nobody clocks it as armor
without a DC check after watching them rest. A flick of **Thaumaturgy** (not the usual drow
Dancing Lights) is a good character tell — table talk, tricks, drawing attention on their
terms.

*Table note on Tath'Shar being trans/gender-neutral — they/them ONLY, no exceptions:*
default to playing it as simply unremarkable in-world — Drow social structure doesn't
police gender the way Tel A'bib's Athyes-flavored culture might, so nobody in the scene has
a reaction to manage. That keeps the spotlight on their actual hook (family, mission,
Maghiel) rather than making their identity the plot. If Phoenix wants to explore reactions
to it later, that's theirs to bring up.

**Their real reason for being here (player-facing, playable immediately):** they believe
their sister — a name they haven't spoken yet — is active somewhere in the region and have
gone looking for allies to help stop whatever's coming. GM-only truth: that sister is
**[[npc:maghiel]]** (she/her) — and in this reset timeline **she is not yet the villain the
prior campaign remembers.** `world/entities.yaml`'s "Black-Thorn-cursed" status for her is
prior-campaign reference only (dnd/CLAUDE.md principle 1); right now she's simply a capable,
dangerous drow wizard, still findable, still (arguably) stoppable before she becomes
anything worse. **Do not reveal the name or the full shape of it yet.** See
`scenarios/mishap-at-durgans-rest.md` for the GM-only truth this is building toward — that
window is closer to closing than anyone at the table knows.

**Before the party leaves this scene, Elmingwed gives Tath'Shar a letter — for Tsendur, not
for the party.** This is the real seed of Option B below, planted here rather than sprung
cold in Scene 4. Its content matters (read it aloud when Tath'Shar shows it to someone, or
save the full read for when they present it at Durgan's Rest):

> *Tsendur — the bearer is Tath'Shar, searching for a sister they have reason to think is
> active in this region: a drow wizard, dangerous, driven, and — I am reliably told —
> hunting artifacts that ought to stay buried. If you hold anything of that description, or
> know who does, guard it and guard yourself. I would take it as a personal favor if you
> gave them what help you can. — Elmingwed*

Elmingwed can plausibly know this much — he's already Tel A'bib's expert in ancient
artifacts and a key information broker (`npc:elmingwed`); a rumor about a drow wizard
shopping for "prohibited artifacts" is exactly the kind of thing that crosses his desk. Don't
over-explain it at the table; let it sit as a small mystery of its own if anyone asks how he
knows.

## Scene 3 — Jeff's Reckoning

The meaty center of the session. Structure it in three beats so it doesn't collapse into
one long haggling scene:

**1. The confession.** Elmingwed asks, plainly, where his share is. Jeff has to admit the
money's gone — invested, not stolen twice, which is a distinction he'll insist on. Play
this for a flash of real desperation: this is the first time his glib survivor act cracks.

**2. The papers.** This is where you decide, live, which version fits your table's night:

- **Version A — Elmingwed already has them.** "No lower merchant in this city crosses me,
  Jeff. You know that." He produces the packet on the spot — instant, total leverage over
  both Jeff *and* Orkideh, no chase required. Best if you want to move fast into Scene 4.
- **Version B — Elmingwed is one step from getting them.** He knows *who* holds the packet
  (he always did — Jeff's "secret" merchant answers to him too) but hasn't collected it yet.
  This opens a **short window** for the party to act first — intercept it, buy it, warn
  Orkideh — before it's out of their hands for good. Best if the table's been leaning into
  Orkideh's secret and you want one more scene of agency before the leverage locks in.

Either way, land the same twist already seeded in `the-tinkerers-strings.md`: **Elmingwed
himself is untouchable** (his weekly lunch with [[npc:boetharch]]), and the papers'
*real* exposure is Orkideh's. Let that sting.

**3. The counteroffer.** With no coin to collect, Elmingwed converts the debt into service
— "reasonable man" framing, carrot before stick. Present it as a genuine three-way fork, not
a script:

| Choice | What it looks like | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Cooperate** — work together to reopen the choked route at [[loc:oasis-of-sia]] | Whole party stays together; Elmingwed's biggest ask, biggest trust payoff | Highest danger — Oasis of Sia is reset-canon *unlooted and cult-held* (see Scene 4) |
| **B. Split up** — Jeff goes out on a caravan (plausibly the very one his money's riding — a reason for Elmingwed to want him *on* it, watching his investment), the rest carry a message to [[npc:tsendur]] at [[loc:durgans-rest]] | Loses Jeff as a hostage/wildcard (thread:jeff-fate trends toward resolved-by-departure); party gets a self-contained mission | Jeff is out of the party's hands going forward — a real, not free, resolution |
| **C. Refuse both** | Party keeps Jeff, walks away from Elmingwed's ask | Patronage cools (thread:elmingwed-deal) — but the world doesn't collapse: **Feltan** and **Al-Esellin**, already seeded in the Back-to-Tel A'bib branch as alternate commissioners, are right there to pick up the slack. Don't punish a "no." |

Whichever the table picks, this is also where you can let the disposition table for Jeff
(delivered / escorted-out / kept / killed — full detail in `the-tinkerers-strings.md`)
actually resolve, since B effectively *is* "delivered, but put to work instead of handed
over for good."

## Scene 4 — The Choice: Sia or Tsendur

Present both live, without steering — but know your table's actual footing before you do:

**Option A — Oasis of Sia.** Fully designed: `scenarios/ashes-at-the-sphinx.md`, adapted
from the "Death at the Oasis" module (One Night Adventures #ONA-15) already in the corpus,
reskinned with the Black Water Cult (reset canon has them at full strength here, unlooted)
and your existing `deity:doorne` compendium entry. A ruined oasis, a real "what do we do
with these survivors" scene, a room-by-room tomb crawl under the Sphinx, and a 24-hour clock
before a cult retrieval patrol arrives for the relic inside. **L2-4**, and Jiyloo — already
tied to Doorne in the compendium — has a genuine personal stake in how the party treats the
temple. At level 2 with possibly 7 bodies in the field (5 PCs + Jiyloo + Jeffrol, if he
stays), this is a solid, honest fit, not an overmatch.

**Option B — the message to Tsendur.** Already primed in Scene 2 — Tath'Shar is already
holding Elmingwed's letter for Tsendur, so this option doesn't need to be pitched cold here;
it's the thing they've presumably been wanting to do since they got it. Fully designed:
`scenarios/mishap-at-durgans-rest.md`, adapted from "A Mishap of Ill Portent" (Vengeance of
the Shunned Part 1) — and it's far more than delivering a message: **this is the actual
origin of Maghiel's villain arc**, not just her "first move," and the Clasp of Vergren
stolen in it is the exact item she later hunts in your already-catalogued "Bridged City —
Scholarly Schism" adventure (`world/adventures.md`), which was never run. Recommend this as
the **featured path** for session 5 if the table doesn't strongly prefer otherwise —
**L1-3**, a better level fit than Sia, and it's scripted to end with Maghiel's escape and
transformation regardless of how the fight goes (read the GM-ONLY TRUTH section in that file
before running it — this is the one deliberate rail in the whole arc). Built-in throughline
after: Durgan's Rest → Bridged City → the Sea of Souls, where the party's already-recovered
spider bracelet pays off.

Keep whichever option isn't chosen alive as background rumor (merchants grumbling about the
western caravans for Sia, per thread:missing-caravans; scholarly gossip about magical
surges for Durgan's Rest) so it's there when the party's ready for it.

**Option C — something else entirely.** Per the ouija-board principle, if the party invents
a third path, let them — the world doesn't need this exact fork honored. Elmingwed's actual
want (leverage + useful hands) is satisfiable lots of ways.

## Pacing

Social/political session — budget accordingly: Scenes 1–2 quick (~15 min, mostly Tath'Shar's
entrance carrying the energy), Scene 3 is the real centerpiece (45–60 min — protect this
one), Scene 4 can run short (~15–20 min) since it's a launch, not a payoff. If it overruns,
it's fine to end on the choice made and open session 5 on the road.
